## Mini Review


1. **Er ist immer noch krank.** What's implied?\
   a. He gets ill often.\
   b. He's been ill for longer than expected.\
   c. He recovered.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** it's gone on longer than expected.

</details>

2. Which is more neutral?\
   a. Der Bus ist noch nicht da.\
   b. Der Bus ist immer noch nicht da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** plain *noch nicht* is the neutral report; *immer noch nicht* adds impatience.

</details>

3. What's the difference between *immer noch* and *immer wieder*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *immer noch* = one situation continuing without a break. *immer wieder* = something happening again and again.

**Explanation:** *immer noch* emphasizes that a situation is continuing, often longer than expected.

</details>

4. **Produce it:** How would you say "I still don't understand", using *immer noch*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich verstehe das immer noch nicht.**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* emphasizes that a situation is continuing, often longer than expected.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** Ask a friend whether they still live in Munich.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Wohnst du immer noch in München?**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* emphasizes that a situation is continuing, often longer than expected.

</details>
