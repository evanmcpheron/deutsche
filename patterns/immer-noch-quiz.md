# Quiz: immer noch

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. Tom has been waiting for the bus much longer than expected. He is still standing at the stop. Which sentence fits best?\
   a. Tom wartet immer wieder.\
   b. Tom wartet immer noch.\
   c. Tom wartet schon.\
   d. Tom wartet nicht mehr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Immer noch* describes one situation that continues and adds the feeling that it has continued longer than expected. *Immer wieder* would describe repeated separate events.

</details>

2. You are simply telling someone that Max has not left the office yet. You do not want to add surprise or impatience. Which sentence is the more neutral choice?\
   a. Max ist nicht mehr im Büro.\
   b. Max ist immer wieder im Büro.\
   c. Max ist noch im Büro.\
   d. Max ist immer noch im Büro.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Plain *noch* gives the neutral fact that the situation continues. *Immer noch* would also say he is still there, but it adds a speaker comment such as surprise, concern, or impatience.

</details>

3. Your train should already be here, but the platform is still empty. Which sentence best expresses your mild frustration?\
   a. Der Zug ist immer noch nicht da.\
   b. Der Zug ist nicht mehr da.\
   c. Der Zug ist schon da.\
   d. Der Zug ist immer wieder da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Immer noch nicht* means “still not” and is common when an expected change continues not to happen. *Nicht mehr da* would mean the train was there and then left.

</details>

4. The phone rings, stops, and later rings again. This happens several times. Which sentence fits?\
   a. Das Telefon klingelt nicht mehr.\
   b. Das Telefon klingelt immer noch.\
   c. Das Telefon klingelt schon.\
   d. Das Telefon klingelt immer wieder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Immer wieder* describes repeated separate events: ring, stop, ring again. *Immer noch* would describe one continuing situation.

</details>

5. Compare **Er ist noch hier** and **Er ist immer noch hier**. What does adding *immer* mainly change?\
   a. It changes the sentence from present to past.\
   b. It adds attitude that his being here is continuing longer than expected.\
   c. It changes “here” into “again.”\
   d. It means he is here every day.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The basic fact remains that he is still here. *Immer noch* adds the speaker's perspective that the continuation is notable, surprising, worrying, or annoying.

</details>

6. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Ist Lea fertig?
B: Nein. Es dauert sehr lange. ___
```

   a. Sie ist immer noch nicht fertig.\
   b. Sie ist immer wieder fertig.\
   c. Sie ist nicht mehr fertig.\
   d. Sie ist schon fertig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The expected finished state has still not arrived, and the long wait gives a reason for the added attitude in *immer noch nicht*.

</details>

## Part B — Continuation, Repetition, and Attitude

7. The intended idea is a completely neutral status report: “He is still here.” How should you judge **Er ist immer noch hier**?\
   a. Incorrect German.\
   b. Correct German, but more loaded than the intended neutral statement.\
   c. Correct only in formal German.\
   d. It means he keeps leaving and returning.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The sentence is correct, but *immer noch* adds speaker attitude. For a neutral report, **Er ist noch hier** is safer.

</details>

8. A dog barks, becomes quiet, then barks again. The same pattern repeats during the evening. Which sentence fits best?\
   a. Der Hund bellt noch.\
   b. Der Hund bellt nicht mehr.\
   c. Der Hund bellt immer noch.\
   d. Der Hund bellt immer wieder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The barking happens in repeated episodes, so *immer wieder* fits. *Immer noch* would point to one continuing episode of barking.

</details>

9. **Ich verstehe das immer noch nicht.** What does *immer noch* add here?\
   a. The speaker understands it repeatedly.\
   b. The speaker understood it before.\
   c. The lack of understanding continues, and the speaker may be frustrated about that.\
   d. The speaker never expects to understand it.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Immer noch nicht* says the expected change has still not happened and commonly adds mild frustration. It does not say the change is impossible forever.

</details>

10. Which pair shows the lesson's contrast correctly?\
   a. **noch** = neutral continuation; **immer noch** = continuation with extra attitude.\
   b. **noch** = repeated events; **immer noch** = one future event.\
   c. **noch** = no longer; **immer noch** = already.\
   d. **noch** = formal; **immer noch** = incorrect in conversation.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Both can describe continuation. The important difference is that *immer noch* comments on the continuation as notable or longer than expected.

</details>

11. Which expression means the same as *immer noch* but is slightly more formal or literary?\
   a. noch immer\
   b. immer wieder\
   c. nicht mehr\
   d. schon noch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The lesson identifies **noch immer** as equivalent in meaning to **immer noch**, but somewhat more formal or literary.

</details>

12. Which sentence keeps the pattern together in the natural position taught in the lesson?\
   a. Wir immer warten noch.\
   b. Immer wir noch warten.\
   c. Wir warten immer noch.\
   d. Wir warten noch nicht immer.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Treat *immer noch* as a chunk: **Wir warten immer noch.** The two words stay together where plain *noch* would normally sit.

</details>

13. Look at the situation:

```text
09:00 — Mia wartet.
10:00 — Mia wartet.
11:00 — Mia wartet. She is now impatient.
```

   Which sentence best describes 11:00?\
   a. Mia wartet nicht mehr.\
   b. Mia wartet immer wieder.\
   c. Mia wartet immer noch.\
   d. Mia wartet schon.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** This is one uninterrupted wait, and the added impatience makes *immer noch* especially appropriate. *Immer wieder* would require separate repeated waits.

</details>

## Part C — Meaning, Form, and Diagnosis

14. A learner reads **Sie ist immer noch krank** as “She is always still sick.” What is the better interpretation?\
   a. She becomes sick again and again.\
   b. One illness is continuing longer than expected.\
   c. She is no longer sick.\
   d. She is already healthy.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Immer noch* is one unit meaning “still, even now.” It does not normally mean the literal combination “always still.”

</details>

15. You began waiting at 10:00 and have not stopped. A learner says **Wir warten immer wieder.** How should you judge it?\
   a. Correct German, but it describes repeated separate waits rather than one continuing wait.\
   b. Incorrect German because *immer* cannot be used with verbs.\
   c. Correct and exactly expresses one uninterrupted wait.\
   d. It means the waiting has ended.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** **Wir warten immer wieder** describes waiting on repeated occasions. For one uninterrupted wait that is continuing longer than expected, use **Wir warten immer noch.**

</details>

16. Reconstruct the sentence from **ich / immer noch / müde / bin**. Which order is natural?\
   a. Immer ich bin noch müde.\
   b. Ich immer bin noch müde.\
   c. Ich bin immer noch müde.\
   d. Ich bin müde noch immer noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Ich bin immer noch müde** keeps the chunk together before the adjective, exactly where plain *noch* could appear.

</details>

17. Which sentence does **not** describe one continuous unfinished situation?\
   a. Wir warten immer noch.\
   b. Ich bin immer noch müde.\
   c. Der Zug ist immer noch nicht da.\
   d. Er kommt immer wieder zu spät.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Immer wieder* describes repeated events. The other three all present one situation as continuing.

</details>

18. It is 17:30. The shop normally closes at 18:00, so you simply report that it is still open. Which sentence is the most neutral?\
   a. Der Laden ist immer wieder offen.\
   b. Der Laden ist nicht mehr offen.\
   c. Der Laden ist immer noch offen.\
   d. Der Laden ist noch offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Nothing in the situation makes the continuation surprising or frustrating, so plain **noch offen** is the better neutral choice. *Immer noch offen* would add an extra comment.

</details>

19. **Select all that apply.** Which situations make *immer noch* or *immer noch nicht* a natural loaded choice?\
   a. The train is late and the platform is still empty: **Der Zug ist immer noch nicht da.**\
   b. After several explanations, you still do not understand: **Ich verstehe das immer noch nicht.**\
   c. A phone rings, stops, and rings again: **Das Telefon klingelt immer noch.**\
   d. You have been waiting far longer than expected: **Wir warten immer noch.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** In a, b, and d, one situation continues longer than expected and the context supports speaker attitude. Option c describes repeated ringing with stops in between, so the relevant contrast would be *immer wieder* rather than *immer noch*.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

20. The train should already be here, but it is still absent. Fill the blank with the pattern from this lesson:

**Der Zug ist ___ da.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **immer noch nicht**

**Explanation:** **Der Zug ist immer noch nicht da** expresses that the expected arrival continues not to happen and adds the speaker's reaction to the delay.

</details>

21. Start with **Er wartet noch.** Change the situation: the wait has become much longer than expected. Change only what is necessary.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Er wartet immer noch.**

**Explanation:** Adding *immer* keeps the continuation meaning but adds the attitude that the waiting has gone on notably long.

</details>

22. Correct the learner sentence **Wir warten immer wieder.** The intended meaning is that one wait has continued without stopping and is taking much longer than expected.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Wir warten immer noch.**

**Explanation:** One continuing wait uses *immer noch*, especially when the delay is notable. *Immer wieder* would describe separate repeated occasions of waiting.

</details>
