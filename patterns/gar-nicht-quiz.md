## Part A — Recognize Strong Negation

1. A classmate is worried that the exercise is very difficult. You have tried it and want to reassure them that it is not difficult at all. Which sentence fits best?\
   a. Das ist gar nicht schwer.\
   b. Das ist gerade schwer.\
   c. Das ist gar kein schwer.\
   d. Das ist noch schwer.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** **Gar nicht schwer** means “not difficult at all.” In this context the strong negation is reassuring rather than negative in tone.

</details>

2. You slept well and feel completely awake. Which sentence strongly says that you are not tired at all?\
   a. Ich bin nicht gar müde.\
   b. Ich bin gar nicht müde.\
   c. Ich bin gar keine müde.\
   d. Ich bin gerade müde.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** With an adjective such as *müde*, use the chunk **gar nicht**: **Ich bin gar nicht müde.** It is stronger than plain *nicht*.

</details>

3. You have no free time whatsoever today. Which sentence uses the lesson's noun-negation pattern?\
   a. Ich habe gar nicht Zeit.\
   b. Ich habe gar keine Zeit.\
   c. Ich habe nicht gar Zeit.\
   d. Ich habe gar keinen Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A noun that would take *kein* still takes *kein* when intensified: **gar keine Zeit**. The lesson explicitly contrasts this with the incorrect *gar nicht Zeit*.

</details>

4. A friend apologizes for a very small mistake. You want to reassure them: “That's no problem at all.” Which sentence fits?\
   a. Das ist gar nicht Problem.\
   b. Das ist gerade kein Problem.\
   c. Das ist gar keine Problem.\
   d. Das ist gar kein Problem.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Problem* is a noun, so the intensified negative is **gar kein Problem**. This is another reassuring use of strong negation.

</details>

5. **Das gefällt mir gar nicht.** What attitude does the sentence express?\
   a. Mild uncertainty.\
   b. Strong approval.\
   c. Strong dislike.\
   d. No opinion.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Gar nicht** intensifies the negative: the speaker does not like it at all. Here the context makes the emphasis strongly disapproving.

</details>

6. Why can **Das ist gar nicht schwer** sound encouraging while **Das gefällt mir gar nicht** sounds strongly negative?\
   a. *Gar nicht* changes meaning completely in each sentence.\
   b. *Gar* means “good” with adjectives and “bad” with verbs.\
   c. The same strong negation is used, but the word or idea being negated determines the emotional direction.\
   d. Only the first sentence is grammatical.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The chunk keeps the same job: strong negation. Negating *schwer* can reassure someone, while strongly negating *gefällt mir* expresses dislike.

</details>

## Part B — Form and Related Patterns

7. What is the best beginner approach to *gar* in **gar nicht**?\
   a. Memorize a separate English translation for *gar*.\
   b. Learn **gar nicht** as a chunk that strengthens negation.\
   c. Treat *gar* as another form of *gerade*.\
   d. Use *gar* only with nouns.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The lesson recommends learning **gar nicht** as one chunk. *Gar* by itself does not give the beginner a useful one-word English translation.

</details>

8. A learner writes **Ich habe gar nicht Zeit.** What is the best correction?\
   a. Ich habe gar keine Zeit.\
   b. Ich habe gar Zeit nicht.\
   c. Ich habe gerade nicht Zeit.\
   d. Ich habe gar nichts Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is a noun, so use the *kein* pattern: **gar keine Zeit**. *Gar nicht + noun* is the common error highlighted in the lesson.

</details>

9. Which statement about **gar** and **gerade** is correct?\
   a. They are two spellings of the same word.\
   b. *Gar* is the short form of *gerade*.\
   c. They can always replace each other.\
   d. They are different words and should not be confused.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The lesson explicitly warns that **gar** and **gerade** are different words. Similar spelling does not make their meanings or functions interchangeable.

</details>

10. Which expression is taught as a very common relative of **gar nicht** with the same basic job “not at all”?\
   a. immer nicht\
   b. schon nicht\
   c. gerade nicht\
   d. überhaupt nicht

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The lesson lists **überhaupt nicht** as another common way to say “not at all,” with a slightly more formal feel.

</details>

11. **Ich habe gar nichts gesagt.** What does **gar nichts** mean here?\
   a. nothing at all\
   b. not tired at all\
   c. nobody at all\
   d. not yet

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** **Gar nichts** is a related intensified negative meaning “nothing at all.” It is one of the recognition forms introduced in the lesson.

</details>

12. You have not begun the task at all yet. Which lesson-taught expression matches that idea?\
   a. schon gar nicht\
   b. noch gar nicht\
   c. gar wieder nicht\
   d. gerade gar

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** **Noch gar nicht** means “not at all yet.” The lesson gives **Ich habe noch gar nicht angefangen** as the model for this pattern.

</details>

13. Which related expression means “nobody at all”?\
   a. gar kein\
   b. gar nichts\
   c. überhaupt nicht\
   d. gar niemand

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** **Gar niemand** intensifies *niemand* and means “nobody at all.” It is taught for recognition rather than as a separate major grammar topic.

</details>

## Part C — Contrast, Naturalness, and Diagnosis

14. Which sentence is the natural everyday way to say “I am not tired at all”?\
   a. Ich gar bin nicht müde.\
   b. Ich bin gar keine müde.\
   c. Ich bin gar nicht müde.\
   d. Ich bin nicht müde gar.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Ich bin gar nicht müde** keeps the intensifying chunk together in a normal position before the adjective.

</details>

15. Someone claims something that you strongly reject as completely untrue. Which sentence gives the stronger denial?\
   a. Das stimmt gar nicht.\
   b. Das stimmt nicht.\
   c. Das stimmt kein.\
   d. Das gar stimmt nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** **Das stimmt nicht** is ordinary negation. **Das stimmt gar nicht** adds the lesson's stronger “not at all” emphasis, which fits a strong rejection.

</details>

16. Which sentence uses **gerade**, not the lesson's intensifier **gar**?\
   a. Das ist gar nicht schlimm.\
   b. Ich habe gar keine Zeit.\
   c. Ich bin gerade müde.\
   d. Das gefällt mir gar nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Gerade** is a different word. The other three choices use *gar* to intensify a negative expression.

</details>

17. Reconstruct the sentence from **das / gar nicht / schlimm / ist**. Which order is natural?\
   a. Gar das ist nicht schlimm.\
   b. Das gar schlimm ist nicht.\
   c. Das ist schlimm gar nicht.\
   d. Das ist gar nicht schlimm.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The natural sentence is **Das ist gar nicht schlimm.** Treat *gar nicht* as a unit that strengthens the negation of *schlimm*.

</details>

18. The intended meaning is “I am not tired at all.” A learner says **Ich bin nicht müde.** How should you judge it?\
   a. Incorrect German.\
   b. Correct German, but weaker than the intended emphatic meaning.\
   c. Correct and exactly as strong as *gar nicht*.\
   d. It means “I am tired right now.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** **Ich bin nicht müde** is normal German, but it gives ordinary negation. Adding **gar** produces the stronger **Ich bin gar nicht müde**: “not tired at all.”

</details>

19. **Select all that apply.** Which sentences use a lesson-taught form of intensified negation correctly?\
   a. Das ist gar nicht schwer.\
   b. Ich habe gar keine Zeit.\
   c. Ich habe gar nichts gesagt.\
   d. Ich bin gerade nicht müde.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** Options a, b, and c correctly use *gar* with *nicht*, *kein*, or *nichts*. Option d is grammatical, but it uses *gerade*, not the lesson's *gar* intensifier, so it does not express the targeted “not at all” pattern.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

20. You want to say that you are **not tired at all**. Fill the blank with the lesson's strong-negation chunk:

**Ich bin ___ müde.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **gar nicht**

**Explanation:** With the adjective *müde*, use **gar nicht** to strengthen ordinary negation to “not at all.”

</details>

21. Correct the sentence:

**Ich habe gar nicht Zeit.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe gar keine Zeit.**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is a noun, so the negation uses **kein**. The intensifier gives **gar keine Zeit**, not *gar nicht Zeit*.

</details>

22. A learner writes **Ich bin gerade nicht müde** but intends the lesson's meaning “I am not tired at all.” Change the sentence to express that intended emphasis.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin gar nicht müde.**

**Explanation:** **Gar nicht** gives the “not at all” meaning. **Gerade** is a different word and does not serve as the lesson's negation intensifier.

</details>
