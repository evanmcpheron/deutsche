# schon mal

**Type:** Pattern (chunk)
**Means:** ever, at some point before now
**Level:** A1–A2 · **Tier 2** · **Recognition First**

## What It Means

*schon mal* (= *schon einmal*) asks or states whether something has **ever happened at least once**.

[schon](../words/schon.md) supplies "already," [mal](../particles/mal.md) supplies "once / at some point." Together they're the German equivalent of English "ever" in questions and "before" in statements.

## In Use

**Warst du schon mal in Deutschland?**
Have you ever been to Germany?

**Ich war schon mal in Berlin.**
I've been to Berlin before.

**Hast du das schon mal probiert?**
Have you ever tried that?

**Ich habe das noch nie gemacht.**
I've never done that. *(the negative uses **noch nie**, not *nicht schon mal*)*

## The Question-and-Answer Set

This is the pattern worth memorizing whole, because it's a standard conversational exchange:

| | German |
|---|---|
| Question | **Warst du schon mal in Wien?** |
| Yes | **Ja, einmal.** / **Ja, schon.** |
| No | **Nein, noch nie.** |

*noch nie* — "never yet" — is the natural negative. Note that it uses [noch](../words/noch.md), not *schon*.

## Also: "in advance"

*schon mal* has a second, slightly different use meaning "already, ahead of the rest":

**Geh schon mal vor.**
Go on ahead.

**Ich fange schon mal an.**
I'll go ahead and start.

Recognize it; it's common in spoken German.

## Common Mistakes

- **Negating it with *nicht*.** *Ich war nicht schon mal dort* is wrong. Use **noch nie**: *Ich war noch nie dort.*
- **Reading *mal* as "times."** In this chunk *mal* is short for *einmal* — "once, at some point."
- **Dropping *schon*.** *Warst du mal in Berlin?* exists but is casual and less clear; the full *schon mal* is safer.
