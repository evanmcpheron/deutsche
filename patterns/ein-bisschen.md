## What It Means

*ein bisschen* = **a little, a bit.** It works with both quantities and adjectives, and it's one of the most useful softeners a beginner can carry around.

## In Use

### With a noun — a small quantity

**Ein bisschen Milch, bitte.**
A little milk, please.

**Ich brauche ein bisschen Zeit.**
I need a bit of time.

**Nur ein bisschen Zucker.**
Just a little sugar.

### With an adjective — slightly

**Das ist ein bisschen teuer.**
That's a bit expensive.

**Ich bin ein bisschen müde.**
I'm a little tired.

**Es ist ein bisschen kalt hier.**
It's a bit cold in here.

### Speaking a language

**Ich spreche ein bisschen Deutsch.**
I speak a little German.

Possibly the single most useful sentence in this repository for an actual trip.

## Relatives

- **ein bisschen mehr** — a little more: *Ein bisschen mehr, bitte.*
- **ein bisschen weniger** — a little less
- **ein wenig** — a little *(slightly more formal, same meaning)*
- **etwas** — some, a little: *etwas Milch* — a bit of milk
- **ein bisschen was** — a little something *(colloquial)*

## Why It Softens

German can sound blunt to English ears, and *ein bisschen* is one of the cheapest ways to take the edge off:

**Das ist teuer.** — That's expensive. *(a verdict)*
**Das ist ein bisschen teuer.** — That's a bit expensive. *(an opening for negotiation)*

**Ich bin müde.** — I'm tired.
**Ich bin ein bisschen müde.** — I'm a little tired. *(no drama)*

## Common Mistakes

- **Changing the form.** *ein bisschen* doesn't decline — it stays exactly like that whatever noun follows. *Ein bisschen Milch*, *ein bisschen Zeit*, *ein bisschen Geld*.
- **Using it with plurals.** For countable plurals you want *ein paar* ("a few"): *ein paar Freunde*, not *ein bisschen Freunde*.
- **Spelling.** One *s* then double *s*: **bisschen**.
