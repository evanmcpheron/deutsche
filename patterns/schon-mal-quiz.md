## Part A — Recognize the Pattern

1. You want to ask a friend whether they have been to Berlin at least once before now. Which question fits best?\
   a. Bist du jetzt in Berlin?\
   b. Warst du noch nie in Berlin?\
   c. Warst du schon mal in Berlin?\
   d. Ich war schon mal in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Warst du schon mal in Berlin?** asks whether the experience has happened at least once before now. *Schon mal* is the lesson's standard “ever / before” chunk.

</details>

2. Someone asks **Warst du schon mal in Wien?** You have never been there. Which answer is natural?\
   a. Nein, noch nie.\
   b. Nein, nicht schon mal.\
   c. Nein, schon nicht.\
   d. Nein, noch mal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The natural negative partner is **noch nie**: **Nein, noch nie.** The lesson specifically warns against trying to negate *schon mal* with *nicht*.

</details>

3. **Ich war schon mal in Köln.** What does the speaker mean?\
   a. The speaker has been in Cologne before at least once.\
   b. The speaker is in Cologne now.\
   c. The speaker has never been in Cologne.\
   d. The speaker goes to Cologne every day.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** In a statement, *schon mal* says that something has happened before at least once. It does not say the speaker is there now or that the event happens regularly.

</details>

4. Your friend is not ready yet, but you can begin the task without them. Which sentence fits the lesson's second use of *schon mal*?\
   a. Ich habe das noch nie gemacht.\
   b. Ich fange schon mal an.\
   c. Warst du schon mal da?\
   d. Ich war schon mal in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** **Ich fange schon mal an** means “I'll go ahead and start.” Here *schon mal* is about starting ahead of the other person, not about a past experience.

</details>

5. **Geh schon mal vor.** What is the speaker telling the other person to do?\
   a. Go back to a place visited before.\
   b. Ask whether someone has gone there before.\
   c. Go on ahead.\
   d. Never go there.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** In **Geh schon mal vor**, the chunk means “go ahead / ahead of the rest.” This is the second spoken use taught in the lesson.

</details>

6. In the experience pattern **Warst du schon mal ...?**, what is *mal* doing?\
   a. It means that the event happens many times.\
   b. It means “now.”\
   c. It is the short form of *einmal*: once / at some point.\
   d. It makes the question negative.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** In this chunk, *mal* is short for **einmal** and points to “once / at some point.” Do not read it as ordinary “times” counting here.

</details>

## Part B — Questions, Answers, and Meaning

7. Complete the short conversation naturally.

```text
A: Hast du das schon mal probiert?
B: Ja, ___.
```

   a. noch nie\
   b. einmal\
   c. nicht schon mal\
   d. noch mal

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** **Ja, einmal** is one of the lesson's standard positive answers. It confirms that the experience happened at least once.

</details>

8. You want to say that you have never done something. Which sentence follows the lesson's pattern?\
   a. Ich habe das schon nicht gemacht.\
   b. Ich habe das nicht schon mal gemacht.\
   c. Ich habe das schon mal nicht gemacht.\
   d. Ich habe das noch nie gemacht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The lesson gives **Ich habe das noch nie gemacht** for “I've never done that.” The negative pattern uses *noch nie*, not *nicht schon mal*.

</details>

9. The intended meaning is “I have never been there.” A learner writes **Ich war nicht schon mal dort.** How should you judge it?\
   a. Correct and natural German.\
   b. Correct only in formal German.\
   c. Not the natural lesson pattern; use **Ich war noch nie dort.**\
   d. It means “I was there once.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The lesson treats negating *schon mal* with *nicht* as an error. For “never,” use **noch nie**: **Ich war noch nie dort.**

</details>

10. Compare **Warst du schon mal in Berlin?** with **Warst du mal in Berlin?** Which statement matches the lesson?\
   a. The version without *schon* is impossible German.\
   b. The version without *schon* exists in casual German, but the full *schon mal* pattern is safer and clearer.\
   c. The version without *schon* means “Are you in Berlin now?”\
   d. The two versions have opposite meanings.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The lesson notes that **Warst du mal ...?** exists conversationally, but recommends the full **schon mal** chunk as the safer pattern for a beginner.

</details>

11. Which question most clearly asks whether an experience has happened at least once before now?\
   a. Bist du jetzt in Deutschland?\
   b. Gehst du schon mal vor?\
   c. Warst du noch nie in Deutschland?\
   d. Warst du schon mal in Deutschland?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** **Warst du schon mal in Deutschland?** is the standard experience question. The other choices ask about the present, going ahead, or whether the person has never been there.

</details>

12. Which expression can replace *schon mal* in the lesson's core meaning without changing the basic idea?\
   a. schon nie\
   b. schon einmal\
   c. noch mal\
   d. noch nie

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The lesson introduces **schon mal** as the shorter conversational form of **schon einmal**. Both can express “ever / at some point before now.”

</details>

13. Read **Warst du schon mal in Wien?** Which simple description matches the question?\
   a. The speaker wants to know whether being in Vienna has happened at least once before now.\
   b. The speaker wants to know whether you are in Vienna at this moment.\
   c. The speaker states that you have never been in Vienna.\
   d. The speaker tells you to go to Vienna ahead of everyone else.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The question is about previous experience: has this happened at least once? It does not ask where you are now.

</details>

## Part C — Contrast and Diagnosis

14. Compare **Ich fange an** and **Ich fange schon mal an** in a situation where another person is not ready yet. What does *schon mal* add?\
   a. The speaker will go ahead and begin now, before the other person.\
   b. The speaker has never started before.\
   c. The speaker asks whether the task has happened before.\
   d. The speaker refuses to start.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** In this use, *schon mal* adds the idea of doing something ahead of the other person or ahead of the rest.

</details>

15. Reconstruct the question from **du / schon mal / in Hamburg / warst**. Which order is natural?\
   a. Du schon mal warst in Hamburg?\
   b. Schon mal du warst in Hamburg?\
   c. Warst du schon mal in Hamburg?\
   d. Warst schon mal du Hamburg in?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A yes/no question begins with the verb: **Warst du schon mal in Hamburg?** The chunk *schon mal* stays together.

</details>

16. Which sentence does **not** use *schon mal* to talk about a previous experience?\
   a. Warst du schon mal in Wien?\
   b. Ich war schon mal in Berlin.\
   c. Hast du das schon mal probiert?\
   d. Geh schon mal vor.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** **Geh schon mal vor** uses the “go ahead / ahead of the rest” meaning. The other three use *schon mal* for something that happened before at least once.

</details>

17. A friend asks **Warst du mal in Berlin?** How should a beginning learner interpret this based on the lesson?\
   a. It can occur in casual German, but **Warst du schon mal in Berlin?** is the clearer pattern to learn.\
   b. It is always incorrect because *schon* can never be omitted.\
   c. It means only “Are you in Berlin now?”\
   d. It means “Go ahead to Berlin.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The shorter question can occur, but the lesson recommends keeping **schon mal** together while you are learning the pattern.

</details>

18. You are both preparing dinner. Your friend is still setting the table, but you can begin cooking now. In **Ich fange schon mal an**, what does *schon mal* mean in this situation?\
   a. “I have never started.”\
   b. “Have I started before?”\
   c. “I started once in the past.”\
   d. “I'll go ahead and start now.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The context selects the second lesson-taught meaning: the speaker begins now, ahead of the other person's readiness.

</details>

19. **Select all that apply.** Someone asks **Warst du schon mal in Wien?** Which replies fit the lesson's standard question-and-answer set?\
   a. Ja, einmal.\
   b. Ja, schon.\
   c. Nein, noch nie.\
   d. Nein, nicht schon mal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** The lesson gives **Ja, einmal**, **Ja, schon**, and **Nein, noch nie** as natural answers. **Nein, nicht schon mal** is the negation error the lesson warns against.

</details>
