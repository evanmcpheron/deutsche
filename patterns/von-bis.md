# von … bis …

## What It Means

*von … bis …* marks a **span**: a stretch of time, a range of numbers, or a journey between two places.

[von](../prepositions/von.md) gives the starting point; *bis* gives the end point.

## In Use

### Times and days

**von Montag bis Freitag**
from Monday to Friday

**von acht bis fünf**
from eight to five

**Wir haben von neun bis achtzehn Uhr geöffnet.**
We're open from nine to six.

### Places

**Wir fahren von Berlin nach Hamburg.**
We're going from Berlin to Hamburg. *(note: **nach** for the destination city, not *bis*)*

**Der Bus fährt von hier bis zum Bahnhof.**
The bus goes from here to the station.

### Ranges

**Kinder von 6 bis 12 Jahren**
children aged 6 to 12

## A Case Note

*bis* on its own takes no article and needs no case decision — *bis Montag*, *bis morgen*, *bis acht*. When an article is needed, German inserts a second preposition, usually *zu*: **bis zum Bahnhof**, **bis zur Kirche**.

That's why you see both *bis Berlin* (no article) and *bis zum Bahnhof* (article, so *zu* + dative).

## *bis* on Its Own

Extremely common in goodbyes:

- **Bis morgen!** — See you tomorrow!
- **Bis später!** — See you later!
- **Bis bald!** — See you soon!
- **Bis dann!** — See you then!

These are among the most useful four-word-or-fewer sentences in German.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using *bis* for a travel destination.** *Ich fahre bis Hamburg* means you're travelling as far as Hamburg (perhaps a leg of a longer trip). For "I'm going to Hamburg," say **nach Hamburg**.
- **Adding an article after bare *bis*.** *bis der Bahnhof* → **bis zum Bahnhof**.
- **Using *zu* for the start.** The start is *von*: *von Montag*, not *zu Montag*.
