# zu Hause vs. nach Hause

**Level:** A1 · **Tier 1** · **Active**

## The Simple Explanation

Two fixed expressions, one letter of difference in meaning:

> **zu Hause** — **at home.** You're there. Location.
> **nach Hause** — **home(ward).** You're going there. Movement.

This is the clearest example in the language of German's location/movement split — and because both are frozen expressions, there are no cases to work out.

## Contrasting Examples

**Ich bin zu Hause.**
I'm at home.

**Ich gehe nach Hause.**
I'm going home.

---

**Wir bleiben heute zu Hause.**
We're staying home today.

**Wir fahren jetzt nach Hause.**
We're driving home now.

---

**Er ist nicht zu Hause.**
He isn't home.

**Wann kommst du nach Hause?**
When are you coming home?

---

**Zu Hause ist es schöner.**
It's nicer at home.

**Ich muss nach Hause.**
I have to go home. *(no verb of motion needed — it's understood)*

## Rule of Thumb

Look at the **verb**:

| Verb | Expression |
|---|---|
| sein, bleiben, arbeiten, essen *(static)* | **zu Hause** |
| gehen, fahren, kommen, müssen *(motion)* | **nach Hause** |

Or ask the German question:

- **Wo?** (where are you?) → **zu Hause**
- **Wohin?** (where are you going?) → **nach Hause**

That's the same *Wo?/Wohin?* test used for the two-way prepositions — see [Cases at a Glance](../reference/cases-at-a-glance.md).

## Why *Hause* and not *Haus*?

*Hause* is a leftover old dative form, preserved inside these two frozen expressions. You don't need to understand it or reproduce it anywhere else — *zu Hause* and *nach Hause* are simply learned whole.

You'll also see **zuhause** written as one word; both spellings are accepted. *nach Hause* stays two words.

## Common Mistakes

- ***Ich gehe zu Hause*** *(meaning "I'm going home")* → **nach Hause.** The most common version of this error.
- ***Ich bin nach Hause*** *(meaning "I'm at home")* → **zu Hause.**
- **Adding an article.** *zu dem Hause* is not a thing. These expressions never take an article.
- **Using *Haus*.** *nach Haus* is heard colloquially but *nach Hause* is the standard form.

## Self-Check

1. You're sitting on your sofa. Which is right?
   a. Ich bin zu Hause.
   b. Ich bin nach Hause.

2. You're leaving the office. Which is right?
   a. Ich gehe zu Hause.
   b. Ich gehe nach Hause.

3. Fill in the pattern: *Wir bleiben heute ___.* (staying home)

4. Which question does *nach Hause* answer — *Wo?* or *Wohin?*

5. **Produce it:** Ask a friend when they're coming home.

6. **Produce it:** Say that he isn't at home.
