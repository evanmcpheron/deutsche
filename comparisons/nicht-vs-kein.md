# nicht vs. kein

**Compares:** [nicht](../words/nicht.md) · [kein](../words/kein.md)
**Level:** A1 · **Tier 1**

## The Simple Explanation

German has two negation words, and the choice depends on **what you're negating**.

> **kein** — negates a **noun** that has *ein* or no article at all.
> **nicht** — negates everything else: verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and nouns with *der/die/das* or a possessive.

English uses "not" and "no" for the same split ("I don't have time" / "I have no time"), but German makes the choice compulsory.

## Contrasting Examples

**Ich habe keine Zeit.**
I don't have time. *(noun with no article → kein)*

**Ich habe die Zeit nicht.**
I don't have the time. *(specific, definite → nicht)*

---

**Das ist kein Problem.**
That's not a problem. *(a noun)*

**Das ist nicht gut.**
That's not good. *(an adjective)*

---

**Er ist kein Arzt.**
He isn't a doctor. *(profession as a noun)*

**Er ist nicht mein Arzt.**
He isn't my doctor. *(possessive → nicht)*

---

**Ich trinke keinen Kaffee.**
I don't drink coffee.

**Ich trinke nicht.**
I don't drink. *(no noun at all → nicht)*

## Rule of Thumb

Look at what comes right after the negation:

| What follows | Use |
|---|---|
| a noun with *ein* | **kein** |
| a noun with no article | **kein** |
| a plural noun with no article | **kein** |
| a noun with *der/die/das* | **nicht** |
| a noun with *mein/dein/…* | **nicht** |
| a name | **nicht** |
| an adjective or adverb | **nicht** |
| nothing (just the verb) | **nicht** |

The shortcut most learners find useful: **if you could put *ein* in front of it, use *kein*.**

## Common Mistakes

- ***Ich habe nicht Zeit.*** → **Ich habe keine Zeit.** The most common negation error at A1, by some distance.
- ***Das ist kein gut.*** → **Das ist nicht gut.** Adjectives never take *kein*.
- **Forgetting the accusative ending.** *Ich habe kein Hunger* → **keinen Hunger**.
- **Doubling negatives.** German doesn't stack them the way casual English does.
