# Quiz: denn vs. weil

[← Back to **denn vs. weil**](denn-vs-weil.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

Both words mean "because." Almost everything here is about what happens to the **verb**.

---

## Part A — Where Does the Verb Go?

1. **Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn …**\
   a. … ich müde bin.\
   b. … müde ich bin.\
   c. … bin ich müde.\
   d. … ich bin müde.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** After *denn* nothing changes — normal word order, verb in second position. Option a is *weil* word order applied to the wrong conjunction.

</details>

2. **Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil …**\
   a. … ich bin müde.\
   b. … bin ich müde.\
   c. … ich müde bin.\
   d. … müde bin ich.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *weil* pushes the verb to the very end of its clause. Option a is the classic learner error — you'll hear native speakers do it casually, but it isn't what you want to learn.

</details>

3. What is the actual difference between the two words?\
   a. Their meanings are completely different.\
   b. The word order in the second clause.\
   c. *denn* is more polite.\
   d. *weil* is used only in questions.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Meaning-wise they're close enough that at A1 you can use whichever you can produce correctly. What differs is where the verb lands.

</details>

4. Someone asks **Warum kommst du nicht?** Which answer works?\
   a. Denn ich keine Zeit habe.\
   b. Denn ich habe keine Zeit.\
   c. Weil ich keine Zeit habe.\
   d. Weil ich habe keine Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Answering *Warum?* needs *weil*, with the verb at the end. Options a and b fail for the same reason, whatever their word order — see the next question.

</details>

5. Why can neither *denn* option in question 4 work?\
   a. Because *keine* is incorrect.\
   b. Because *Zeit* is a noun.\
   c. Because *denn* connects two subordinate clauses.\
   d. Because *denn* cannot begin a standalone answer sentence.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *denn* joins two full main clauses inside one sentence; it can't open a sentence on its own. That's why it can never answer a bare *Warum?*

</details>

6. Where does the verb go after *weil*?\
   a. In second position.\
   b. Directly after *weil*.\
   c. At the end of the clause.\
   d. Before the subject.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Right to the end, behind everything else in that clause. The longer the clause, the further it travels — which is where the practice is.

</details>

## Part B — Diagnose the Error

7. A learner writes **Ich gehe nicht, denn ich krank bin.** What is wrong?\
   a. After *denn*, normal word order remains: *denn ich bin krank.*\
   b. *denn* must be replaced with *weil*.\
   c. *krank* must come before *ich*.\
   d. The comma is missing.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The verb was sent to the end, which is what *weil* asks for. After *denn* you simply add a comma and carry on speaking normally.

</details>

8. Another learner writes **Ich gehe nicht, weil ich bin krank.** What is wrong?\
   a. After *weil*, normal word order remains.\
   b. After *weil*, the verb goes to the end: *weil ich krank bin.*\
   c. *weil* must be replaced with *denn*.\
   d. *krank* is incorrect here.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The mirror image of question 7 — the verb stayed put when it should have moved. These two errors between them account for most *weil* mistakes at A1.

</details>

9. **Was ist denn das?** What is *denn* doing in that question?\
   a. It gives a reason.\
   b. It connects two main clauses.\
   c. It means “because” here.\
   d. It is the question particle here and has nothing to do with giving a reason.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Two different words that happen to be spelled the same. The particle softens a question and adds curiosity or surprise; it has nothing to do with reasons.

</details>

10. Which sentence can begin with its conjunction?\
   a. Weil ich müde bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.\
   b. Denn ich müde bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.\
   c. Denn ich bin müde, bleibe ich zu Hause.\
   d. Weil ich bin müde, bleibe ich zu Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Only *weil* can lead. Note that the main clause then starts with its verb — *bleibe ich* — because the whole *weil* clause has taken first position.

</details>

11. What does the lesson suggest as a practical A1 strategy?\
   a. Always use *weil* and never *denn*.\
   b. Use *denn* while subordinate-clause word order is still difficult, then add *weil*.\
   c. Avoid both words.\
   d. Always use *denn*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *denn* costs you nothing while you're still building sentences slowly. Switch as your word order settles, since *weil* is what you'll hear most in conversation.

</details>

12. **Wir gehen nicht, denn es regnet** and **Wir gehen nicht, weil es regnet.** Why do they look almost identical?\
   a. Because *denn* and *weil* are the same word.\
   b. Because the sentence is a question.\
   c. Because *es regnet* has only one verb, so the word-order difference is barely visible.\
   d. Because both sentences are incorrect.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** With a two-word clause there's nowhere for the verb to move to. The difference appears the moment the clause has more in it — an object, a time word, an adjective.

</details>

13. Which pair is correctly formed?\
   a. …, denn ich Deutsch lerne. / …, weil ich lerne Deutsch.\
   b. …, denn ich lerne Deutsch. / …, weil ich lerne Deutsch.\
   c. …, denn ich Deutsch lerne. / …, weil ich Deutsch lerne.\
   d. …, denn ich lerne Deutsch. / …, weil ich Deutsch lerne.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *denn* keeps the verb second; *weil* sends it behind the object. Each of the other options gets at least one half backwards.

</details>

## Part C — Build It Correctly

14. **Ich lerne Deutsch, weil …**\
   a. … ich in Berlin wohne.\
   b. … ich wohne in Berlin.\
   c. … in Berlin ich wohne.\
   d. … wohne ich in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The verb goes past *in Berlin* to the end. This is the version worth drilling, because the longer clause is where the movement actually shows.

</details>

15. **Ich lerne Deutsch, denn …**\
   a. … ich in Berlin wohne.\
   b. … in Berlin ich wohne.\
   c. … wohne ich in Berlin.\
   d. … ich wohne in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Nothing moves — it reads exactly like a standalone sentence. Compare question 14: same information, two different shapes.

</details>

16. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Warum isst du nichts?
B: ___
```

   a. Denn ich habe keinen Hunger.\
   b. Denn ich keinen Hunger habe.\
   c. Weil ich keinen Hunger habe.\
   d. Weil ich habe keinen Hunger.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Warum?* is answered with *weil*, verb last. Note *keinen Hunger* rather than *nicht Hunger* — a bare noun still takes *kein*.

</details>

17. Which sentence is correct German?\
   a. Er kommt nicht, denn er ist krank.\
   b. Er kommt nicht, denn er krank ist.\
   c. Denn er ist krank, kommt er nicht.\
   d. Er kommt nicht, denn ist er krank.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Comma, then a completely normal main clause. Option c fails because *denn* can't open a sentence, and d inverts the verb and subject for no reason.

</details>

18. Which sentence is correct German?\
   a. Er kommt nicht, weil er ist krank.\
   b. Er kommt nicht, weil er krank ist.\
   c. Er kommt nicht, weil ist er krank.\
   d. Weil er ist krank, kommt er nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Verb at the end of the *weil* clause. Option d gets the front position right but leaves the verb in the wrong place inside the clause.

</details>

19. Which word do German speakers use more in everyday conversation?\
   a. weil\
   b. denn\
   c. Both about equally often.\
   d. Neither.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *weil* is the one you'll hear constantly, which is why it's worth the extra word-order work. *denn* reads slightly more written or deliberate.

</details>

20. How would you judge **Ich bleibe hier, denn ich keine Zeit habe**?\
   a. Correct — exactly that meaning.\
   b. Incorrect — after *denn*, the verb stays in second position: *denn ich habe keine Zeit.*\
   c. Correct German, but it means something different.\
   d. Correct, but impolite.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The *weil* pattern has been used with *denn*. Either fix works: change the word order, or change the conjunction to *weil*.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

21. **Select all that apply.** Which sentences are correct?\
   a. Ich komme nicht, denn ich bin krank.\
   b. Ich komme nicht, weil ich krank bin.\
   c. Weil ich krank bin, komme ich nicht.\
   d. Denn ich krank bin, komme ich nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** Three correct ways to say the same thing. Option d breaks both rules at once — *denn* can't start a sentence, and it doesn't take verb-final order either.

</details>

22. Build the sentence with *weil*. Start with **Ich komme nicht,**

```text
weil / keine / habe / ich / Zeit
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich komme nicht, weil ich keine Zeit habe.**

**Explanation:** *habe* travels all the way past *keine Zeit* to the end. If you produced *weil ich habe keine Zeit*, you've made the most common error in the whole lesson.

</details>

23. Build the same idea with *denn*. Start with **Ich komme nicht,**

```text
denn / keine / habe / ich / Zeit
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich komme nicht, denn ich habe keine Zeit.**

**Explanation:** Same five words, and this time the verb stays in second position. Writing questions 22 and 23 one after the other is the single most useful exercise here.

</details>

24. Rewrite this sentence using *weil*: **Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn das Wetter ist schlecht.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil das Wetter schlecht ist.**

**Explanation:** *ist* moves behind *schlecht*. You could also front the clause: *Weil das Wetter schlecht ist, bleibe ich zu Hause* — and then the main clause starts with its verb.

</details>

25. **Produce it:** Someone asks *Warum lernst du Deutsch?* Answer in one sentence.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Weil ich nach Deutschland fahren möchte.**

**Explanation:** Any *weil* clause with the verb at the end works — *Weil ich in Berlin wohne*, *Weil es mir Spaß macht*, *Weil ich es brauche*. What you can't do here is start with *denn*: it can't open a sentence, so it can't answer *Warum?*

</details>
