# Quiz: noch vs. schon

[← Back to **noch vs. schon**](noch-vs-schon.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

Nothing here tells you which of the two words is being tested. Read the situation first and decide for yourself.

---

## Part A — Which Sentence Fits?

1. You call a friend at seven in the morning. He doesn't pick up, and you know he's a late sleeper.\
   a. Er schläft schon.\
   b. Er schläft wieder.\
   c. Er schläft nicht mehr.\
   d. Er schläft noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The sleep started last night and hasn't ended — nothing has run out yet. *schon* would claim he fell asleep earlier than you'd expect, which is an odd thing to say at 7 a.m.

</details>

2. You call the same friend at nine in the evening. He went to bed at eight, which is very early for him.\
   a. Er schläft noch.\
   b. Er schläft schon.\
   c. Er schläft nicht mehr.\
   d. Er schläft noch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The sleeping has already begun, and sooner than anyone expected — that gap between fact and expectation is *schon*. Notice that the same three words describe both questions; only the small word changes.

</details>

3. The restaurant opens at six and closes at eleven. It is half past ten.\
   a. Das Restaurant ist noch offen.\
   b. Das Restaurant ist schon offen.\
   c. Das Restaurant ist schon zu.\
   d. Das Restaurant ist noch nicht offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The opening hours haven't run out. Half an hour is left, and *noch* is exactly the word for a supply that isn't finished.

</details>

4. The restaurant opens at six. It is a quarter to six and the door is already unlocked.\
   a. Das Restaurant ist schon offen.\
   b. Das Restaurant ist noch offen.\
   c. Das Restaurant ist noch nicht offen.\
   d. Das Restaurant ist nicht mehr offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Open, and ahead of schedule. Compare question 3: same restaurant, same adjective, and only the relationship to the expected time has moved.

</details>

5. You have written half of a report that is due tomorrow. Your boss asks how it's going.\
   a. Ich bin schon fertig.\
   b. Ich bin nicht mehr fertig.\
   c. Ich bin noch nicht fertig.\
   d. Ich bin schon nicht fertig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Not finished, and still expected to finish — *noch nicht*. Option d is the trap: *schon nicht* is not the negative of *schon*, and it doesn't mean "not yet."

</details>

6. You hand the report in a day early and your boss looks surprised.\
   a. Ich bin noch fertig.\
   b. Ich bin noch nicht fertig.\
   c. Ich bin nicht mehr fertig.\
   d. Ich bin schon fertig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Done, sooner than expected. The surprise in the situation is the surest sign that *schon* is the word you want.

</details>

7. Which is the negative of **schon**?\
   a. schon nicht\
   b. nicht mehr\
   c. noch nicht\
   d. nicht schon

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The negatives cross over, which is why this pair has to be memorized rather than derived. *schon* → *noch nicht*; the other word's negative is in the next question.

</details>

8. Which is the negative of **noch**?\
   a. nicht mehr\
   b. noch nicht\
   c. schon nicht\
   d. nicht noch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch* → *nicht mehr*. Together with question 7 this is the whole table: already / not yet, still / not any more. Neither pair lines up the way English suggests.

</details>

## Part B — Interpret and Contrast

9. One of these asks whether someone has arrived. Which?\
   a. Bist du noch da?\
   b. Bist du schon da?\
   c. Beide Fragen.\
   d. Keine von beiden.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *schon da* asks about arrival by now. *noch da* assumes you were already there and checks whether that's still true — the question you'd ask on the phone after a silence.

</details>

10. What is the difference between **Wir warten schon eine Stunde** and **Wir warten noch eine Stunde**?\
   a. Beide sagen genau dasselbe.\
   b. Der erste Satz ist eine Frage.\
   c. Der erste Satz spricht über die Zukunft.\
   d. Der erste zählt vergangene Zeit, der zweite kommende Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *schon eine Stunde* = we've been here an hour already. *noch eine Stunde* = there's another hour to go. Same noun, opposite directions in time.

</details>

11. Look at the times:

```text
14:00 — Der Bus fährt ab.
15:00 — Der Bus fährt.
16:00 — Der Bus fährt.
```

   Es ist 16:00. Welcher Satz passt?\
   a. Der Bus fährt noch.\
   b. Der Bus fährt schon.\
   c. Der Bus fährt noch nicht.\
   d. Der Bus ist schon da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The journey started two hours ago and hasn't ended. *schon* would only fit the moment of departure, when the leaving was the news.

</details>

12. Read the sequence:

```text
Montag: Anna sucht eine Wohnung.
Dienstag: Anna sucht eine Wohnung.
Mittwoch: Anna findet eine Wohnung.
```

   Am Mittwochabend. Welcher Satz passt?\
   a. Anna sucht noch eine Wohnung.\
   b. Anna sucht noch nicht.\
   c. Anna sucht wieder eine Wohnung.\
   d. Anna hat schon eine Wohnung.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The search has ended, so nothing is continuing — option a describes Monday and Tuesday, not Wednesday evening. *schon* fits the result arriving after two days of looking.

</details>

13. You are on the platform. The train was due at ten. It is five past, the track is empty, and the train is listed as delayed.\
   a. Der Zug ist schon weg.\
   b. Der Zug ist nicht mehr da.\
   c. Der Zug ist noch nicht da.\
   d. Der Zug ist schon da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Not arrived, and still expected — *noch nicht*. Options a and b both put the train in the past, which the delay notice rules out.

</details>

14. Different day. You reach the platform at 09:55 and the display shows that your train left at 09:50.\
   a. Der Zug ist noch nicht da.\
   b. Der Zug ist noch da.\
   c. Der Zug ist schon weg.\
   d. Der Zug kommt noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Gone, and earlier than you managed to get there. Compare question 13 — the two sentences look almost identical in English ("the train isn't here") but describe opposite situations.

</details>

15. You want to say: **I haven't eaten yet.** A learner writes **Ich habe schon nicht gegessen.**\
   a. Richtig — genau diese Bedeutung.\
   b. Für diese Bedeutung falsch — richtig ist *noch nicht*.\
   c. Richtig, aber unhöflich.\
   d. Richtig, aber nur in Fragen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Correct: **Ich habe noch nicht gegessen.** Sticking *nicht* onto *schon* does not produce "not yet" — the negative of *schon* is a different word entirely.

</details>

16. You want to say: **He doesn't live here any more.** A learner writes **Er wohnt nicht noch hier.**\
   a. Richtig — genau diese Bedeutung.\
   b. Falsch — richtig ist *Er wohnt nicht mehr hier.*\
   c. Korrektes Deutsch, aber es bedeutet etwas anderes.\
   d. Richtig, aber unhöflich.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The other half of the same table. *nicht noch* is not a pair German uses; "not any more" is *nicht mehr*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Attitude

17. Complete the dialogue. B ate an hour ago.

```text
A: Wollen wir essen gehen?
B: Danke, ich habe ___ gegessen.
```

   a. schon\
   b. noch\
   c. noch nicht\
   d. nicht mehr

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The eating is behind him, and mentioning it declines the invitation politely. Option c would accept it — "I haven't eaten yet" is close to a yes.

</details>

18. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Wohnst du ___ in Köln?
B: Ja, seit zehn Jahren.
```

   a. schon nicht\
   b. nicht mehr\
   c. wieder\
   d. noch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** A is checking whether an old fact still holds. B confirms it, and *seit zehn Jahren* shows there was no break — which would have ruled out *wieder*.

</details>

19. Complete the dialogue. It is half past seven in the morning and the boss usually arrives at eight.

```text
A: Ist der Chef schon im Büro?
B: Nein, er ist ___ nicht da.
```

   a. schon\
   b. nicht mehr\
   c. wieder\
   d. noch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** A asked with *schon*, so the natural negative answer comes back as *noch nicht*. Option b would mean he came in and has already left again.

</details>

20. You are nervous before an exam and a friend says **Das wird schon.** What is *schon* doing?\
   a. Es heißt hier "bereits".\
   b. Es beruhigt — "keine Sorge".\
   c. Es fragt nach der Zeit.\
   d. Es verneint den Satz.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** This is *schon* as a particle rather than as "already." Recognize it — a word-by-word reading ("that will already") gets you nowhere.

</details>

21. Which sentence can carry a quiet "and I wish he'd go"?\
   a. Er ist schon hier.\
   b. Er ist noch hier.\
   c. Er ist nicht mehr hier.\
   d. Er ist noch nicht hier.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *noch* points at something that hasn't ended — including a visit that has gone on long enough. Neither word is neutral; the attitude is part of the meaning.

</details>

22. And which one can carry "and I wasn't ready"?\
   a. Er ist schon hier.\
   b. Er ist noch hier.\
   c. Er ist noch nicht hier.\
   d. Er ist nicht mehr hier.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Early arrival, and the speaker is caught out. Put questions 21 and 22 side by side: one word, two completely different social situations.

</details>

23. In which sentence does *noch* **not** mean "still"?\
   a. Ich bin noch hier.\
   b. Das Restaurant ist noch offen.\
   c. Möchtest du noch einen Kaffee?\
   d. Er schläft noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Here *noch* means "another" — one more cup. It's the same underlying idea (the coffee hasn't run out either), but fixing *noch* as "still" makes this sentence unreadable.

</details>

24. You have a concert ticket. The doors open at seven. It is half past six and a queue has formed outside a locked entrance.\
   a. Die Türen sind schon offen.\
   b. Die Türen sind nicht mehr offen.\
   c. Die Türen sind noch zu.\
   d. Die Türen sind wieder zu.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Shut, and expected to open — the closed state hasn't ended yet. Options b and d both need the doors to have been open earlier.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

25. **Select all that apply.** Which sentences say that something has **not happened yet**?\
   a. Der Zug ist noch nicht da.\
   b. Ich bin schon fertig.\
   c. Wir haben noch keine Tickets.\
   d. Ich habe noch nicht gegessen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c, d**

**Explanation:** All three keep *noch* in front of the negation, which is what produces "yet" and leaves the thing still expected. Note that c uses *noch keine* rather than *noch nicht*, because a noun is being negated.

</details>

26. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You promised to be home by seven. It is ten past, you're still at your desk, and your partner calls.

```text
Ich bin ___ im Büro — tut mir leid.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **noch**

**Explanation:** You haven't left, and the other person expected you to have. *schon im Büro* would only make sense first thing in the morning, when arriving early is the news.

</details>

27. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   Your guests were invited for eight. At half past seven the doorbell rings.

```text
Sie sind ___ da!
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **schon**

**Explanation:** Arrival ahead of the expected time. *noch da* would describe guests who haven't left yet — the end of the evening rather than the start.

</details>

28. Fill in the missing words. No options given. Two words.

   A colleague asks whether you have booked the flights for the trip. You haven't.

```text
Ich habe die Flüge ___ ___ gebucht.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **noch nicht**

**Explanation:** *Die Flüge* is definite, so the negation is *nicht* rather than *kein* — and *noch* in front of it says the booking is still to come. The order is fixed.

</details>

29. Put the words in order.

```text
nicht / bin / noch / ich / fertig
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin noch nicht fertig.**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* stays together, in that order, in front of the adjective. This is the single most useful sentence on the page — you'll need it constantly.

</details>

30. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Der Laden ist noch offen.**\
   New situation: it is a quarter to seven in the morning, the shop normally opens at seven, and the shutters are already up.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Der Laden ist schon offen.**

**Explanation:** Change only the small word. *noch offen* means the closing time hasn't arrived; *schon offen* means the opening time has been beaten. Both describe an open shop, at opposite ends of the day.

</details>
