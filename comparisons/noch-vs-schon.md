# noch vs. schon

**Compares:** [noch](../words/noch.md) · [schon](../words/schon.md)
**Level:** A1 · **Tier 1**

[Category Index](./README.md) | [Next → erst vs. schon](erst-vs-schon.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## The Simple Explanation

Both words compare a fact against an expectation — but they point in opposite directions.

> **noch** — it hasn't run out / it hasn't happened yet. Looking at what's **still going**.
> **schon** — it has already happened, sooner than you'd think. Looking at what's **already done**.

Picture a task with a start and an end. *schon* is about having passed the start. *noch* is about not having reached the end.

## Contrasting Examples

**Ich bin noch hier.**
I'm still here. *(I haven't left)*

**Ich bin schon hier.**
I'm already here. *(I arrived early)*

---

**Er schläft noch.**
He's still asleep.

**Er schläft schon.**
He's already asleep.

---

**Das Restaurant ist noch offen.**
The restaurant is still open.

**Das Restaurant ist schon offen.**
The restaurant is open already.

---

**Ich bin noch nicht fertig.**
I'm not finished yet.

**Ich bin schon fertig.**
I'm finished already.

## Rule of Thumb

Ask yourself: **am I talking about something that hasn't ended, or something that has already started?**

- Hasn't ended → **noch**
- Already started → **schon**

And for the negative, remember this pair rather than deriving it:

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| **schon** (already) | **noch nicht** (not yet) |
| **noch** (still) | **nicht mehr** (not any more) |

That table is worth memorizing outright. The negatives don't line up the way English suggests, which is where most errors come from.

## Common Mistakes

- **Using *schon nicht* for "not yet."** The negative of *schon* is **noch nicht**.
- **Using *nicht noch* for "not any more."** That's **nicht mehr**.
- **Fixing *noch* as "still" and *schon* as "already."** Both words do more than that — *noch einen Kaffee* is "another," and *schon* can be pure reassurance (*Das wird schon*).
- **Hearing them as neutral.** Both carry an attitude. *Er ist schon hier* can mean "and I wasn't ready"; *Er ist noch hier* can mean "and I wish he'd go."
