# Quiz: gerade vs. jetzt

[← Back to **gerade vs. jetzt**](gerade-vs-jetzt.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

Nothing here tells you which word is being tested. Decide whether the sentence describes an **action in progress** or marks a **change**.

---

## Part A — In Progress, or a Change?

1. Your phone rings and you are halfway through dinner.\
   a. Ich esse gerade.\
   b. Ich esse jetzt.\
   c. Ich bin essen.\
   d. Ich esse schon.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** You're in the middle of it, which is what *gerade* says. Option b would sound like an announcement — "right, I'm eating now" — rather than an explanation for why you can't talk.

</details>

2. You've just finished studying and start your first job this month.\
   a. Ich arbeite gerade.\
   b. Ich bin arbeiten.\
   c. Ich arbeite gerade jetzt.\
   d. Ich arbeite jetzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** This is a change of life stage — as opposed to before. *jetzt* marks that shift; *gerade* would only describe what you're doing this second.

</details>

3. Everyone has their coats on and you're calling the departure.\
   a. Wir gehen gerade.\
   b. Gerade gehen wir.\
   c. Jetzt gehen wir.\
   d. Wir gehen gerade jetzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A decision, not a description — *Jetzt gehen wir* is close to "right, off we go." Front position suits it, because you're marking a point on the timeline.

</details>

4. You're halfway out of the door when someone calls after you.\
   a. Jetzt gehen wir.\
   b. Wir gehen gerade.\
   c. Wir gehen jetzt schon.\
   d. Wir gehen wieder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The leaving is under way, so it's a description of what's happening. Compare question 3: same departure, one moment apart, different word.

</details>

5. A friend texts to ask what you're up to at this second.\
   a. Was machst du jetzt?\
   b. Was machst du gerade?\
   c. Was machst du schon?\
   d. Was machst du immer?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Was machst du gerade?* is the standard message. *Was machst du jetzt?* leans towards "what are you going to do now?" — a question about the next step.

</details>

6. Your colleague moved to Berlin last year and lives there these days.\
   a. Er ist gerade in Berlin.\
   b. Er ist noch in Berlin.\
   c. Er ist schon in Berlin.\
   d. Er ist jetzt in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** A permanent change — Berlin is where he lives as of now. Option a would make it sound like a short trip.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

7. Same colleague, different year: he's in Berlin for a three-day conference.\
   a. Er ist jetzt in Berlin.\
   b. Er ist wieder in Berlin.\
   c. Er ist noch in Berlin.\
   d. Er ist gerade in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Temporary, happening at the moment. Put this beside question 6: one word decides whether your colleague has emigrated or is away for the week.

</details>

8. Which English test helps you choose between the two?\
   a. "Already" → gerade; "still" → jetzt.\
   b. "Again" → gerade; "now" → jetzt.\
   c. "At the moment" → gerade; "as of now" → jetzt.\
   d. "Not yet" → gerade; "any more" → jetzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** If you could add "at the moment," use *gerade*. If you could add "as of now," use *jetzt*. It's a quick check that works for most A1 sentences.

</details>

9. A learner writes **Ich bin arbeiten.** What is wrong?\
   a. *arbeiten* muss zu *arbeite* werden, sonst nichts.\
   b. Deutsch hat keine *-ing*-Form — richtig ist *Ich arbeite gerade.*\
   c. Man braucht *werden* statt *sein*.\
   d. *arbeiten* muss am Satzanfang stehen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** There is no German equivalent of "am -ing." The plain present tense plus *gerade* does the whole job.

</details>

10. Someone calls while you're at your desk under a deadline. Which reply means "call me back"?\
   a. Ich arbeite jetzt.\
   b. Ich arbeite viel.\
   c. Ich arbeite hier.\
   d. Ich arbeite gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *gerade* says you're mid-task, so this isn't a good moment. This is the single most useful sentence on the page.

</details>

11. And which of those sounds like a life update rather than a reason to hang up?\
   a. Ich arbeite jetzt.\
   b. Ich arbeite gerade.\
   c. Ich arbeite gerade nicht.\
   d. Ich arbeite noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Ich arbeite jetzt* marks a change — you have a job now, whereas before you didn't. Say it to someone ringing you and they'll congratulate you rather than ring off.

</details>

12. **gerade jetzt** — when do people use it?\
   a. Wenn etwas jeden Tag passiert.\
   b. Wenn etwas schon vorbei ist.\
   c. Wenn etwas erst später kommt.\
   d. Wenn der Zeitpunkt besonders ungünstig ist.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Stacking the two is emphatic and usually complains about bad timing — *Warum gerade jetzt?* Most sentences want one word or the other, not both.

</details>

13. **Sie ist gerade gegangen.** Was stimmt?\
   a. Sie geht in einer Minute.\
   b. Sie ist vor kurzem gegangen.\
   c. Sie ist noch hier.\
   d. Sie geht jeden Tag um diese Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** With a past form, *gerade* means "just" — a moment ago. The word still points at the present moment; it says the leaving happened right on its edge.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

14. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Kann ich Herrn Weber sprechen?
B: Er ist ___ nicht da. Rufen Sie in einer Stunde noch mal an.
```

   a. gerade\
   b. jetzt\
   c. schon\
   d. immer

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *gerade nicht da* is the standard office phrase — away at the moment, back later. That's exactly what B's second sentence confirms.

</details>

15. Complete the dialogue. B has just started at university.

```text
A: Und was machst du nach der Schule?
B: ___ studiere ich in Leipzig.
```

   a. Gerade\
   b. Schon\
   c. Jetzt\
   d. Wieder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** School then, university now — a change of stage. Note the word order: with *Jetzt* in front, the verb follows immediately.

</details>

16. Which sentence marks a **change** rather than describing an action in progress?\
   a. Wir essen gerade.\
   b. Jetzt wohnen wir in Köln.\
   c. Er telefoniert gerade.\
   d. Sie kocht gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Living somewhere isn't an action you're in the middle of — it's a state that has changed. The other three are all things you could interrupt.

</details>

17. You want to say: **I'm in the middle of cooking.** A learner writes **Ich koche jetzt.**\
   a. Falsches Deutsch.\
   b. Richtig — genau diese Bedeutung.\
   c. Korrektes Deutsch, aber es klingt eher nach einer Entscheidung.\
   d. Richtig, aber unhöflich.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The sentence is fine — it just sounds like you've decided to start cooking, not that you're standing over a pan. For that: **Ich koche gerade.**

</details>

18. Which question asks about what comes next as much as what's happening?\
   a. Was machst du jetzt?\
   b. Was machst du gerade?\
   c. Was machst du immer?\
   d. Was machst du wieder?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *jetzt* can mean "from now on," so the question stretches into the near future. *gerade* is strictly about this second.

</details>

19. In which sentence is *gerade* the time word that *jetzt* could compete with?\
   a. Ich arbeite gerade.\
   b. Der Weg ist gerade.\
   c. Gehen Sie geradeaus.\
   d. Das ist nicht gerade billig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Only a uses *gerade* as a time adverb. In b it's the adjective "straight," in c it's part of *geradeaus*, and in d it's the set phrase *nicht gerade* — "not exactly."

</details>

20. **Er ist gerade nicht da.** What does the sentence quietly imply?\
   a. Er kommt nie wieder.\
   b. Er war noch nie hier.\
   c. Er ist kurz weg und kommt zurück.\
   d. Er arbeitet nicht mehr hier.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *gerade* limits the absence to this moment, so a return is assumed. Drop it and *Er ist nicht da* sounds much more final.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

21. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does *gerade* mean "at this moment"?\
   a. Ich lese gerade.\
   b. Der Tisch ist gerade.\
   c. Sie ist gerade angekommen.\
   d. Er telefoniert gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, d**

**Explanation:** Both describe something under way right now. In c the past form shifts it to "a moment ago," and in b *gerade* is the adjective "straight."

</details>

22. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A friend calls while you're sitting on a crowded bus and can't really talk.

```text
Ich sitze ___ im Bus — ich rufe später an.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **gerade**

**Explanation:** Mid-situation, so *gerade*. *Ich sitze jetzt im Bus* would sound like you'd just announced a change of transport.

</details>

23. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You've moved from Munich to Hamburg and are telling an old friend where you live.

```text
Ich wohne ___ in Hamburg.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **jetzt**

**Explanation:** Munich before, Hamburg as of now — a change. *gerade in Hamburg* would suggest you were there for the weekend.

</details>

24. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich arbeite jetzt bei einer Bank.**\
   New situation: a friend has phoned, you're in the middle of a task, and you want to call back later.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich arbeite gerade.**

**Explanation:** The bank drops out entirely — it belongs to the life-update sentence. What you need now is the one that says "this is a bad moment," and *Ich arbeite gerade, ich rufe zurück* is how that usually comes out.

</details>

25. **Produce it:** Someone asks *Wo ist Anna?* She's on the phone at this moment.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Sie telefoniert gerade.**

**Explanation:** *Sie ist gerade am Telefon* works equally well. Either way the *gerade* is what stops it sounding like a general statement about Anna's phone habits.

</details>
