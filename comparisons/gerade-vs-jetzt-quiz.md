## Self-Check


1. Someone calls at a bad moment. Which is better?\
   a. Ich esse jetzt.\
   b. Ich esse gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich esse gerade* ("I'm in the middle of eating").

</details>

2. **Er ist gerade in Berlin.** What's implied?\
   a. He's moved there.\
   b. He's there temporarily.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** temporarily; he's there at the moment.

</details>

3. Which word marks a change ("from now on")?\
   a. jetzt\
   b. gerade

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *jetzt*.

</details>

4. Why is *Ich bin arbeiten* wrong?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** German has no continuous tense. The present tense alone covers "I am doing," and *gerade* adds "right now": **Ich arbeite gerade.**

**Explanation:** *gerade* focuses tightly on the immediate moment; *jetzt* can refer more broadly to “now” as the current period or situation.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** Tell a friend you're cooking right now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich koche gerade.**

**Explanation:** *gerade* focuses tightly on the immediate moment; *jetzt* can refer more broadly to “now” as the current period or situation.

</details>
