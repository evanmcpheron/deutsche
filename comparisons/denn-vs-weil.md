# denn vs. weil

**Compares:** [denn](../particles/denn.md) · *weil*
**Level:** A1–A2 · **Tier 2**

[← zu Hause vs. nach Hause](zu-hause-vs-nach-hause.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## The Simple Explanation

Both mean "because." The difference is **what happens to the verb**.

> **denn** — the word order stays normal. Verb in second position.
> **weil** — the verb moves to the **end** of the clause.

Meaning-wise they're close enough that at A1 you can use whichever you can produce correctly.

## Contrasting Examples

**Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde.**
**Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich müde bin.**
I'm staying home because I'm tired.

Look at the second half of each: *denn ich **bin** müde* keeps the verb in the usual place. *weil ich müde **bin*** pushes it to the end.

---

**Wir gehen nicht, denn es regnet.**
**Wir gehen nicht, weil es regnet.**
We're not going because it's raining.

*(With a one-word verb phrase like this, both look almost identical — the difference shows up as soon as the clause has more in it.)*

---

**Ich lerne Deutsch, denn ich wohne in Berlin.**
**Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Berlin wohne.**
I'm learning German because I live in Berlin.

## Rule of Thumb

- **denn** — easier for a beginner: nothing changes, just add a comma and carry on. But it can only join two full main clauses, and it can't start a sentence.
- **weil** — the one you'll need eventually, and the one German speakers use more in conversation. It can also start a sentence: *Weil ich müde bin, bleibe ich zu Hause.*

A practical strategy at A1: **use *denn* while your word order is still shaky, and start switching to *weil* as it settles.**

## Answering "Why?"

When someone asks *Warum?*, the natural answer uses *weil*:

**Warum kommst du nicht? — Weil ich keine Zeit habe.**
Why aren't you coming? — Because I don't have time.

*denn* cannot do this — it can't begin a sentence.

## Common Mistakes

- **Sending the verb to the end after *denn*.** *…, denn ich müde bin* is wrong. That's *weil* word order.
- **Keeping normal order after *weil*.** *…, weil ich bin müde* is the classic learner error. (You'll hear native speakers do it in casual speech, but it's not what you want to learn.)
- **Starting a sentence with *denn* to mean "because."** Not possible. Use *weil*.
- **Confusing this *denn* with the question particle.** *Was ist denn das?* has nothing to do with reasons — see [denn](../particles/denn.md).

## Self-Check

1. Which is correct?\
   a. Ich bleibe hier, weil ich bin müde.\
   b. Ich bleibe hier, weil ich müde bin.

2. Which is correct?\
   a. Ich bleibe hier, denn ich bin müde.\
   b. Ich bleibe hier, denn ich müde bin.

3. Someone asks *Warum lernst du Deutsch?* Can you start your answer with *denn*?

4. What happens to the verb after *weil*?

5. **Produce it:** Say "I'm staying home because I'm tired" — using *weil*.

6. **Produce it:** Say the same thing using *denn*.
