# Quiz: bei vs. mit

[← Back to **bei vs. mit**](bei-vs-mit.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

Nothing here tells you which of the two words is being tested. Ask yourself whether the sentence names a companion or a place.

---

## Part A — Companion, or Place?

1. You've moved back in with your parents while you save up for a flat.\
   *Ich wohne im Moment ___ Eltern.*\
   a. mit meinen\
   b. bei meine\
   c. bei meinen\
   d. mit meiner

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** It's their place and you're the one staying there → *bei*, with the dative plural *meinen Eltern*. Option a would describe a flatshare between equals — and with *wohnen* German would want *zusammen* in that sentence too.

</details>

2. Your commute is forty minutes on the train each way.\
   *Ich fahre jeden Tag ___ Zug zur Arbeit.*\
   a. mit dem\
   b. beim\
   c. bei dem\
   d. mit den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A means of transport takes *mit* plus the dative. Options b and c are the same form written two ways, and either would put you standing next to a train rather than sitting in one.

</details>

3. You and Anna share an office and work on the same projects.\
   *Ich arbeite ___ Anna.*\
   a. bei\
   b. mit\
   c. beim\
   d. bei der

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A colleague is someone you work alongside → *mit*. Option a would promote Anna to your employer, which is a slightly awkward thing to say by accident.

</details>

4. You've been at the same electronics company for six years.\
   *Ich arbeite ___ Siemens.*\
   a. bei\
   b. mit\
   c. beim\
   d. mit dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** An employer takes *bei*, and company names go without an article — so no contraction either. Option b would mean your firm has a project running with theirs.

</details>

5. You're meeting your brother and the two of you are going to the cinema.\
   *Ich gehe ___ Bruder ins Kino.*\
   a. bei meinem\
   b. beim\
   c. mit meinem\
   d. bei meinen

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Accompaniment → *mit* plus dative. Option a is the error the lesson warns about: *bei* names a place, so it can't be pressed into service to mean "along with".

</details>

6. A friend asks where you're sleeping in Cologne. Your cousin has a spare room.\
   *Ich schlafe ___ Cousine.* (die Cousine = cousin)\
   a. mit meiner\
   b. bei meine\
   c. beim\
   d. bei meiner

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** At someone's place → *bei* plus dative *meiner Cousine*. Option a says something rather different, and it's a good illustration of why these two aren't interchangeable versions of "with".

</details>

7. You're on the phone telling a friend roughly where you are: a few metres from the station entrance.\
   *Ich bin gerade ___ Bahnhof.*\
   a. mit dem\
   b. bei\
   c. beim\
   d. bei den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** This is *bei* in its "near" sense, and *bei dem* → *beim*. The preposition needs its dative article, which is why bare b can't work in front of a noun like this.

</details>

8. You never use a card at the bakery.\
   *Ich bezahle ___ Bargeld.* (das Bargeld = cash)\
   a. mit\
   b. bei\
   c. beim\
   d. bei dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The thing you use to do something → *mit*, and *Bargeld* needs no article here. Option c is what you'd want for the place: *beim Bäcker*.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

9. **Ich wohne mit meiner Schwester zusammen** and **Ich wohne bei meiner Schwester.** What is the difference?\
   a. There is no difference.\
   b. The second sentence is incorrect.\
   c. In the first sentence, she lives somewhere else.\
   d. In the first, we share the home; in the second, it is her home and I am staying with her.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Both are correct German and both put two sisters under one roof — but one of them makes you a flatmate and the other makes you a guest. This is the question that comes up constantly in real conversations, and the preposition answers it.

</details>

10. A learner means *I'm going to the cinema with my friend* and writes **Ich gehe bei meinem Freund ins Kino.**\
   a. Correct.\
   b. Incorrect — *bei* cannot mean “together with.”\
   c. Correct, but impolite.\
   d. Correct German with a different meaning.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Unlike most of the mix-ups in this repository, this one doesn't produce a different sentence — it produces no sentence at all. *bei* has no companion meaning to fall back on, so the phrase simply can't attach to *gehen*.

</details>

11. **Wir arbeiten mit Siemens.** What does that sentence say? (der Arbeitgeber = employer)\
   a. Siemens is our employer.\
   b. We are looking for work at Siemens.\
   c. Our company and Siemens are working on a project together.\
   d. We work near Siemens.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *mit* makes Siemens a partner. The employer sentence is *Wir arbeiten bei Siemens* — one preposition apart, and a very different thing to write on a form.

</details>

12. Which preposition do you need for **going** to somebody's place?\
   a. zu\
   b. bei\
   c. mit\
   d. beim

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *bei* is static and has no movement version of itself. The journey is *Ich gehe zu meiner Schwester*; once you're through the door it becomes *Ich bin bei meiner Schwester*.

</details>

13. Which sentence is about **how** somebody travels?\
   a. Ich bin beim Auto.\
   b. Ich fahre mit dem Auto.\
   c. Ich wohne bei Anna.\
   d. Ich gehe mit Anna.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *mit* covers both companions and means, and here it's the means. Option d is the very same word doing the companion job — so the word alone never tells you which idea is in play; the rest of the sentence does.

</details>

14. English "by" turns up in all of these. In which one is *mit* the right German word?\
   a. by the station\
   b. written by my teacher\
   c. by my sister's place\
   d. by bus

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Transport → *mit dem Bus*. The others go three separate ways: *beim Bahnhof*, *von meiner Lehrerin*, *bei meiner Schwester*. Translating "by" mechanically is how learners end up with all four of these wrong.

</details>

15. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does the gap need *bei*? (das Rad = bike)\
   a. Ich wohne ___ meinen Großeltern.\
   b. Ich fahre ___ dem Rad zur Arbeit.\
   c. Sie arbeitet ___ einer Schule.\
   d. Wir treffen uns ___ mir.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c, d**

**Explanation:** Someone's home, an employer, and "at my place" — three flavours of the same idea, a location connected to a person. Only b is a means of transport, and those always take *mit*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

16. Complete the dialogue. B is visiting her parents and staying the night.

```text
A: Wo schläfst du am Wochenende?
B: ___ meinen Eltern.
```

   a. Mit\
   b. Beim\
   c. Zu\
   d. Bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** A's *Wo?* asks for a place, and somebody's home is *bei*. Option c would answer *Wohin?* instead — the journey there rather than the night itself.

</details>

17. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Wie kommst du zum Konzert?
B: Ich fahre ___ Bus.
```

   a. mit dem\
   b. bei dem\
   c. beim\
   d. mit

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Wie?* asks about the means → *mit* plus dative. *mit dem Bus, mit dem Zug, mit dem Auto* are worth learning as whole phrases; there's no contraction to remember, because *mit* never has one.

</details>

18. Complete the dialogue. B can't talk for long.

```text
A: Hast du kurz Zeit?
B: Nicht wirklich, ich bin gerade ___ Arzt.
```

   a. mit dem\
   b. beim\
   c. bei\
   d. zum

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** She's in his rooms, so *bei dem* → *beim*. Option d is what she'd have said half an hour earlier on the way there — the same appointment, but a preposition for the journey rather than the arrival.

</details>

19. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Gehst du allein ins Kino?
B: Nein, ich gehe ___ Kollegin. (die Kollegin = colleague)
```

   a. mit meiner\
   b. bei meiner\
   c. beim\
   d. bei einer

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A's *allein* sets up the companion reading, and companions take *mit*. Option b would answer a question about where B is, which isn't what was asked.

</details>

20. A friend texts **Ich bin bei Tom.** Where is she?\
   a. Sie geht gerade zu Tom.\
   b. Sie ist in Toms Wohnung.\
   c. Sie kommt mit Tom.\
   d. Sie wohnt in Toms Straße.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *bei* plus a person means at that person's place. Option c is the *mit* reading — travelling in his company, which tells you nothing at all about where either of them is.

</details>

21. **Ich arbeite bei meinem Bruder** and **Ich arbeite mit meinem Bruder.** Which one belongs to somebody whose brother pays their wages?\
   a. Both.\
   b. Neither.\
   c. The second.\
   d. The first.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *bei* points at the employer; *mit* only says the two of you are on the job together. Families run both arrangements, and the preposition is the one thing distinguishing them.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

22. Which sentence does **not** work in German?\
   a. Ich fahre mit dem Fahrrad.\
   b. Ich bin bei meiner Tante.\
   c. Ich gehe bei meiner Tante.\
   d. Ich gehe mit meiner Tante.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *bei* can't carry movement. Use *zu* for the trip and keep *bei* for after you've arrived. Option d is perfectly good and means your aunt is coming along with you.

</details>

23. Fill in the missing words. No options given.

   You're describing your commute: twenty minutes on the underground.

```text
Ich fahre ___ U-Bahn. (die U-Bahn = underground)
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **mit der**

**Explanation:** Means of transport → *mit* plus dative, and *die U-Bahn* is feminine. Both words stay separate: *mit* forms no contractions at all, so there's nothing to shorten.

</details>

24. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You're spending three weeks at your grandparents' house this summer.

```text
Im Sommer bin ich drei Wochen ___ meinen Großeltern.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **bei**

**Explanation:** Their house, you as the guest. *mit meinen Großeltern* would say the three of you are going somewhere together and leave the location completely open.

</details>

25. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A form asks who you work for. You've been at the post office for years.

```text
Ich arbeite ___ der Post.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **bei**

**Explanation:** Employers take *bei*. Note that *bei der* stays in two words — the contraction *beim* only exists for the masculine and neuter *bei dem*.

</details>

26. Put the words in order.

```text
meinem Freund / ich / mit / ins Konzert / gehe
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich gehe mit meinem Freund ins Konzert.**

**Explanation:** Verb second, companion before destination. Swapping in *bei* wouldn't change the meaning so much as break the sentence, since *bei* has no way of attaching a companion to a verb of motion.

</details>

27. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich bin bei meiner Schwester.**\
   New situation: you've just put your coat on and you're setting off for her flat now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich gehe zu meiner Schwester.**

**Explanation:** *Ich fahre zu meiner Schwester* is just as good if it's far enough to drive. What matters is that neither *bei* nor *mit* can be pressed into service here: the journey needs a third preposition entirely, and forgetting that is what produces *Ich gehe bei meiner Schwester*.

</details>
