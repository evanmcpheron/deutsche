# The A1 Priority System

[Category Index](README.md) | [Next → Active vs. Passive Vocabulary](active-vs-passive-vocabulary.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Not all small words deserve the same effort. This page sorts them into three tiers, so you know what to drill and what to simply let wash over you.

## How This Ranking Was Made

The tiers below are cross-checked against standard A1 references rather than resting on impression alone:

- **The Goethe-Institut's *Start Deutsch 1* (A1) word list.** The official vocabulary inventory for the A1 exam: roughly 650 words, of which about half are expected to be active vocabulary at this level. Words appearing there as core entries are treated here as Tier 1 or 2.
- **The sequence used by widely-taught A1 courses** — Deutsche Welle's *Nicos Weg* (A1), and standard textbook series such as *Menschen A1* and *Netzwerk A1*. What these introduce in their first units is heavily weighted toward Tier 1 here.

**Where a standard reference and this repository's instincts disagreed, the reference won.** Two examples of that in practice:

- *eigentlich*, *eben*, and *halt* feel useful and are extremely common in real speech, but they aren't core A1 production targets in the standard A1 inventories — so they sit in Tier 2 and 3 rather than Tier 1, despite how often you'll hear them.
- *für*, *um*, and *am* are unglamorous and easy to overlook when you're focused on tricky particles, but they're everywhere in beginner course material, so they're Tier 1.

The tiers are still a judgement call at the edges — but they're an informed one, not a guess.

## Tier 1 — Learn Actively Now

Extremely common, essential, and safe to produce. Aim to use these yourself.

**Negation**
[nicht](../words/nicht.md) · [kein](../words/kein.md)

**Core adverbs**
[auch](../words/auch.md) · [noch](../words/noch.md) · [schon](../words/schon.md) · [nur](../words/nur.md) · [sehr](../words/sehr.md) · [immer](../words/immer.md) · [wieder](../words/wieder.md) · [vielleicht](../words/vielleicht.md)

**Prepositions**
[mit](../prepositions/mit.md) · [zu](../prepositions/zu.md) · [nach](../prepositions/nach.md) · [bei](../prepositions/bei.md) · [aus](../prepositions/aus.md) · [von](../prepositions/von.md) · [für](../prepositions/fuer.md) · [um](../prepositions/um.md) · [in](../prepositions/in.md) · [auf](../prepositions/auf.md)

**Chunks**
[noch nicht](../patterns/noch-nicht.md) · [noch ein …](../patterns/noch-ein.md) · [nicht mehr](../patterns/nicht-mehr.md) · [immer noch](../patterns/immer-noch.md) · [von … bis …](../patterns/von-bis.md) · [ein bisschen](../patterns/ein-bisschen.md) · **zu Hause / nach Hause**

*One caveat inside Tier 1:* [in](../prepositions/in.md) and [auf](../prepositions/auf.md) are Tier 1 by frequency, but their **case** behaviour is a slow habit rather than a fact. Use them from day one; expect the dative/accusative choice to stay wobbly for weeks.

## Tier 2 — Recognition Plus Gradual Production

Common enough that you'll meet them constantly, but either subtler or less urgent. Understand them now; start producing them when they feel natural.

[erst](../words/erst.md) · [gerade](../words/gerade.md) · [an](../prepositions/an.md) · [mal](../particles/mal.md) · [denn](../particles/denn.md) · [doch](../particles/doch.md) · [eigentlich](../particles/eigentlich.md) · [schon mal](../patterns/schon-mal.md) · [gar nicht](../patterns/gar-nicht.md)

Two exceptions worth pulling forward into active use early:

- ***doch* as the contradicting "yes"** — a discrete rule, immediately useful, no subtlety required. See [doch](../particles/doch.md).
- ***erst* in opening hours and timetables** — you don't need to produce it, but misreading *erst um zehn* can make you miss a train.

## Tier 3 — Recognition First

You will hear these constantly in real German. You do not need to produce them at A1, and trying to usually backfires.

[eben](../particles/eben.md) · [halt](../particles/halt.md) · [ja](../particles/ja.md) *(as a particle)* · **wohl** *(a guess: Er ist wohl krank — he's probably ill)* · **schon** *(as reassurance: Das wird schon)* · **überhaupt** *(at all)*

The reason for the demotion isn't difficulty — it's **risk**. Used slightly wrong, a resignation or shared-knowledge particle can make you sound dismissive or presumptuous, in a way that a mis-chosen preposition never does.

## What "Beyond A1" Looks Like Here

A few things appear in this repository so you can recognize them, clearly marked as beyond what you need to master now:

- The **genitive** case (*das Auto meines Bruders*) — use *von* instead
- *nicht nur … sondern auch …* and *sowohl … als auch …*
- *immer* + comparative (*immer kälter*)
- The remaining two-way prepositions: *über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen*
- *nach und nach*, *von Anfang an*

If a page marks something as A2 or "recognize only," you can safely skim it.
