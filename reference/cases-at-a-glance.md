# Cases at a Glance

[← Preposition Overview](preposition-overview.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [Next → Modal Particles Overview](modal-particles-overview.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

The minimum you need about cases to use the prepositions in this repository. This is not a grammar course — it's a lookup page.

## What a Case Is

German nouns don't change much, but the **articles in front of them do**, depending on the noun's job in the sentence. The three you need at A1:

| Case | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Nominative** | the subject — who's doing it | **Der Mann** ist hier. |
| **Accusative** | the direct object — what's affected | Ich sehe **den Mann**. |
| **Dative** | the indirect object, and after certain prepositions | Ich helfe **dem Mann**. |

*(There's a fourth, the genitive. Skip it for now — everyday German uses **von** instead.)*

## The Article Tables

### Definite (*der/die/das*)

| | masc. | fem. | neut. | plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nom.** | der | die | das | die |
| **Acc.** | **den** | die | das | die |
| **Dat.** | **dem** | **der** | **dem** | **den** (+n on the noun) |

### Indefinite (*ein/eine*) and *kein*

| | masc. | fem. | neut. | plural (kein only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Nom.** | ein | eine | ein | keine |
| **Acc.** | **einen** | eine | ein | keine |
| **Dat.** | **einem** | **einer** | **einem** | **keinen** |

### Pronouns

| Nom. | Acc. | Dat. |
|---|---|---|
| ich | mich | **mir** |
| du | dich | **dir** |
| er | ihn | **ihm** |
| sie | sie | **ihr** |
| es | es | **ihm** |
| wir | uns | uns |
| ihr | euch | euch |
| sie / Sie | sie / Sie | **ihnen / Ihnen** |

The pair to internalize first: **mir/dir** (dative, after *mit, bei, von, zu*) vs. **mich/dich** (accusative, after *für, um*). *für mich*, *mit mir* — those two phrases carry most of the load at A1.

## Prepositions and Their Cases

| Always dative | Always accusative | Two-way |
|---|---|---|
| mit, zu, nach, bei, aus, von, seit | für, um, durch, ohne, gegen | in, auf, an, über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen |

## The Location vs. Movement Rule

This is the one pattern worth understanding rather than memorizing, because it governs the most common prepositions in the language.

**Two-way prepositions** — *in, auf, an*, and their less frequent relatives — take a different case depending on what the sentence describes:

> **Location — answers *Wo?* ("where is it?") — DATIVE.**
> The thing is already there, at rest. Nothing is travelling.

> **Movement toward a destination — answers *Wohin?* ("where is it going?") — ACCUSATIVE.**
> Something is moving into or onto the place.

### Worked Examples

| Wo? → dative | Wohin? → accusative |
|---|---|
| Das Buch liegt auf **dem** Tisch. | Ich lege das Buch auf **den** Tisch. |
| Die Tasse steht auf **dem** Regal. | Stell die Tasse auf **das** Regal. |
| Ich bin **im** Kino. | Ich gehe **ins** Kino. |
| Wir sind **in der** Stadt. | Wir fahren **in die** Stadt. |
| Das Bild hängt **an der** Wand. | Ich hänge das Bild **an die** Wand. |
| Das Kind ist **im** Bett. | Das Kind geht **ins** Bett. |

### Three Shortcuts

1. **The contraction tells you.** *im, am, beim, vom* are dative → location. *ins, ans* are accusative → movement.
2. **The verb tells you.** *liegen, stehen, sitzen, sein, bleiben* → location, dative. *legen, stellen, setzen, gehen, fahren* → movement, accusative.
3. **Ask the question out loud.** *Wo?* or *Wohin?* If you can't tell, picture whether anything is travelling.

### What This Does *Not* Apply To

Fixed-case prepositions never switch, no matter what the sentence means:

**Ich fahre mit dem Zug.** *(movement in the sentence — but *mit* is still dative)*
**Ich gehe zu meiner Mutter.** *(movement — *zu* is still dative)*

So don't extend the location/movement logic to *mit, zu, nach, bei, aus, von*. It applies **only** to the two-way group.

## Time Expressions With These Prepositions Are Dative

**in einer Stunde**, **am Montag**, **im Sommer**, **vor einem Jahr** — nothing is moving, so nothing is accusative.

That covers time expressions built on a two-way preposition. Time expressions with **no preposition at all** are a separate case, and they're usually **accusative**: *jeden Tag* (every day), *nächste Woche* (next week), *letzten Montag* (last Monday), *diesen Sommer* (this summer). You'll meet *jeden Tag* early, so it's worth knowing the two groups behave differently rather than expecting one rule to cover both.

## What You Can Safely Postpone

- The genitive case
- Adjective endings (*ein guter Mann* vs. *der gute Mann*)
- The dative plural *-n* on nouns (*mit den Kindern*)
- *über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen* — same rule, lower frequency
