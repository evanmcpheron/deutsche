Answer each question before opening its solution. The six core words are deliberately mixed together. Focus on the **situation or relationship** the German expresses rather than translating one word at a time.

---

## Part A — Read the Situation

**1.** The café closes at 18:00. It is 17:50, and customers can still order. Which sentence fits?

   a. Das Café ist schon zu. \
   b. Das Café ist noch offen. \
   c. Das Café ist schon offen. \
   d. Das Café ist nicht offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *noch offen* describes a state that is continuing: the café has not closed yet. *schon zu* would mean it has closed already.

</details>

**2.** A meeting starts at 8:00. Paul arrives at 7:45, earlier than you expected. Which sentence fits best?

   a. Paul ist nicht da. \
   b. Paul ist noch da. \
   c. Nur Paul ist da. \
   d. Paul ist schon da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *schon da* presents his arrival as completed by now, with the sense that it happened earlier than expected. *noch da* would mean he has not left.

</details>

**3.** You have to leave immediately and cannot stay to talk. Which sentence naturally describes your situation?

   a. Ich habe keine Zeit. \
   b. Ich habe die Zeit nicht. \
   c. Ich habe nur eine Frage. \
   d. Ich habe schon Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is a noun used here without a definite article, so German negates it with *kein*: *keine Zeit*. *nicht Zeit* is the common A1 error.

</details>

**4.** Anna says, *Ich fahre morgen nach Berlin.* Her sister is going there tomorrow as well. Which sentence fits the sister?

   a. Ich fahre auch nach Berlin. \
   b. Ich fahre noch nach Berlin. \
   c. Ich fahre nur nach Berlin. \
   d. Ich fahre nicht nach Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds another person to something already true: Anna is going, and her sister is going too. *noch* would not express “me too” here.

</details>

**5.** A ticket office had many tickets this morning. Now exactly three remain. Which sentence fits best?

   a. Wir haben schon drei Karten. \
   b. Wir haben auch drei Karten. \
   c. Wir haben keine drei Karten. \
   d. Wir haben nur noch drei Karten.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *nur noch drei Karten* means three tickets remain and that is all. Plain *noch drei Karten* would say three are left without the same “only” restriction.

</details>

**6.** You taste the soup. It is cold, although you expected it to be warm. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Die Suppe ist schon warm. \
   b. Die Suppe ist nur warm. \
   c. Die Suppe ist nicht warm. \
   d. Die Suppe ist auch warm.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *warm* is an adjective, so it is negated with *nicht*: *nicht warm*. *kein* negates nouns, not adjectives.

</details>

**7.** You are doing an exercise. Your friend asks whether you are finished, but you still need a few minutes. Which answer fits?

   a. Ich bin schon fertig. \
   b. Ich bin nicht fertig. \
   c. Ich bin auch nicht fertig. \
   d. Ich bin noch nicht fertig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* marks something as still pending but expected: you are not finished yet. *schon fertig* would mean the opposite state has already been reached.

</details>

**8.** You have finished one coffee and want a second one. What is the natural request?

   a. Noch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   b. Auch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   c. Nur einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   d. Keinen Kaffee, bitte.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch einen Kaffee* adds one more of the same thing for the same person. *auch einen Kaffee* normally adds you to someone else who is having coffee.

</details>

**9.** You expected a museum ticket to be expensive, but it costs five euros. Which sentence best emphasizes the small amount?

   a. Das kostet schon fünf Euro. \
   b. Das kostet auch fünf Euro. \
   c. Das kostet noch fünf Euro. \
   d. Das kostet nur fünf Euro.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *nur fünf Euro* limits the price to five euros: no more than that. The other small words express different relationships, not simple restriction.

</details>

**10.** You are looking for a hotel in a very small village. There is no hotel there. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Hier gibt es das Hotel nicht. \
   b. Hier gibt es kein Hotel. \
   c. Hier gibt es nur ein Hotel. \
   d. Hier gibt es schon ein Hotel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Hotel* is the noun being negated, so *kein Hotel* is the natural form. *nicht* is not used this way with an indefinite noun.

</details>

## Part B — Dialogue, Contrast, and Error Diagnosis

**11.** Complete the dialogue.

A: Ich bin müde.\
B: ___

   a. Ich noch. \
   b. Ich auch. \
   c. Ich kein. \
   d. Ich schon.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch* adds the second speaker to the same positive statement: “me too.” *Ich noch* does not have that function.

</details>

**12.** Complete the dialogue.

A: Ich habe keinen Hunger.\
B: ___

   a. Ich schon nicht. \
   b. Ich noch nicht. \
   c. Ich auch nicht. \
   d. Ich nur nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch nicht* means the negative statement is true for the second speaker too: “me neither.” The order is *auch nicht*.

</details>

**13.** Anna orders a coffee. Paul has not ordered anything yet, but he wants the same drink as Anna. What should Paul say?

   a. Ich möchte keinen Kaffee. \
   b. Ich möchte nur einen Kaffee. \
   c. Ich möchte noch einen Kaffee. \
   d. Ich möchte auch einen Kaffee.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Paul is joining Anna in having coffee, so *auch* fits. *noch einen Kaffee* would normally mean Paul himself is adding another coffee to one he already had.

</details>

**14.** Ten minutes later, Paul has finished that coffee and asks the server for a second one. What should he say now?

   a. Auch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   b. Noch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   c. Keinen Kaffee, bitte. \
   d. Nicht einen Kaffee, bitte.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The same person wants one more of the same item, so *noch einen Kaffee* is the natural choice. This is the contrast with Question 13.

</details>

**15.** It is almost lunchtime. You want to ask Anna whether she has eaten by now. Complete the question: **Hast du ___ gegessen?**

   a. noch \
   b. schon \
   c. kein \
   d. nur

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** In a question, *schon* asks whether the expected event has happened by now: *Hast du schon gegessen?* English often uses “yet” here, but German keeps *schon*.

</details>

**16.** Max starts the same exercise at 9:00. At 9:20 he is still working. His teacher asks, *Bist du fertig?* Max answers:

   a. Noch nicht. \
   b. Schon. \
   c. Auch. \
   d. Kein.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Noch nicht* is a complete answer meaning the expected result has not happened yet. It contrasts directly with *schon*.

</details>

**17.** Everyone else in your family is staying home. You are the only person going to Berlin. Which sentence expresses that?

   a. Ich fahre auch nach Berlin. \
   b. Ich fahre noch nach Berlin. \
   c. Nur ich fahre nach Berlin. \
   d. Ich fahre nicht nach Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Nur ich* limits the people who are going: you and nobody else. Position matters; *nur* limits what follows it.

</details>

**18.** You drink water, but you do not drink coffee, tea, or juice. Which sentence fits best?

   a. Ich trinke nur Wasser. \
   b. Nur ich trinke Wasser. \
   c. Ich trinke auch Wasser. \
   d. Ich trinke noch Wasser.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *nur Wasser* limits what you drink to water. *Nur ich trinke Wasser* instead limits the person: it says you are the only one who drinks water.

</details>

**19.** The intended idea is: “I have no money.” Judge the sentence **Ich habe nicht Geld.**

   a. Correct and natural. \
   b. Correct, but it says you have only a little money. \
   c. Incorrect here; use **Ich habe kein Geld.** \
   d. Incorrect here; use **Ich habe schon Geld.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The noun *Geld* is being negated and has no definite article, so German uses *kein*: *Ich habe kein Geld.* This is a core *nicht* versus *kein* distinction.

</details>

**20.** The intended idea is: “The hotel is not expensive.” Judge the sentence **Das Hotel ist kein teuer.**

   a. Correct and natural. \
   b. Incorrect here; use **Das Hotel ist nicht teuer.** \
   c. Correct, but it means the hotel is only expensive. \
   d. Incorrect here; use **Das Hotel ist keine teuer.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *teuer* is an adjective, not a noun, so it takes *nicht*: *Das Hotel ist nicht teuer.* Changing the ending on *kein* does not fix the problem.

</details>

## Part C — Apply the Distinctions

**21.** Compare:

**Ich bin hier.**\
**Ich bin noch hier.**

What does *noch* add?

   a. It says the speaker has just arrived. \
   b. It says the speaker is the only person there. \
   c. It makes the sentence negative. \
   d. It says the situation continues: the speaker has not left yet.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Both sentences say the speaker is here. *noch* adds the relationship to an expected endpoint: leaving has not happened yet.

</details>

**22.** Compare:

**Ich habe eine Frage.**\
**Ich habe nur eine Frage.**

What does *nur* change?

   a. It limits the request to one question and can make it sound smaller. \
   b. It means the question has already been answered. \
   c. It means another person has a question too. \
   d. It negates the word *Frage*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *nur* places a boundary around *eine Frage*: one question and no more. The basic fact “I have a question” remains true.

</details>

**23.** A hostel already offers breakfast. You learn that it offers dinner as well. Which sentence fits?

   a. Im Hostel gibt es noch Abendessen. \
   b. Im Hostel gibt es kein Abendessen. \
   c. Im Hostel gibt es auch Abendessen. \
   d. Im Hostel gibt es nur Abendessen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds dinner to another thing already offered. *nur Abendessen* would mean dinner is the only thing offered.

</details>

**24.** You want to say that you have no hunger. Which sentence has the correct form?

   a. Ich habe nicht Hunger. \
   b. Ich habe kein Hunger. \
   c. Ich habe keinen Hunger. \
   d. Ich habe keine Hunger.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Hunger* is masculine and is the object of *haben*, so the form is *keinen Hunger*. The noun-negation choice is *kein*, with the required accusative *-en* ending.

</details>

**25. Select all that apply.** Which sentences are natural German for the situation they express?

   a. Das ist kein gut. \
   b. Ich habe keine Zeit. \
   c. Ich bin kein müde. \
   d. Das Wetter ist nicht schön.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b, d**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is a noun, so *keine Zeit* is correct. *schön* is an adjective, so *nicht schön* is correct. *gut* and *müde* are adjectives and cannot be negated with *kein*.

</details>

**26.** A shop opens at 8:00. It is 7:55 and the door is still locked. Which sentence best expresses that the expected opening has not happened yet?

   a. Der Laden ist schon offen. \
   b. Der Laden ist noch nicht offen. \
   c. Der Laden ist noch offen. \
   d. Der Laden ist nur heute offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Opening is expected but has not happened yet, so *noch nicht offen* fits. *schon offen* would say the expected state has already begun.

</details>

**27.** You return at 7:58 and are surprised that the shop has opened before 8:00. Which sentence best expresses that earlier-than-expected opening?

   a. Der Laden ist noch offen. \
   b. Der Laden ist nicht offen. \
   c. Der Laden ist nur offen. \
   d. Der Laden ist schon offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *schon offen* presents the opening as already true by this point, with the expectation that it might have happened later. *noch offen* would instead focus on not having closed yet.

</details>

**28.** Which sentence does **not** use *nur* to limit an amount, item, person, or duration?

   a. Wir bleiben nur zwei Tage. \
   b. Ich habe nur eine Frage. \
   c. Wir haben noch zwei Tage. \
   d. Das kostet nur fünf Euro.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Wir haben noch zwei Tage* says two days remain; it does not use *nur* at all. The other three sentences each place a limit around what follows *nur*.

</details>

**29.** What situation best matches **Wir haben nur noch zwei Minuten**?

   a. Two minutes remain, and that is all the time left. \
   b. We have already waited for two minutes. \
   c. We need two additional minutes after the current time ends. \
   d. We have no time at all.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch zwei Minuten* says two minutes remain; adding *nur* makes that remaining amount feel limited: only two minutes are left.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

For these final questions, write the German before opening the solution. Natural alternatives are acceptable when they express the same target meaning correctly.

**30. Sentence reconstruction.** Put these pieces into a natural sentence:

`heute / nicht / ich / kommen / kann`

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich kann heute nicht kommen.**

**Explanation:** With a second verb at the end, *nicht* comes before that final verb: *nicht kommen*.

</details>

**31. Error correction.** Correct this sentence without changing its intended meaning:

**Ich habe nicht Zeit.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe keine Zeit.**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is the noun being negated, so use the negative article *kein*: feminine *keine Zeit*.

</details>

**32. Unsupported cloze.** Complete the reply with one Stage 1 word.

A: Ich komme morgen mit.\
B: Ich ___!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **auch**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch!* adds the second speaker to the same positive statement: “me too.”

</details>

**33. Changed situation.** Start with:

**Ich bin noch nicht fertig.**

Now the situation changes: you finish earlier than expected. Change only what is necessary to express the new situation.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin schon fertig.**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* describes a result that is still pending. Once the result has arrived — especially earlier than expected — *schon* expresses the new relationship.

</details>

**34. Controlled production.** You are about to leave, and only two minutes remain. Write one natural sentence using **nur noch**.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural answer is **Wir haben nur noch zwei Minuten.**

**Explanation:** *noch zwei Minuten* says two minutes remain; *nur* limits that remaining amount to two.

</details>
