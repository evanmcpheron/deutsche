This review deliberately interleaves concepts. The question does not tell you which word or rule is being tested. Most examples are new, and later questions require more retrieval with fewer cues.

## Part A — Mixed Meaning and Timeline

**1.** The bakery closes at 18:00. It is 17:55 and the door is open. Which sentence fits?

   a. Die Bäckerei ist schon zu. \
   b. Die Bäckerei ist wieder offen. \
   c. Die Bäckerei ist nicht mehr offen. \
   d. Die Bäckerei ist noch offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *noch offen* describes a state that continues up to now: the bakery has not closed yet.

</details>

**2.** You expected Anna at 9:00. She arrives at 8:40. Which sentence best captures your surprise?

   a. Anna ist schon da. \
   b. Anna ist noch da. \
   c. Anna ist wieder da. \
   d. Anna ist nicht mehr da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *schon da* says the expected arrival has already happened by now, earlier than expected.

</details>

**3.** A train does not arrive until 11:00, and you wish it came earlier. Which sentence fits?

   a. Der Zug kommt schon um elf. \
   b. Der Zug kommt erst um elf. \
   c. Der Zug kommt wieder um elf. \
   d. Der Zug kommt noch um elf.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *erst um elf* means “not until eleven” and frames the time as later than desired or expected.

</details>

**4.** Paul was sick, recovered, and becomes sick again two weeks later. Which sentence fits?

   a. Paul ist wieder krank. \
   b. Paul ist noch krank. \
   c. Paul ist schon krank. \
   d. Paul ist nicht mehr krank.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A state that stopped and later returned takes *wieder*. *noch* would mean the illness never stopped.

</details>

**5.** You are doing an exercise. Your teacher asks whether you are finished, but you still need five minutes. Which reply is best?

   a. Schon. \
   b. Nicht mehr. \
   c. Noch nicht. \
   d. Wieder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* marks an expected result that has not happened yet.

</details>

**6.** You used to work at a hotel, but you left that job last year. Which sentence fits now?

   a. Ich arbeite wieder im Hotel. \
   b. Ich arbeite noch im Hotel. \
   c. Ich arbeite nicht mehr im Hotel. \
   d. Ich arbeite schon im Hotel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *nicht mehr* says a previously true state has ended: you no longer work there.

</details>

**7.** Anna orders tea. You want tea too, but you have not ordered anything yet. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich möchte auch einen Tee. \
   b. Ich möchte noch einen Tee. \
   c. Ich möchte nur einen Tee. \
   d. Ich möchte keinen Tee.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds you to another person's choice: Anna is having tea, and you are too. *noch einen Tee* would normally mean another tea for the same person.

</details>

**8.** You have finished one coffee and want a second. Which request is natural?

   a. Auch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   b. Noch einen Kaffee, bitte. \
   c. Kein Kaffee, bitte. \
   d. Nur Kaffee, bitte.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *noch einen Kaffee* means one more of the same item for the same person.

</details>

**9.** You have exactly three minutes before you must leave, and no more time than that. Which sentence best expresses the limited remaining amount?

   a. Wir haben schon drei Minuten. \
   b. Wir haben wieder drei Minuten. \
   c. Wir haben nur noch drei Minuten. \
   d. Wir haben gar nicht drei Minuten.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *nur noch drei Minuten* means three minutes remain and that is all. *noch* gives the remaining amount; *nur* limits it.

</details>

**10.** The restaurant is extremely expensive; you want to say it is “not cheap at all.” Which sentence is natural?

   a. Das Restaurant ist kein billig. \
   b. Das Restaurant ist noch nicht billig. \
   c. Das Restaurant ist nicht mehr billig. \
   d. Das Restaurant ist gar nicht billig.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *gar nicht* strengthens a negation: “not at all.” Because *billig* is an adjective, the negation is *nicht*, not *kein*.

</details>

**11.** You have a small amount of German ability and want to describe it modestly. Which sentence fits?

   a. Ich spreche sehr Deutsch. \
   b. Ich spreche ein bisschen Deutsch. \
   c. Ich spreche viel gut Deutsch. \
   d. Ich spreche erst Deutsch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *ein bisschen Deutsch* is the fixed A1 pattern for “a little German.” The form *ein bisschen* does not change.

</details>

**12.** You are on the phone while cooking and want to say you are in the middle of cooking right now. Which sentence fits best?

   a. Ich koche immer. \
   b. Ich koche gerade. \
   c. Ich koche wieder. \
   d. Ich koche erst.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *gerade* marks an action in progress at this exact moment. German does not need a separate continuous tense.

</details>

**13.** It rained at 8:00, 9:00, and 10:00 without stopping. At 10:00 you are annoyed that it continues. Which sentence fits best?

   a. Es regnet wieder. \
   b. Es regnet immer wieder. \
   c. Es regnet schon. \
   d. Es regnet immer noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* describes one continuous situation that is lasting longer than expected. *immer wieder* would describe separate repeated episodes.

</details>

**14.** You want to say the hotel is very good. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Das Hotel ist sehr gut. \
   b. Das Hotel ist viel gut. \
   c. Das Hotel ist sehr viel. \
   d. Das Hotel ist gut viel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *sehr* expresses intensity with adjectives: *sehr gut*. *viel* expresses quantity, not adjective intensity.

</details>

**15.** You want to say you have a lot of time. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich habe sehr Zeit. \
   b. Ich habe schon Zeit. \
   c. Ich habe nur Zeit. \
   d. Ich habe viel Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *viel* goes with a noun quantity: *viel Zeit*. *sehr* does not modify nouns this way.

</details>

**16.** You are not sure whether the museum is open and want to say “maybe.” Which word fits the uncertainty?

   a. immer \
   b. schon \
   c. vielleicht \
   d. wieder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *vielleicht* expresses possibility or uncertainty: perhaps/maybe.

</details>

## Part B — Prepositions, Case, and Travel

**17.** You are going to Berlin. Complete: **Ich fahre ___ Berlin.**

   a. zum \
   b. in die \
   c. bei \
   d. nach

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Named cities take *nach* for destination: *nach Berlin*.

</details>

**18.** You are on your way to the doctor. Complete: **Ich gehe ___ Arzt.**

   a. zum \
   b. beim \
   c. nach dem \
   d. aus dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A person/professional destination takes *zu* + dative: *zum Arzt*.

</details>

**19.** You are originally from Germany. Complete: **Ich komme ___ Deutschland.**

   a. von \
   b. nach \
   c. aus \
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Home-country origin takes *aus*: *aus Deutschland*. A current starting point often takes *von*.

</details>

**20.** You are travelling from the station to your hotel; the station is the starting point of this trip. Complete: **Ich komme ___ Bahnhof.**

   a. aus dem \
   b. bei dem \
   c. nach dem \
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The station is the starting point of this trip, so use *von dem* → *vom Bahnhof*.

</details>

**21.** You bought a present intended for your father. Complete: **Das Geschenk ist ___ Vater.**

   a. für meinen \
   b. von mein \
   c. bei meinen \
   d. mit meinem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The intended recipient takes *für* + accusative: *für meinen Vater*.

</details>

**22.** You are at your brother's apartment. Complete: **Ich bin ___ meinem Bruder.**

   a. mit \
   b. zu \
   c. von \
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Being at someone's place takes *bei* + dative. Going there would take *zu*.

</details>

**23.** You are going into the cinema. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich gehe im Kino. \
   b. Ich gehe nach Kino. \
   c. Ich gehe ins Kino. \
   d. Ich gehe beim Kino.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Movement into a building takes *in* + accusative: *ins Kino*.

</details>

**24.** You are already inside the cinema. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich bin ins Kino. \
   b. Ich bin nach Kino. \
   c. Ich bin im Kino. \
   d. Ich bin zum Kino.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Location inside the building takes *in* + dative: *im Kino*.

</details>

**25.** The keys are lying on the table. Complete: **Die Schlüssel liegen auf ___ Tisch.**

   a. den \
   b. dem \
   c. der \
   d. das

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** This is location (*Wo?*), so two-way *auf* takes dative: *auf dem Tisch*.

</details>

**26.** You put the keys onto the table. Complete: **Ich lege die Schlüssel auf ___ Tisch.**

   a. den \
   b. dem \
   c. der \
   d. das

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The keys move to a new position (*Wohin?*), so *auf* takes accusative: *auf den Tisch*.

</details>

**27.** You have an appointment on Tuesday at 9:00. Which phrase is correct?

   a. am Dienstag um neun \
   b. um Dienstag am neun \
   c. im Dienstag um neun \
   d. nach Dienstag bei neun

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Days take *am*; clock times take *um*: *am Dienstag um neun*.

</details>

**28.** The café is open Monday through Friday. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Das Café ist zu Montag bis Freitag offen. \
   b. Das Café ist aus Montag nach Freitag offen. \
   c. Das Café ist von Montag bis Freitag offen. \
   d. Das Café ist bei Montag für Freitag offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A span uses the fixed pattern *von … bis …*: *von Montag bis Freitag*.

</details>

## Part C — Negation, Tone, and Error Diagnosis

**29.** Intended meaning: “I have no money.” Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich habe nicht Geld. \
   b. Ich habe kein Geld. \
   c. Ich habe nicht kein Geld. \
   d. Ich habe keine Geld.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** An indefinite/uncountable noun is negated with *kein*: *kein Geld*. *nicht Geld* is the common learner error.

</details>

**30.** Intended meaning: “The room is not clean.” Which sentence is natural?

   a. Das Zimmer ist kein sauber. \
   b. Das Zimmer ist keine sauber. \
   c. Das Zimmer ist nicht sauber. \
   d. Das Zimmer ist nicht ein sauber.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *sauber* is an adjective, so it is negated with *nicht*: *nicht sauber*.

</details>

**31.** Someone says **Ich habe keine Zeit.** You also have no time. Which reply is natural?

   a. Ich noch nicht. \
   b. Ich auch nicht. \
   c. Ich nicht auch. \
   d. Ich kein.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch nicht* is the standard “me neither” pattern.

</details>

**32.** Someone asks **Hast du keinen Hunger?** You are hungry. Which one-word answer clearly contradicts the negative question?

   a. Doch. \
   b. Ja. \
   c. Eben. \
   d. Mal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Doch* is the special contradiction answer to a negative question or statement.

</details>

**33.** Your friend is hesitant about joining. Which sentence warmly pushes against that hesitation?

   a. Komm ja mit! \
   b. Komm eben mit! \
   c. Komm denn mit! \
   d. Komm doch mit!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Particle *doch* makes the invitation encouraging and persuasive: “Come on, come with us.”

</details>

**34.** Which question sounds like a conversational follow-up rather than a clipped request for information?

   a. Was machst du mal? \
   b. Was machst du ja? \
   c. Was machst du eben? \
   d. Was machst du denn?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Particle *denn* in questions adds interest and conversational warmth.

</details>

**35.** You have been discussing work. A new thought occurs to you and you ask where your friend lives. Which sentence best signals the aside?

   a. Wo wohnst du ja? \
   b. Wo wohnst du denn nicht? \
   c. Wo wohnst du eigentlich? \
   d. Wo wohnst du halt?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** In questions, *eigentlich* often introduces a small topic shift: “by the way / come to think of it.”

</details>

**36.** A friend asks you to wait. Which version makes the short command smaller and more casual?

   a. Warte ja. \
   b. Warte mal. \
   c. Warte denn. \
   d. Warte eben.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *mal* commonly softens imperatives and casual requests: *Warte mal.*

</details>

**37.** Which sentence means “Have you ever been to Vienna?” using the source-taught chunk?

   a. Warst du wieder in Wien? \
   b. Warst du schon mal in Wien? \
   c. Warst du immer in Wien? \
   d. Warst du noch in Wien?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *schon mal* asks whether something has happened at some point before now: *Warst du schon mal …?*

</details>

**38.** Which sentence best expresses one continuous wait that is lasting surprisingly long?

   a. Wir warten immer wieder. \
   b. Wir warten wieder. \
   c. Wir warten schon mal. \
   d. Wir warten immer noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* is one continuing situation with added surprise or impatience. *immer wieder* would mean separate repeated waits.

</details>

**39.** Which sentence is the natural way to say “I cannot come today” with a modal verb?

   a. Ich kann heute nicht kommen. \
   b. Ich nicht kann heute kommen. \
   c. Ich kann nicht heute kommen nicht. \
   d. Ich kann heute kommen nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** With the second verb at the end, *nicht* comes before that final verb: *nicht kommen*.

</details>

**40.** Which sentence correctly gives a reason with **denn** as a conjunction?

   a. Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich müde bin. \
   b. Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde. \
   c. Denn ich bin müde, ich bleibe zu Hause. \
   d. Ich bleibe denn zu Hause ich bin müde.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Conjunction *denn* means “because” and keeps normal main-clause word order: *denn ich bin müde*. This is separate from particle *denn* in questions.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve, Transform, and Produce

Write your answer before opening the solution. Natural alternatives are acceptable when they preserve the target distinction and use correct A1 German.

**41. Error correction.** Correct the sentence for “I have no time”:

**Ich habe nicht Zeit.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe keine Zeit.**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is the noun being negated, so use *kein*: feminine *keine Zeit*.

</details>

**42. Changed situation.** Start with **Der Laden ist noch offen.** The shop closes. Change the sentence to say it is no longer open.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Der Laden ist nicht mehr offen.**

**Explanation:** *noch* marks continuation; *nicht mehr* marks that the previous state has ended.

</details>

**43. Changed situation.** Start with **Ich bin noch nicht fertig.** You finish sooner than expected. Change the sentence appropriately.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin schon fertig.**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* describes a pending result; *schon* describes the result as reached by now, especially early.

</details>

**44. Sentence reconstruction.** Put the pieces into a natural sentence:

`am Montag / um acht / der Film / beginnt`

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Der Film beginnt am Montag um acht.**

**Explanation:** The day takes *am* and the clock time takes *um*.

</details>

**45. Unsupported cloze.** Complete with the natural preposition:

**Ich fahre ___ dem Zug nach Hamburg.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **mit**

**Explanation:** Means of transport takes *mit* + dative: *mit dem Zug*.

</details>

**46. Controlled production.** Say that you are going home now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich gehe jetzt nach Hause.**

**Explanation:** *nach Hause* is the fixed expression for movement homeward. *zu Hause* is location.

</details>

**47. Controlled production.** Order another coffee after finishing your first one.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Noch einen Kaffee, bitte.**

**Explanation:** *noch einen Kaffee* requests one more of the same item for the same person.

</details>

**48. Controlled production.** You left the hotel for dinner and have now returned. Say that you are back.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin wieder da.**

**Explanation:** *wieder da* says you were here, left, and have returned. It is a core active chunk from the *wieder* lesson.

</details>

## Scoring Guide

Use the score diagnostically rather than as a final grade.

- **41–48:** strong cumulative recognition and controlled application. Keep spacing the review rather than immediately repeating it.
- **33–40:** identify the two or three concepts behind your errors and revisit those lesson/comparison pages.
- **24–32:** the interleaving is exposing real gaps; review the specific lesson or comparison behind the repeated errors before retaking.
- **Under 24:** return to the core words and preposition decision patterns before adding more review load.

For production questions, accept a natural alternative if it expresses the target relationship correctly. Do not require exact wording when German allows more than one good sentence.
