These questions are about **tone**, not meaning. In most of them, removing the particle would leave the sentence true — but different in feel. That's what you're being asked to identify.

Only one question here asks you to produce anything, because these words are **Recognition First**. That's deliberate.

---

## Part A — What Does the Particle Do?

**1.** Warte mal.
- a. A sharper order than *Warte!*
- b. A softer, more casual version of *Warte!*
- c. A question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** softer and more casual.

</details>

**2.** Wo warst du denn?
- a. An accusation.
- b. A curious, friendly question.
- c. A command.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** curious and friendly. Without *denn* the same question can sound like an interrogation.

</details>

**3.** Komm doch mit!
- a. A neutral invitation.
- b. A warm, persuasive invitation.
- c. A refusal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** warm and persuasive; you're overcoming a hesitation.

</details>

**4.** Das ist ja teuer!
- a. Answering "yes."
- b. Surprise at the price.
- c. Asking about the price.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** surprise. Mid-sentence *ja* is never "yes."

</details>

**5.** Das ist eben so.
- a. Resigned acceptance.
- b. Anger.
- c. A question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** resigned acceptance: that's just how it is.

</details>

**6.** Wie alt bist du eigentlich?
- a. A suspicious demand.
- b. A friendly, slightly off-topic question.
- c. A correction.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** a friendly aside, often introducing a new topic.

</details>

**7.** Das ist halt so.
- a. Formal written German.
- b. Casual spoken resignation.
- c. An order to stop.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** casual spoken resignation, and more southern in flavour.

</details>

## Part B — Choose the Right Response

**8.** Someone asks **Hast du keinen Hunger?** and you *are* hungry.\
   a. Ja.\
   b. Doch.\
   c. Nein.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Doch.* (Contradicting a negative question.)

</details>

**9.** Someone says **Ich habe keine Zeit.** How do you say "me neither"?\
   a. Ich auch.\
   b. Ich auch nicht.\
   c. Ich nicht auch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch nicht.*

</details>

**10.** You want to say "not really" in answer to a question.\
   a. Eigentlich nicht.\
   b. Gar nicht.\
   c. Noch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Eigentlich nicht.*

</details>

**11.** You strongly agree with what someone just said.\
   a. Eben!\
   b. Doch!\
   c. Schon!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Eben!*

</details>

## Part C — Same Sentence, With and Without

Say what the particle adds.

**12.** Komm mit! / Komm doch mit!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *doch* turns a plain invitation into an encouraging one

**Explanation:** closer to "oh, do come with us."

</details>

**13.** Was machst du? / Was machst du denn?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *denn* adds interest and warmth. It makes the question conversational rather than clipped.

**Explanation:** This matches the conversational effect of the particle being tested; the particle mainly changes tone or stance rather than the core facts.

</details>

**14.** Kannst du mir helfen? / Kannst du mir mal helfen?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *mal* makes the request smaller and more casual

**Explanation:** "give me a hand" rather than "can you help me."

</details>

**15.** Es ist nicht weit. / Es ist ja nicht weit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *ja* appeals to something you both already know: "it isn't far, after all"

**Explanation:** often meant reassuringly.

</details>

## Part D — Produce It

**16.** Someone says *Du kommst nicht mit* ("you're not coming"), but you are. Contradict them in one word.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Doch!**

**Explanation:** This matches the conversational effect of the particle being tested; the particle mainly changes tone or stance rather than the core facts.

</details>

### What Success Looks Like Here

Not a perfect score. If you can now hear that *Wo warst du denn?* is friendlier than *Wo warst du?* — without being able to explain exactly why — that's the skill. It develops through exposure over months.

The trap to avoid is the opposite one: reading this page and immediately starting to sprinkle particles into your own sentences. Keep recognizing for a good while longer.
