# Contrast 01 — Near-Identical Sentences

**Covers:** [noch](../words/noch.md) · [schon](../words/schon.md) · [wieder](../words/wieder.md) · [erst](../words/erst.md) · [nicht mehr](../patterns/nicht-mehr.md) · [zu Hause / nach Hause](../comparisons/zu-hause-vs-nach-hause.md)
**Do this after:** Stage 1, and again after Stage 3

[← Recognition 01](recognition-01.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [Next → Sentence Completion 01](sentence-completion-01.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

The sentences in this review are deliberately similar. The task is to notice the **timeline, change of state, expectation, or location/movement relationship** created by one small difference.

---

## Part A — Choose the Situation That Fits

**1.** Paul was sick Monday and Tuesday. He never recovered, and he is sick on Wednesday too. Which sentence fits Wednesday?

   a. Paul ist wieder krank. \
   b. Paul ist noch krank. \
   c. Paul ist nicht mehr krank. \
   d. Paul ist schon krank.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** There was no break in the illness, so *noch krank* describes one continuing state. *wieder krank* would require a recovery and then a return of the illness.

</details>

**2.** Paul recovered on Wednesday. On Saturday he becomes sick a second time. Which sentence fits Saturday?

   a. Paul ist noch krank. \
   b. Paul ist schon krank. \
   c. Paul ist nicht mehr krank. \
   d. Paul ist wieder krank.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The state stopped and later returned, so *wieder* is required. The gap is the key difference from *noch*.

</details>

**3.** Your train leaves at 8:10. You look at the clock and are surprised that it is already 8:00. Which sentence fits your reaction?

   a. Es ist schon acht Uhr. \
   b. Es ist erst acht Uhr. \
   c. Es ist noch acht Uhr. \
   d. Es ist wieder acht Uhr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *schon acht Uhr* presents 8:00 as later or further along than you expected: “It’s eight already.”

</details>

**4.** Your appointment is at 11:00. It is 8:00, and a friend is worried about being late. You want to say there is plenty of time. Which sentence fits?

   a. Es ist schon acht Uhr. \
   b. Es ist wieder acht Uhr. \
   c. Es ist erst acht Uhr. \
   d. Es ist nicht mehr acht Uhr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *erst acht Uhr* presents the same clock time as an early point: only 8:00 so far. *schon* would push the attitude in the opposite direction.

</details>

**5.** The shop opens at 10:00. It is 9:30, the doors are closed, and you were hoping to shop now. Which sentence best describes the opening time?

   a. Das Geschäft öffnet schon um zehn. \
   b. Das Geschäft öffnet erst um zehn. \
   c. Das Geschäft ist wieder um zehn offen. \
   d. Das Geschäft ist noch um zehn offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *erst um zehn* means “not until ten”: the relevant point is later than desired or expected. There is no visible *nicht*, but the time before ten is excluded.

</details>

**6.** Anna was at the hotel at 7:00. At 8:00 she has left. Which sentence fits 8:00?

   a. Anna ist noch nicht im Hotel. \
   b. Anna ist schon im Hotel. \
   c. Anna ist nicht mehr im Hotel. \
   d. Anna ist wieder im Hotel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** She was there before and is no longer there now, so *nicht mehr* marks a state that has stopped.

</details>

**7.** Anna is expected at the hotel at 8:00. It is 7:50 and she has not arrived. Which sentence fits?

   a. Anna ist noch nicht im Hotel. \
   b. Anna ist nicht mehr im Hotel. \
   c. Anna ist wieder im Hotel. \
   d. Anna ist schon im Hotel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* says the expected state has not begun yet. *nicht mehr* would say the state existed before and has ended.

</details>

**8.** You are in your apartment and will stay there this evening. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich gehe heute nach Hause. \
   b. Ich bin heute nach Hause. \
   c. Ich fahre heute zu Hause. \
   d. Ich bleibe heute zu Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *zu Hause* is the fixed expression for location: being or staying at home. *nach Hause* is for movement toward home.

</details>

**9.** You leave work and start the trip home. Which sentence is natural?

   a. Ich bin jetzt zu Hause. \
   b. Ich gehe jetzt nach Hause. \
   c. Ich gehe jetzt zu Hause. \
   d. Ich bin jetzt nach Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Going home is movement toward home, so German uses the fixed expression *nach Hause*.

</details>

**10.** The restaurant was closed for renovation and opens today. Which sentence best describes today?

   a. Das Restaurant ist noch offen. \
   b. Das Restaurant ist wieder offen. \
   c. Das Restaurant ist schon offen. \
   d. Das Restaurant ist nicht mehr offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The restaurant was open, then closed, and now has returned to the open state. That stop-and-return timeline is *wieder*.

</details>

## Part B — Timelines and Minimal Pairs

**11.** Timeline:

`07:45 — Lukas arrives.`\
`08:00 — Lukas is still there.`\
`08:30 — Lukas leaves.`

You are checking whether Lukas has left yet. Which sentence fits 08:00?

   a. Lukas ist noch da. \
   b. Lukas ist wieder da. \
   c. Lukas ist nicht mehr da. \
   d. Lukas ist noch nicht da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** From arrival through 08:00 there is one uninterrupted state: he is *noch da*. At 08:30, *nicht mehr da* would fit.

</details>

**12.** Timeline:

`09:00 — The café is open.`\
`12:00 — The café closes.`\
`14:00 — The café opens.`

Which sentence fits 14:00?

   a. Das Café ist noch offen. \
   b. Das Café ist schon offen. \
   c. Das Café ist nicht mehr offen. \
   d. Das Café ist wieder offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The open state was interrupted and returned, so *wieder offen* is the precise choice.

</details>

**13.** Compare **Der Zug ist noch nicht da.** and **Der Zug ist nicht mehr da.** Which description is correct?

   a. The first says the train left; the second says it is expected later. \
   b. Both mean exactly the same thing. \
   c. The first says arrival is still pending; the second says the train was there and has left. \
   d. The first means the train returned; the second means it is early.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* points forward to an expected event that has not happened. *nicht mehr* points backward to a state that existed and has ended.

</details>

**14.** Compare **Ich bin noch hier.** and **Ich bin schon hier.** What changes?

   a. *noch* says you have not left; *schon* says you have arrived by now, perhaps earlier than expected. \
   b. *noch* says you returned; *schon* says you are leaving. \
   c. *noch* is negative; *schon* is positive. \
   d. There is no meaningful difference.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Both sentences locate you here, but the timeline is opposite: *noch* looks toward a departure that has not happened; *schon* looks back at an arrival that has happened.

</details>

**15.** Which situation fits **Wir warten schon eine Stunde** better than **Wir warten erst eine Stunde**?

   a. The speaker thinks one hour is a short amount so far. \
   b. The speaker thinks one hour is a substantial amount of waiting. \
   c. The speaker stopped waiting and started again. \
   d. The speaker will not begin waiting until one hour from now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** With amounts, *schon* colours the amount as more than expected or a lot. *erst* would frame the same amount as only a little so far.

</details>

**16.** Which situation fits **Ich lerne erst zwei Monate Deutsch**?

   a. The speaker stopped learning German two months ago. \
   b. The speaker has learned German before and restarted. \
   c. The speaker thinks two months is a long time. \
   d. The speaker presents two months as only a short amount of progress so far.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *erst* measures progress as less far along than the listener might think: only two months so far.

</details>

**17.** Which sentence means the speaker used to live in Berlin but does not now?

   a. Ich wohne noch nicht in Berlin. \
   b. Ich wohne wieder in Berlin. \
   c. Ich wohne nicht mehr in Berlin. \
   d. Ich wohne schon in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *nicht mehr* requires a previous true state that has ended: the speaker lived there before, but no longer does.

</details>

**18.** Which sentence means the speaker left home and later returned?

   a. Ich bin wieder zu Hause. \
   b. Ich bin noch zu Hause. \
   c. Ich gehe nach Hause. \
   d. Ich bin nicht mehr zu Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *wieder zu Hause* means the state “at home” has returned after an interruption. *noch zu Hause* would mean the speaker never left.

</details>

**19.** A train is scheduled for 10:00, which feels frustratingly late. Which sentence captures that attitude?

   a. Der Zug kommt schon um zehn. \
   b. Der Zug kommt noch um zehn. \
   c. Der Zug kommt wieder um zehn. \
   d. Der Zug kommt erst um zehn.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *erst um zehn* means not until ten and frames ten as later than wanted or expected.

</details>

**20.** A train unexpectedly arrives at 09:30 although you thought it would be later. Which sentence best fits?

   a. Der Zug ist noch nicht da. \
   b. Der Zug ist schon da. \
   c. Der Zug ist nicht mehr da. \
   d. Der Zug ist wieder da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *schon da* says the expected arrival has already happened by now, with an earlier-than-expected feeling.

</details>

## Part C — Correct, Incorrect, or Correct but Different

**21.** Intended situation: Maria was ill, recovered, and is ill again. Judge **Maria ist noch krank.**

   a. Correct and expresses the intended timeline. \
   b. Incorrect German. \
   c. Grammatically correct, but it says the illness continued without a recovery. \
   d. Grammatically correct, but it says Maria is healthy now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch krank* is good German, but it describes continuation. The intended stop-and-return timeline needs *wieder krank*.

</details>

**22.** Intended situation: you are currently at home. Judge **Ich bin nach Hause.**

   a. Correct and natural. \
   b. Correct but unusually formal. \
   c. Correct, but it means you are going home. \
   d. Incorrect for this meaning; say **Ich bin zu Hause.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *zu Hause* is the fixed location expression. *nach Hause* belongs with movement toward home and cannot follow *sein* for this meaning.

</details>

**23.** Intended situation: the shop was open, but it has now closed. Judge **Das Geschäft ist noch offen.**

   a. Grammatically correct, but it expresses the opposite timeline: the shop has not closed yet. \
   b. Incorrect German. \
   c. Correct and expresses that the shop has closed. \
   d. Correct, but it means the shop reopened.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch offen* says the open state continues. To say the shop is no longer open, use *nicht mehr offen*.

</details>

**24.** Intended situation: the train has not arrived yet but is expected. Which sentence is the most natural?

   a. Der Zug ist nicht mehr da. \
   b. Der Zug ist wieder da. \
   c. Der Zug ist schon da. \
   d. Der Zug ist noch nicht da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht da* presents arrival as pending. *nicht mehr da* would mean the train had been there and then left.

</details>

**25.** Intended idea: “I have no time left.” Which sentence is the natural noun pattern?

   a. Ich habe keine Zeit mehr. \
   b. Ich habe nicht mehr Zeit. \
   c. Ich habe noch keine Zeit. \
   d. Ich habe wieder keine Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** With a noun, *nicht mehr* becomes the split pattern *kein … mehr*: *keine Zeit mehr*. This says a previous supply of time has run out.

</details>

**26.** Which sentence is incorrect for standard German with the intended meaning “I’m going home”?

   a. Ich fahre nach Hause. \
   b. Ich muss nach Hause. \
   c. Ich gehe zu Hause. \
   d. Ich gehe nach Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** For movement toward home, use *nach Hause*. *zu Hause* describes location, not the destination of *gehen*.

</details>

**27.** The time is exactly 8:00. Which pair can both be factually true while expressing opposite attitudes toward the same time?

   a. **Es ist noch acht Uhr. / Es ist wieder acht Uhr.** \
   b. **Es ist schon acht Uhr. / Es ist erst acht Uhr.** \
   c. **Es ist nicht mehr acht Uhr. / Es ist noch nicht acht Uhr.** \
   d. **Es ist wieder acht Uhr. / Es ist nicht mehr acht Uhr.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *schon acht* frames 8:00 as later/further along than expected; *erst acht* frames the identical fact as early/only so far.

</details>

**28.** Which statement about *noch* and *wieder* is correct?

   a. *noch* requires a break; *wieder* requires continuous action. \
   b. They are interchangeable whenever English says “still.” \
   c. *noch* normally describes continuation without a gap; *wieder* describes return after a gap. \
   d. *wieder* always means “not yet.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The mental timeline test is simple: no gap → *noch*; a stop and restart → *wieder*.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Transform

Write your answer before opening the solution. Natural alternatives are acceptable when they preserve the target distinction.

**29. Changed situation.** Start with **Er ist noch krank.** Now change the situation: he recovered, then became ill again. Change only the small word that must change.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Er ist wieder krank.**

**Explanation:** Recovery creates a gap, so continuation with *noch* becomes recurrence with *wieder*.

</details>

**30. Changed situation.** Start with **Der Zug ist noch nicht da.** Now the train arrives earlier than expected. Change the sentence to match the new situation.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Der Zug ist schon da.**

**Explanation:** The expected event was pending with *noch nicht*; once it has happened by now, especially early, *schon* fits.

</details>

**31. Error correction.** Correct this sentence for the intended meaning “I don’t have any bread left”:

**Ich habe nicht mehr Brot.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe kein Brot mehr.**

**Explanation:** With a noun, German uses *kein … mehr*, placing the negative article before the noun and *mehr* after it.

</details>

**32. Unsupported cloze.** Complete with **erst** or **schon**:

Your bus does not come until 11:00, and you wish it came earlier.\
**Der Bus kommt ___ um elf.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **erst**

**Explanation:** *erst um elf* means “not until eleven” and presents the time as later than desired.

</details>

**33. Sentence reconstruction.** Put the pieces into a natural sentence meaning “We are going home now”:

`jetzt / wir / nach Hause / gehen`

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Wir gehen jetzt nach Hause.**

**Explanation:** *nach Hause* is the fixed destination expression for movement homeward.

</details>

**34. Controlled production.** Write one short sentence saying that a café is no longer open.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural answer is **Das Café ist nicht mehr offen.**

**Explanation:** *nicht mehr* says the open state existed before and has now ended.

</details>
