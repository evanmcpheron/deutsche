## Core Idea

German present-tense verbs change their ending to match the subject. For a regular verb such as **machen**, remove `-en` to find the stem **mach-**, then add the person ending.

| Person | Ending | `machen` |
|---|---:|---|
| ich | `-e` | ich **mache** |
| du | `-st` | du **machst** |
| er / sie / es | `-t` | er **macht** |
| wir | `-en` | wir **machen** |
| ihr | `-t` | ihr **macht** |
| sie / Sie | `-en` | sie / Sie **machen** |

The same pattern works for many common A1 verbs: `lernen`, `wohnen`, `kaufen`, `spielen`, `fragen`, `sagen`, `brauchen`.

## The Useful Shortcut

Three forms are especially predictable:

- `wir` = infinitive: **wir machen**
- `sie / Sie` = infinitive: **sie machen / Sie machen**
- `er / sie / es` and `ihr` usually share the same `-t` form: **er macht / ihr macht**

That leaves `ich` and `du` as the two forms you retrieve most often.

## Present Tense Covers More Than English “Present”

German often uses Präsens where English uses either simple present or present progressive.

**Ich lerne Deutsch.**
I learn German / I am learning German.

**Was machst du?**
What do you do? / What are you doing?

German does not need a separate “am/is/are + -ing” form.

## The Conjugated Verb Is Position Two in a Statement

**Ich lerne heute Deutsch.**

**Heute lerne ich Deutsch.**

The first element can change, but the conjugated verb remains the second element. Do not keep the English subject-verb order after moving something to the front:

- ❌ `Heute ich lerne Deutsch.`
- ✅ `Heute lerne ich Deutsch.`

## Questions

### Yes/no questions: verb first

**Lernst du Deutsch?**
Are you learning German?

**Wohnt ihr hier?**
Do you all live here?

### W-questions: question word, then verb

**Wo wohnst du?**
Where do you live?

**Was macht ihr?**
What are you doing?

## Negation Does Not Change the Conjugation

**Ich komme nicht.**
I am not coming.

**Er arbeitet heute nicht.**
He is not working today.

The verb still agrees with the subject.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Leaving the infinitive unchanged:** `ich machen` instead of **ich mache**.
- **Using the wrong `du` ending:** `du macht` instead of **du machst**.
- **Forgetting `ihr`:** `ihr machen` instead of **ihr macht**.
- **Treating German like English progressive:** `Ich bin lernen` is not “I am learning.” Use **Ich lerne.**
- **Breaking verb-second word order:** `Heute ich arbeite` instead of **Heute arbeite ich.**

## What to Memorize

Memorize the six endings as a pattern:

> **-e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en**

Then practice them in real sentences instead of reciting the sequence alone.
