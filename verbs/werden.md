# werden — to become

**Type:** Irregular verb
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Core recognition, useful active production
**Knowledge goal:** Active for common present uses

## Core Idea

At A1, the most useful meaning of `werden` is **to become / get**.

| Person | Present |
|---|---|
| ich | **werde** |
| du | **wirst** |
| er / sie / es | **wird** |
| wir | **werden** |
| ihr | **werdet** |
| sie / Sie | **werden** |

The `du` and `er/sie/es` forms are the irregular forms to notice: **wirst, wird**.

## Common A1 Uses

**Es wird kalt.**
It is getting cold.

**Ich werde müde.**
I am getting tired.

**Das Wetter wird besser.**
The weather is getting better.

**Wann wirst du 36?**
When do you turn 36?

## `sein` vs. `werden`

Use `sein` for a state and `werden` for a change into that state.

**Ich bin müde.**
I am tired.

**Ich werde müde.**
I am getting tired.

**Es ist kalt.**
It is cold.

**Es wird kalt.**
It is getting cold.

## About the Future Tense

`werden + infinitive` can form a future tense, but German often uses ordinary Präsens for near-future A1 statements:

**Morgen arbeite ich.**
I am working tomorrow.

Do not make future-tense formation a priority yet. Learn the present forms of `werden` first.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- ❌ `du werdst` → ✅ **du wirst**
- ❌ `er werdt` → ✅ **er wird**
- Using `werden` just because English says “will” when simple German present tense is enough.
- Confusing a state with a change: `Ich werde müde` means the tiredness is developing.
