# Essential Präteritum

**Type:** Past-tense forms
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Selective
**Knowledge goal:** Active for `war` and `hatte`; recognition-first for common modal forms

## Core Idea

German has a simple past tense called **Präteritum**. You do not need to learn the full Präteritum system at A1.

The exception is that a few very common verbs appear in Präteritum constantly, even in ordinary conversation. Learn these selectively instead of trying to replace Perfekt.

## `sein` — `war`

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| ich | war |
| du | warst |
| er / sie / es | war |
| wir | waren |
| ihr | wart |
| sie / Sie | waren |

**Ich war gestern zu Hause.**
I was at home yesterday.

**Wie war der Film?**
How was the movie?

These forms are worth active production.

## `haben` — `hatte`

| Person | Form |
|---|---|
| ich | hatte |
| du | hattest |
| er / sie / es | hatte |
| wir | hatten |
| ihr | hattet |
| sie / Sie | hatten |

**Ich hatte keine Zeit.**
I did not have time.

Active production is useful, but `war` is usually the first priority.

## Common Modal Präteritum

You will also hear and read:

- `können` → **konnte**
- `müssen` → **musste**
- `wollen` → **wollte**
- `dürfen` → **durfte**
- `sollen` → **sollte**

Examples:

**Ich konnte nicht kommen.**
I could not come.

**Wir mussten arbeiten.**
We had to work.

**Er wollte nach Hause gehen.**
He wanted to go home.

At A1, recognition is more important than producing every person of every modal past tense.

## Do Not Generalize Präteritum to Everything Yet

For an ordinary past action, conversational German usually favors Perfekt:

**Ich habe Deutsch gelernt.**

not as an A1 production priority:

`Ich lernte Deutsch.`

The second sentence is grammatical, but learning full written-style Präteritum would add a large amount of work without helping your early conversation proportionally.

## `war` vs. `ist gewesen`

Both can refer to the past:

**Ich war in Berlin.**

**Ich bin in Berlin gewesen.**

At A1, learn **war** because it is extremely common and compact. You do not need to force a rigid “spoken vs. written” rule onto every context.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- Using present `bin` for a completed past situation.
- Building `war` with an auxiliary: `Ich habe war` is wrong.
- Trying to use English “did” as a German helper verb.
- Attempting full Präteritum production before common Perfekt is stable.
