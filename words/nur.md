# nur

**Type:** Adverb / particle
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

[← schon](schon.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [Next → sehr](sehr.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

*nur* draws a line around something and says: **that's all — nothing beyond this.**

English uses "only" or "just," and for once the mapping is fairly clean. The thing to watch is **position**: *nur* limits whatever comes right after it.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Only" — limiting an amount

**Ich habe nur zehn Euro.**
I only have ten euros.

**Wir bleiben nur zwei Tage.**
We're only staying two days.

**Das kostet nur drei Euro.**
That costs only three euros.

### 2. "Just" — playing something down

**Ich habe nur eine Frage.**
I just have one question.

**Das war nur ein Witz.**
That was just a joke.

**Ich wollte nur helfen.**
I only wanted to help.

### 3. Limiting a person or thing

**Nur ich war da.**
Only I was there.

**Ich trinke nur Wasser.**
I only drink water.

**Wir haben nur Zimmer mit Dusche.**
We only have rooms with a shower.

### 4. In encouragement or warnings *(Tier 2 — recognize)*

**Komm nur!**
Come on, do come.

**Mach das nur nicht!**
Whatever you do, don't do that.

Here *nur* isn't limiting anything — it's intensifying a request. This is *nur* behaving like a modal particle; see [Modal Particles Overview](../reference/modal-particles-overview.md).

## Position Changes the Meaning

**Ich trinke nur Wasser.**
Water is the only thing I drink.

**Nur ich trinke Wasser.**
I'm the only one who drinks water.

**Ich habe nur zwei Kinder.**
I have only two children *(not more)*.

Whatever follows *nur* is what's being limited. Same as [auch](auch.md), and the same trap.

## Common Chunks

- **nur noch** — only … left: *Wir haben nur noch zwei Minuten.* — We only have two minutes left.
- **nicht nur … sondern auch …** — not only … but also … *(A2 — recognize)*
- **nur ein bisschen** — just a little → [ein bisschen](../patterns/ein-bisschen.md)
- **nur so** — just because, no particular reason: *Warum? — Nur so.*

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich habe nur zehn Euro.**
**Ich habe nur eine Frage.**
**Wir bleiben nur bis Freitag.**
**Das ist nur Wasser.**
**Ich wollte nur fragen.**

Each one shrinks something: less money, fewer questions, a shorter stay, a smaller claim.

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich habe eine Frage.**
I have a question.

**Ich habe nur eine Frage.**
I just have one question. *(this won't take long / don't worry)*

The second version is softer. That's why *nur* is so common in polite speech — it makes a request smaller.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Putting it at the end.** English lets you say "I have ten euros only." German wants *nur* before the limited item: *Ich habe nur zehn Euro.*
- **Confusing *nur noch* with *noch*.** *Wir haben noch zwei Minuten* = we still have two minutes. *Wir haben nur noch zwei Minuten* = we only have two minutes left. The second one is worried.
- **Mixing up *nur* and *erst*.** Both can translate as "only," but *erst* is specifically about time or amounts that are expected to grow: see [erst](erst.md).

## Active or Passive?

**Active** for uses 1–3. **Recognition First** for use 4, the encouraging/warning particle.

## You May Hear

- **Nur zu!** — Go ahead!
- **Das war nur Spaß.** — That was just a joke.
- **Wir haben nur noch eins.** — We've only got one left.
- **Nur so.** — No particular reason.
