## Core Idea

*erst* marks something as **earlier or smaller than you'd expect** — the point reached so far is less than the listener might assume.

That single idea produces what look like three different English words:

- "only" (a small amount so far)
- "not until" (later than you'd like)
- "first" (before anything else)

*erst* is the opposite number of [schon](schon.md): *schon* says "already, that's a lot," *erst* says "only so far." See [erst vs. schon](../comparisons/erst-vs-schon.md).

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Only" — a small amount so far

**Es ist erst acht Uhr.**
It's only eight o'clock. *(there's plenty of time)*

**Ich lerne erst zwei Monate Deutsch.**
I've only been learning German for two months.

**Wir sind erst am Anfang.**
We're only at the beginning.

### 2. "Not until" — later than expected

**Der Zug kommt erst um zehn.**
The train doesn't come until ten.

**Das Geschäft öffnet erst um neun.**
The shop doesn't open until nine.

**Ich komme erst morgen.**
I'm not coming until tomorrow.

This use trips English speakers up badly, because there's no visible "not" in the German. *erst um zehn* already means "not before ten."

### 3. "First" — before anything else

**Erst essen, dann arbeiten.**
Eat first, then work.

**Ich muss erst duschen.**
I have to shower first.

## Common Chunks

- **erst mal** — first of all, for now: *Erst mal einen Kaffee.* — Coffee first.
- **erst um …** — not until … (a clock time)
- **erst wenn …** — only when … *(A2)*
- **zuerst** — first (in a sequence) — a close relative, and often interchangeable with *erst* in use 3

## Pattern Recognition

**Es ist erst sieben Uhr.** — early, don't worry
**Ich bin erst seit Montag hier.** — only since Monday
**Der Bus fährt erst um acht.** — not before eight
**Erst mal Kaffee!** — coffee before anything else

Every one says "less far along than you might think."

## What Changes Without It?

**Es ist acht Uhr.**
It's eight o'clock. *(neutral fact)*

**Es ist erst acht Uhr.**
It's only eight. *(relax, we have time)*

**Es ist schon acht Uhr.**
It's eight already. *(hurry up)*

Three sentences, identical facts, opposite emotional messages. This trio is the clearest illustration in the whole repository of why translation alone doesn't teach these words.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Missing the hidden negative.** *Der Zug kommt erst um zehn* means the train does **not** come before ten. Learners often read it as "the train comes at ten, first."
- **Substituting *nur*.** *Ich habe nur zehn Euro* (only ten euros — a fixed amount) vs. *Es ist erst acht Uhr* (only eight — a point on a scale that will keep moving). *nur* limits; *erst* measures progress.
- **Confusing it with *erste*** (= "first" as an adjective: *der erste Tag*). Related, but a different word class.

## Active or Passive?

**Recognition First.** You'll meet *erst* constantly in timetables, shop hours, and everyday talk, and misreading it can make you miss a train. Producing it can wait — *nur* and *nicht … bis* will cover you in the meantime.

## You May Hear

- **Wir haben erst ab neun geöffnet.** — We don't open until nine.
- **Erst mal langsam!** — Slow down / one thing at a time.
- **Ich bin erst seit gestern hier.** — I only got here yesterday.
- **Das kommt erst später.** — That comes later.
