# auch

**Type:** Adverb; also used as a light emphasis particle
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — very high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

## Core Idea

*auch* adds someone or something to what's already true. English says "also," "too," or "as well" — German says *auch*, and puts it **directly before the thing being added**.

That position is the whole game. Move *auch* and you change who is included.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Me too" — adding a person

**Ich komme auch.**
I'm coming too.

**Wir fahren auch nach Berlin.**
We're going to Berlin as well.

**Ich auch!**
Me too! *(a complete answer on its own)*

### 2. Adding a thing

**Ich möchte auch einen Kaffee.**
I'd like a coffee too.

**Wir haben auch Tee.**
We have tea as well.

### 3. In the negative — "not … either"

**Ich auch nicht.**
Me neither.

**Das weiß ich auch nicht.**
I don't know that either.

Note the order: **auch nicht**, not *nicht auch*.

### 4. As a light emphasis particle

Here *auch* isn't adding anything — it's confirming that something makes sense, roughly "no wonder" or "well, yes."

**Es ist auch kalt heute.**
It really is cold today. / Well, it is cold today.

**Du bist auch immer müde.**
You're always tired, aren't you.

This use is *Tier 2* — recognize it, don't chase it.

## Position Changes the Meaning

Same words, different placement, different sentence:

**Ich trinke auch Kaffee.**
I drink coffee too. *(as well as other people, or as well as tea)*

**Auch ich trinke Kaffee.**
*I* too drink coffee. *(emphasis on me — slightly formal/emphatic)*

**Ich trinke auch gern Kaffee.**
I like drinking coffee too.

The rule of thumb: whatever comes **right after** *auch* is what's being added.

## Common Chunks

- **ich auch** — me too
- **ich auch nicht** — me neither
- **auch gut** — that works too / fine by me
- **auch nicht schlecht** — not bad either
- **sowohl … als auch …** — both … and … *(A2 — recognize only)*

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich habe Hunger. — Ich auch.**
**Ich habe keinen Hunger. — Ich auch nicht.**
**Kommst du mit? — Ja, ich komme auch mit.**
**Wir gehen auch ins Kino.**
**Das ist auch möglich.**

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich komme.**
I'm coming.

**Ich komme auch.**
I'm coming too. *(someone else is already coming)*

*auch* smuggles in a whole extra fact: that this isn't the first case. That's why dropping it sounds abrupt when someone has just told you their plans.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Putting it at the end, English-style.** "I'm coming too" tempts you into *Ich komme too* → *Ich komme auch* is right, but be careful in longer sentences: *Ich fahre auch nach Berlin*, not *Ich fahre nach Berlin auch*.
- **Reversing *auch nicht*.** It's *Ich auch nicht*, never *Ich nicht auch*.
- **Confusing it with [noch](noch.md).** *auch* = also, in addition to someone/something else. *noch* = in addition, one more of the same. See [auch vs. noch](../comparisons/auch-vs-noch.md).

## Active or Passive?

**Active** for uses 1–3 — *ich auch* and *ich auch nicht* are among the most useful two-word replies in German. **Recognition First** for the emphasis particle in use 4.

## You May Hear

- **Ich auch.** — Me too.
- **Auch gut.** — That's fine too.
- **Haben Sie auch Tee?** — Do you have tea as well?
- **Das dachte ich auch.** — I thought so too.
