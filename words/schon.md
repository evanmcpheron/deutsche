# schon

**Type:** Adverb / particle
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — very high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

## Core Idea

*schon* says: **it has already happened — earlier than you might think.**

It's the mirror image of [noch](noch.md). *noch* means something hasn't run out yet; *schon* means something has already arrived. Both are about the relationship between a fact and an expectation.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Already"

**Er ist schon hier.**
He's already here.

**Ich bin schon fertig.**
I'm already finished.

**Das Restaurant ist schon zu.**
The restaurant is already closed.

### 2. In questions — "yet / by now"

**Bist du schon da?**
Are you there yet?

**Hast du schon gegessen?**
Have you eaten yet?

**Ist der Zug schon weg?**
Has the train already gone?

English switches to "yet" in questions; German just keeps *schon*.

### 3. With amounts of time — "as much as"

**Wir warten schon eine Stunde.**
We've been waiting for an hour already.

**Ich lerne schon zwei Jahre Deutsch.**
I've been learning German for two years now.

Here *schon* colours the number: it makes it sound like **a lot**. The opposite colouring comes from [erst](erst.md) — see [erst vs. schon](../comparisons/erst-vs-schon.md).

### 4. As reassurance — "it'll be fine"

**Das wird schon.**
It'll be all right.

**Das schaffst du schon.**
You'll manage, don't worry.

**Er kommt schon.**
He'll come, don't worry.

This is *schon* working as a particle rather than as "already," and it's very common in speech. Recognize it; don't try to build sentences with it yet.

## Common Chunks

- **schon mal** — ever, at some point before now → [full page](../patterns/schon-mal.md)
- **schon wieder** — yet again *(usually annoyed)*: *Schon wieder du!* — You again!
- **schon gut** — it's fine, never mind: *Schon gut, kein Problem.*
- **noch nicht … schon** — not a chunk; if you're negating, you want [noch nicht](../patterns/noch-nicht.md), because *schon nicht* is not the negative of *schon*.
- **schon lange** — for a long time now

## Pattern Recognition

**Bist du schon da?** — earlier than expected?
**Ich bin schon fertig.** — done, and sooner than you'd think
**Wir warten schon eine Stunde.** — an hour, which is a lot
**Schon wieder!** — again, and too soon
**Das wird schon.** — reassurance

The thread: *schon* pushes something **earlier** or makes it feel **more** than expected.

## What Changes Without It?

**Er ist hier.**
He's here.

**Er ist schon hier.**
He's already here. *(sooner than expected — maybe you're not ready)*

And against *noch*:

**Ich bin noch hier.** — I haven't left yet.
**Ich bin schon hier.** — I've already arrived.

Same speaker, same place, opposite direction of travel.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Negating with *schon nicht*.** To say "not yet," use **noch nicht**. *schon nicht* exists but means something quite different and is not what you want at A1.
- **Missing the reassurance use.** If someone says *Das wird schon*, they aren't saying "that will already." They're saying "don't worry."
- **Overusing it in questions.** *Hast du schon gegessen?* is natural, but not every question needs *schon* — it specifically asks about "by now."
- **Confusing it with [erst](erst.md).** *Wir warten schon seit einer Stunde* = an hour, that's a lot. *Wir warten erst seit einer Stunde* = only an hour so far.

## Active or Passive?

**Active** for uses 1–3 — *Ich bin schon fertig* and *Hast du schon …?* are everyday sentences. **Recognition First** for use 4, the reassurance particle.

## You May Hear

- **Schon gut.** — It's fine / never mind.
- **Warst du schon mal in Deutschland?** — Have you ever been to Germany?
- **Schon wieder?** — Again?
- **Das wird schon.** — It'll work out.
- **Ich komme schon!** — I'm coming! *(slightly impatient)*
