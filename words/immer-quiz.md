# Quiz: immer

[← Back to **immer**](immer.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

---

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. Your bus has arrived late every single morning this month.\
   a. Der Bus kommt noch zu spät.\
   b. Der Bus kommt wieder zu spät.\
   c. Der Bus kommt immer zu spät.\
   d. Der Bus kommt schon zu spät.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Every time, without exception — that's plain *immer*. Option b would describe one more late morning without claiming a pattern.

</details>

2. A regular customer walks into her usual café and says **Wie immer, bitte.**\
   a. Das Gleiche wie sonst.\
   b. Etwas Neues.\
   c. Nichts, danke.\
   d. Bitte schneller.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Wie immer* = "as always," i.e. the usual order. It's a compact phrase that only works where a habit already exists.

</details>

3. He has been sitting in the waiting room for two hours and is visibly fed up.\
   a. Er wartet immer noch.\
   b. Er wartet immer wieder.\
   c. Er wartet immer.\
   d. Er wartet wieder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* is one unbroken wait that has gone on longer than it should. Option c would say he waits as a general habit, which isn't the situation.

</details>

4. A colleague turned up late on Monday, again on Wednesday, and again on Friday — separate occasions each time.\
   a. Er kommt immer noch zu spät.\
   b. Er kommt noch zu spät.\
   c. Er kommt schon zu spät.\
   d. Er kommt immer wieder zu spät.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *immer wieder* = over and over, as a series of separate events. *immer noch* in option a would need one single unbroken lateness, which doesn't make sense here.

</details>

5. In which sentence does *immer* **not** mean "always, every time"?\
   a. Sie ist immer freundlich.\
   b. Er ist immer noch krank.\
   c. Wir essen immer zusammen.\
   d. Ich gehe immer um acht ins Bett.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *immer noch* is a unit meaning "still, even now" — nothing to do with frequency. This is the main thing to learn about *immer*: the chunks pull it away from "always."

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

6. What is the difference between **Der Bus kommt zu spät** and **Der Bus kommt immer zu spät**?\
   a. The second sentence refers only to today.\
   b. The second sentence complains about the bus in general.\
   c. The second sentence is a question.\
   d. Both say exactly the same thing.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Without *immer* you are reporting today. With *immer* you are making a claim about the bus in general — which is why it usually arrives as a complaint.

</details>

7. **Sie ruft immer wieder an.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. She calls once.\
   b. She never calls.\
   c. She calls repeatedly — again and again.\
   d. She is calling right now.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *immer wieder* means repeatedly, as separate events. Reading it word by word as "always again" is the trap — it's one chunk with its own meaning.

</details>

8. You want to say: **He's still ill — this is dragging on.** A learner writes **Er ist immer krank.**\
   a. Correct — exactly that meaning.\
   b. Incorrect German.\
   c. Correct, but impolite.\
   d. Correct German, but it means something different.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Er ist immer krank* is fine German, but it says he's ill all the time as a general pattern. For one long illness you need **Er ist immer noch krank.**

</details>

9. A learner writes **Immer ich trinke Kaffee**, meaning "I always drink coffee." What is wrong?\
   a. *Kaffee* needs an article.\
   b. You need *immer noch* here.\
   c. *trinke* is conjugated incorrectly.\
   d. The verb must be in second position: *Ich trinke immer Kaffee.*

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *immer* normally sits after the verb and subject. Starting a sentence with *Immer* is possible for emphasis, but then the verb has to follow it directly — the subject can't come in between.

</details>

10. It rained on Monday, stopped on Tuesday, rained on Wednesday, stopped on Thursday, and rained again on Friday.\
   a. Es regnet immer noch.\
   b. Es regnet immer wieder.\
   c. Es regnet immer.\
   d. Es regnet noch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Separate spells of rain with dry gaps between them — that's *immer wieder*. Options a and d both require the rain never to have stopped.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

11. Compare **Er ist noch krank** and **Er ist immer noch krank.** What does *immer* add?\
   a. Impatience or surprise.\
   b. Politeness.\
   c. A second illness.\
   d. A question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Plain *noch* states a fact neutrally. *immer noch* comments on it: this has been going on too long. The facts are identical; the attitude isn't.

</details>

12. Which sentence says the shop is not always open?\
   a. Das Geschäft ist nicht immer offen.\
   b. Das Geschäft ist immer noch offen.\
   c. Das Geschäft ist immer offen.\
   d. Das Geschäft ist wieder offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *nicht immer* is the plain negative of the frequency use. Option b is about right now — still open, later than you expected — and d is about the shop reopening.

</details>

13. **Es wird immer kälter.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. It is always cold.\
   b. It is getting warmer.\
   c. It is getting colder and colder.\
   d. It used to be colder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *immer* + comparative means "more and more" — colder and colder. Recognize this pattern; you don't need to produce it at A1.

</details>

14. Complete the dialogue. B has been ill for three weeks without a break.

```text
A: Wie geht es dir?
B: Nicht gut. Ich bin ___ krank.
```

   a. immer\
   b. wieder\
   c. immer noch\
   d. immer wieder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** One long illness, no gaps, and B is fed up — exactly what *immer noch* is for. Option d would describe someone who keeps falling ill and recovering.

</details>

15. **fast immer** — what does this mean?\
   a. Never.\
   b. Almost every time.\
   c. Exactly every time.\
   d. Only once.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *fast* softens *immer* to "nearly always" — useful when you don't want to overclaim. Option c would be plain *immer*.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

16. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does *immer* **not** mean "always"?\
   a. Er wartet immer noch.\
   b. Sie ruft immer wieder an.\
   c. Der Bus kommt immer zu spät.\
   d. Es wird immer kälter.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** Those are the three chunks: *immer noch* = still, *immer wieder* = over and over, *immer* + comparative = more and more. Only c is the plain frequency use.

</details>

17. Fill in the missing words. No options given. Two words.

   You lent your friend a book in March. It is now August and he hasn't given it back. You mention it to someone, slightly annoyed.

```text
Er hat mein Buch ___ ___.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **immer noch**

**Explanation:** *Er hat mein Buch immer noch.* Plain *noch* would be the neutral report; *immer noch* adds the five months of impatience, which is the point of the sentence.

</details>

18. Put the words in order to say that you always drink coffee in the morning.

```text
Kaffee / trinke / immer / morgens / ich
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich trinke morgens immer Kaffee.**

**Explanation:** Verb second, then the time expression, then *immer*, then the thing. *Ich trinke immer morgens Kaffee* is also acceptable and shifts the stress slightly onto the time of day.

</details>
