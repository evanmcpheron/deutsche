## Core Idea

*nicht* cancels something. It's the general-purpose "not" — it negates verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and specific named things.

The word itself is easy. **Where it goes in the sentence** is the part that takes practice, so most of this page is about position.

German has a second negation word, *kein*, used with nouns. Which one to use is a real decision every time: see [nicht vs. kein](../comparisons/nicht-vs-kein.md).

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. Negating the whole sentence — *nicht* goes late

**Ich verstehe nicht.**
I don't understand.

**Er kommt heute nicht.**
He isn't coming today.

**Das weiß ich nicht.**
I don't know that.

Notice there's no German equivalent of "do/does/don't." German just adds *nicht*.

### 2. Negating an adjective — *nicht* goes right before it

**Das ist nicht gut.**
That isn't good.

**Die Suppe ist nicht warm.**
The soup isn't warm.

**Das Hotel ist nicht teuer.**
The hotel isn't expensive.

### 3. Negating a specific word or phrase — *nicht* goes right before that word

**Ich fahre nicht nach Berlin.**
I'm not going to Berlin. *(→ somewhere else, maybe)*

**Nicht heute, bitte.**
Not today, please.

**Ich wohne nicht in München.**
I don't live in Munich.

### 4. Negating with a second verb at the end

When the sentence ends with a verb, *nicht* comes **before** that verb.

**Ich kann nicht kommen.**
I can't come.

**Wir wollen heute nicht arbeiten.**
We don't want to work today.

**Der Zug fährt nicht ab.** *(separable verb: abfahren)*
The train isn't departing.

## Position: a working rule of thumb

Put *nicht* **as late as possible** — but **before** any of these, if present:

| Put *nicht* before … | Example |
|---|---|
| an adjective or adverb | Das ist **nicht** billig. |
| a prepositional phrase | Ich gehe **nicht** ins Kino. |
| a verb at the end | Ich kann **nicht** schlafen. |
| a separable prefix | Ich rufe dich **nicht** an. |
| the one word you're contradicting | **Nicht** ich — er! |

That covers the great majority of A1 sentences. German speakers also move *nicht* around for emphasis, and no rule catches every case — this is one of those areas where the pattern is idiomatic rather than fully rule-governed. Copy sentences you've heard rather than deriving each one.

## Common Chunks

- **noch nicht** — not yet → [full page](../patterns/noch-nicht.md)
- **nicht mehr** — not any more → [full page](../patterns/nicht-mehr.md)
- **gar nicht** — not at all → [full page](../patterns/gar-nicht.md)
- **auch nicht** — not … either: *Ich auch nicht.* — Me neither.
- **nicht so** — not that/so: *Nicht so schnell!* — Not so fast!
- **noch nicht mehr?** — not a real chunk; if you're tempted by it, you want either *noch nicht* or *nicht mehr*.

## Pattern Recognition

Read these together and notice where *nicht* lands:

**Ich habe keine Zeit.** *(noun → kein)*
**Ich habe heute nicht viel Zeit.** *(viel Zeit → nicht)*
**Ich komme nicht.**
**Ich komme heute nicht.**
**Ich komme heute nicht mit.**
**Ich kann heute nicht mitkommen.**

The sentence grows, and *nicht* keeps sliding right — but always stops just before the verb material at the end.

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich verstehe das.**
I understand that.

**Ich verstehe das nicht.**
I don't understand that.

And compare the two negations:

**Ich trinke nicht.**
I don't drink. *(as a habit — possibly no alcohol at all)*

**Ich trinke keinen Kaffee.**
I don't drink coffee. *(a specific noun is negated → [kein](kein.md))*

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *nicht* with a noun.** *Ich habe nicht Zeit* → **Ich habe keine Zeit.** If you're negating a noun, you probably want [kein](kein.md).
- **Putting *nicht* right after the subject**, on the English pattern "I not come." *Ich nicht komme* is wrong; it's *Ich komme nicht.*
- **Inventing a helper verb.** There's no German "do" — *Ich tue nicht verstehen* doesn't exist.
- **Forgetting it moves.** *nicht* isn't glued to the verb the way English "not" is glued to "do."

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** You need this from day one, and you need it in the right position. Practice by taking sentences you already know and negating them.

## You May Hear

- **Das geht nicht.** — That's not possible / that won't work.
- **Ich weiß nicht.** — I don't know.
- **Nicht schlecht!** — Not bad!
- **Das macht nichts.** — That doesn't matter. *(nichts = "nothing," a different word — don't mix them up)*
