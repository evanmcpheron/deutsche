## Core Idea

*sehr* turns up the **intensity** of an adjective, adverb, or verb. It never tells you *how much of something* there is — that's *viel*.

> **sehr** = to a high degree (very)
> **viel** = a large quantity (much, a lot)

Getting this wrong is one of the most common early errors, so it has its own page: [sehr vs. viel](../comparisons/sehr-vs-viel.md).

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. With an adjective — "very"

**Das ist sehr gut.**
That's very good.

**Die Suppe ist sehr heiß.**
The soup is very hot.

**Der Bahnhof ist sehr weit.**
The station is very far.

### 2. With an adverb

**Er spricht sehr schnell.**
He speaks very fast.

**Sie singt sehr schön.**
She sings very beautifully.

### 3. With certain verbs — "a lot, very much"

**Das freut mich sehr.**
That makes me very happy.

**Danke sehr!**
Thank you very much.

**Es tut mir sehr leid.**
I'm very sorry.

Here *sehr* follows the verb rather than preceding it.

## Common Chunks

- **danke sehr** — thank you very much
- **bitte sehr** — here you are / you're welcome
- **sehr gut** — very good *(also a school grade: the top one)*
- **sehr gern** — with pleasure, gladly: *Kommst du mit? — Sehr gern!*
- **nicht sehr** — not very: *Das ist nicht sehr teuer.*

## Pattern Recognition

**Das ist sehr schön.**
**Das Hotel ist sehr klein.**
**Ich bin sehr müde.**
**Er arbeitet sehr viel.** ← *sehr* intensifies *viel*, which counts the quantity
**Danke sehr.**

That fourth one is worth staring at: *sehr viel* = "very much / a great deal." The two words do different jobs and stack happily.

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich bin müde.**
I'm tired.

**Ich bin sehr müde.**
I'm very tired.

And the error to avoid:

**Ich habe viel Zeit.** ✓ — I have a lot of time. *(quantity of a noun)*
**Ich habe sehr Zeit.** ✗ — not German.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *sehr* with a noun.** English "very" only attaches to adjectives too, but learners still produce *sehr Geld*. Use *viel*: **viel Geld**.
- **Using *viel* with an adjective.** *Das ist viel gut* → **Das ist sehr gut.**
- **Forgetting *sehr* after verbs.** *Das freut mich sehr* puts *sehr* at the end, not before the verb.

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** Simple, frequent, and low-risk — apart from the *viel* confusion, which is worth fixing early.

## You May Hear

- **Danke sehr.** — Thanks very much.
- **Bitte sehr.** — Here you go.
- **Sehr gern.** — Gladly.
- **Das ist sehr nett von Ihnen.** — That's very kind of you. *(formal)*
