## Mini Review


1. **Der Zug kommt erst um zehn.** What does this mean?\
   a. The train comes before ten.\
   b. The train doesn't come until ten.\
   c. The train is the first one at ten.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** not until ten.

</details>

2. Someone says **Es ist erst sieben Uhr.** What are they suggesting?\
   a. Hurry, it's late.\
   b. Relax, it's early.\
   c. The clock is wrong.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** relax, there's time.

</details>

3. Choose the natural word: *Ich lerne ___ zwei Monate Deutsch.* (only two months so far)\
   a. schon\
   b. erst\
   c. immer

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich lerne erst zwei Monate Deutsch.*

</details>

4. Contrast: **Es ist erst acht Uhr.** / **Es ist schon acht Uhr.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Same time, opposite attitude. *erst acht* = only eight, plenty of time. *schon acht* = eight already, we're behind.

**Explanation:** *erst* frames something as later, less, or earlier in a sequence than another possibility, depending on context.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** The shop opens at nine and it's eight now. How would you tell someone, using *erst*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Das Geschäft öffnet erst um neun.**

**Explanation:** *erst* frames something as later, less, or earlier in a sequence than another possibility, depending on context.

</details>
