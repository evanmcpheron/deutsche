## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Ich bin ___ nicht fertig.*\
   a. schon\
   b. noch\
   c. aus

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich bin noch nicht fertig.*

</details>

2. **Ich bin noch hier.** What is the overall situation?\
   a. I arrived a moment ago.\
   b. I haven't left yet.\
   c. I'm coming later.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** I haven't left yet. The situation is continuing.

</details>

3. In a café, the waiter asks **Noch etwas?** What is he doing?\
   a. Asking if you want anything else.\
   b. Asking if you're still waiting.\
   c. Asking whether you've paid.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** anything else? It's the standard shop and café question.

</details>

4. What's the difference between **Ich möchte einen Kaffee** and **Ich möchte noch einen Kaffee**?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Without *noch*, you're ordering a coffee

**Explanation:** presumably your first. With *noch*, you've already had one and want another.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm not finished yet" in German, using *noch*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin noch nicht fertig.**

**Explanation:** *noch* commonly marks something continuing, something not yet complete, or one more of something, depending on the pattern.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** You want one more beer. How would you ask for it politely, using *noch*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Noch ein Bier, bitte.** (Or *Ich möchte noch ein Bier, bitte.*)

**Explanation:** *noch* commonly marks something continuing, something not yet complete, or one more of something, depending on the pattern.

</details>
