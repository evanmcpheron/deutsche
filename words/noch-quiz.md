# Quiz: noch

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. It is ten at night. Anna started work at eight in the morning and has not left the office once.\
   a. Anna arbeitet schon.\
   b. Anna arbeitet wieder.\
   c. Anna arbeitet noch.\
   d. Anna arbeitet nicht mehr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The work started and has never stopped, so the situation is still running: *Anna arbeitet noch.* *Wieder* would mean she stopped and started a second time, and *nicht mehr* would mean she has finished.

</details>

2. Im Café. Der Kellner fragt: **Noch etwas?** Was möchte er wissen?\
   a. Ob du noch wartest.\
   b. Ob du etwas bestellen möchtest.\
   c. Ob du schon bezahlt hast.\
   d. Ob du noch Zeit hast.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Noch etwas?* is the standard shop and café question: you have ordered something, and he is offering one more item. This is *noch* as "an additional amount," not *noch* as "still."

</details>

3. Look at the times:

```text
18:00 — Der Supermarkt ist offen.
19:00 — Der Supermarkt ist offen.
20:00 — Der Supermarkt schließt.
```

   Es ist 19:30. Welcher Satz passt?\
   a. Der Supermarkt ist schon zu.\
   b. Der Supermarkt ist wieder offen.\
   c. Der Supermarkt ist noch offen.\
   d. Der Supermarkt ist nicht mehr offen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** At 19:30 the opening hours have not run out yet — that is exactly what *noch* points at. Both *schon zu* and *nicht mehr offen* would put you half an hour into the future.

</details>

4. You arrive late at a small hotel without a booking and want to know whether any rooms are available.\
   a. Haben Sie schon Zimmer frei?\
   b. Haben Sie noch Zimmer frei?\
   c. Haben Sie wieder Zimmer frei?\
   d. Haben Sie nur Zimmer frei?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** You are asking whether the supply of rooms is used up — a classic *noch* question, and one you will hear constantly at receptions. *Schon* would ask whether rooms became free earlier than expected, which is a different question.

</details>

5. **Wir haben noch zehn Minuten.** What does the speaker mean?\
   a. We have to leave in ten minutes.\
   b. We have been waiting for ten minutes.\
   c. We are ten minutes late.\
   d. We worked for ten minutes.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** This is *noch* for a remaining amount — ten minutes are left before the time runs out. Option b would be *Wir warten schon zehn Minuten*, where *schon* measures time already spent rather than time left.

</details>

6. In which sentence does *noch* **not** describe a situation that is continuing?\
   a. Er schläft noch.\
   b. Das Restaurant ist noch offen.\
   c. Ich bin noch hier.\
   d. Möchtest du noch einen Tee?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** In d, *noch* means "one more" — an additional tea after the first. The other three describe a state that has not ended. All four are the same underlying idea ("hasn't run out"), but only d is about quantity rather than duration.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

7. What is the difference between **Ich möchte einen Kaffee** and **Ich möchte noch einen Kaffee**?\
   a. Der zweite Satz ist höflicher.\
   b. Der zweite Satz ist eine Frage.\
   c. Beim zweiten Satz hatte der Sprecher schon einen Kaffee.\
   d. Beim zweiten Satz bestellt der Sprecher für jemand anderen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch ein…* means "another one" and assumes there was a first. *noch* does not make a request more polite — that job belongs to *bitte* and to *möchte* itself.

</details>

8. Paul was ill on Monday. By Wednesday he had recovered and went back to work. On Friday he wakes up ill.\
   a. Paul ist noch krank.\
   b. Paul ist wieder krank.\
   c. Paul ist immer noch krank.\
   d. Paul ist schon krank.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The illness stopped and came back, which is *wieder*. Both *noch* and *immer noch* would claim he never got better in between — and *immer noch* would add impatience on top.

</details>

9. You are standing on the platform. Your train is delayed, the track is empty, and the display still lists it as expected.\
   a. Der Zug ist noch nicht da.\
   b. Der Zug ist nicht mehr da.\
   c. Der Zug ist wieder da.\
   d. Der Zug ist schon da.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* = it hasn't arrived yet, and it is still expected. *nicht mehr* would mean it had been there and has now gone — the opposite direction in time.

</details>

10. Someone opens the fridge and asks: **Ist noch Milch da?**\
   a. Whether there is any milk left.\
   b. Whether the milk is still good.\
   c. Whether someone should buy milk.\
   d. Whether the milk has arrived.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch* points at a supply that may not be used up. Nothing in the sentence is about the quality or age of the milk — that would need a different word altogether.

</details>

11. Compare **Er schläft noch** with **Er schläft immer noch.** What does *immer* add?\
   a. Es ist die höflichere Form.\
   b. Der Sprecher findet es überraschend oder zu lang.\
   c. Er schläft jeden Tag sehr lange.\
   d. Er ist gerade eingeschlafen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Plain *noch* states a fact. *immer noch* comments on it — surprise, or mild impatience that it has gone on this long. Option c is the trap: *immer* here does not mean "every day."

</details>

12. You want to say: **I don't have tickets yet.** A learner writes **Ich habe keine Tickets mehr.** How would you judge that sentence?\
   a. Richtig — genau diese Bedeutung.\
   b. Falsches Deutsch.\
   c. Richtig, aber unhöflich.\
   d. Korrektes Deutsch, aber es bedeutet etwas anderes.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The sentence is perfectly good German — it means the tickets are used up or sold out. For "not yet," the tickets are still ahead of you: **Ich habe noch keine Tickets.**

</details>

13. Which word order is correct?\
   a. Ich bin noch nicht fertig.\
   b. Ich bin nicht noch fertig.\
   c. Ich bin fertig noch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *noch nicht* is a fixed pair in that order, and it sits before the adjective. *nicht noch* is not the negative of *noch* — reversing the two words doesn't reverse the meaning, it just breaks the sentence.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Judgement

14. Complete the dialogue. B moved away from Hamburg last year.

```text
A: Wohnst du noch in Hamburg?
B: ___
```

   a. Ja, noch.\
   b. Ja, schon lange.\
   c. Nein, noch nicht.\
   d. Nein, nicht mehr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *nicht mehr* answers a *noch* question in the negative: it was true, and it has stopped being true. *noch nicht* would mean he hasn't moved to Hamburg yet — the situation reversed.

</details>

15. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Bist du fertig? Das Taxi wartet.
B: Nein, ich brauche ___ fünf Minuten.
```

   a. schon\
   b. wieder\
   c. noch\
   d. erst

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Five minutes are outstanding — the time hasn't been used yet. *erst* would comment that five minutes is a short time so far, which is not what someone keeping a taxi waiting wants to say.

</details>

16. A learner wants to say "I haven't eaten yet" and writes **Ich habe nicht noch gegessen.** What went wrong?\
   a. Die Wörter stehen in der falschen Reihenfolge — es muss *noch nicht* heißen.\
   b. Man braucht hier *kein* statt *nicht*.\
   c. *noch* passt nicht; richtig wäre *schon nicht*.\
   d. Das Verb steht an der falschen Stelle.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Correct: **Ich habe noch nicht gegessen.** The order is fixed. And *schon nicht* is not the negative of *schon* either — when you negate one of these, you reach for *noch nicht*.

</details>

17. On the phone, after a long silence, someone says: **Sind Sie noch da?** What is the speaker worried about?\
   a. Dass die Person gerade erst angekommen ist.\
   b. Dass die Verbindung weg ist.\
   c. Dass die Person zu früh gekommen ist.\
   d. Dass die Person noch einmal anrufen soll.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *noch da* = still there, hasn't gone. On the phone that means the line: the speaker assumes you were there and is checking whether that is still true.

</details>

18. At the till, your shopping doesn't fit in one bag. What is the most natural way to ask for a second one?\
   a. Ich will wieder eine Tüte.\
   b. Ich möchte eine Tüte mehr.\
   c. Kann ich noch eine Tüte haben?\
   d. Habe ich noch eine Tüte?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch eine* is the everyday way to ask for one more of something. Option b is understandable but stiff; option a would suggest you had a bag, lost it, and want the same one back.

</details>

19. Read the sequence:

```text
Montag: Lena ist in Berlin.
Dienstag: Lena ist in Berlin.
Mittwoch: Lena ist in Berlin.
```

   Am Mittwochabend schreibt sie ihrem Mann. Welcher Satz passt?\
   a. Ich bin schon in Berlin.\
   b. Ich bin wieder in Berlin.\
   c. Ich bin erst in Berlin.\
   d. Ich bin noch in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** One unbroken stay, still going on Wednesday. *schon* would fit the moment she arrived; *wieder* would need her to have left and returned.

</details>

20. **Ich war noch nie in Wien.** Was stimmt?\
   a. Er war letztes Jahr in Wien.\
   b. Er ist gerade in Wien.\
   c. Er hat Wien bis heute nicht besucht.\
   d. Er fährt bald nach Wien.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *noch nie* = never so far. The *noch* leaves the door open — up to now it hasn't happened — but the sentence says nothing about future plans, so d is an assumption rather than a reading.

</details>

21. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does *noch* say that something has **not been used up**?\
   a. Wir haben noch Brot.\
   b. Hast du noch Zeit?\
   c. Ich komme noch mal.\
   d. Ist noch Platz im Auto?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** Bread, time and space are all supplies that could run out, and *noch* says they haven't. In c, *noch mal* is the chunk meaning "one more time / again" — a repeated event rather than a remaining amount.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

22. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   It is eight in the evening. A friend calls and asks whether you have left work. You are at your desk.

```text
Ich bin ___ im Büro.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **noch**

**Explanation:** *Ich bin noch im Büro.* You are there, you haven't left, and the caller probably expected otherwise — that expectation is exactly what *noch* carries.

</details>

23. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   In a restaurant. Your glass is empty and you would like a second glass of wine.

```text
___ ein Glas Wein, bitte.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Noch**

**Explanation:** *Noch ein Glas Wein, bitte.* The fuller *Ich möchte noch ein Glas Wein, bitte* works too. Note that it is *ein Glas*, not *einen* — *Glas* is neuter, even though *Wein* is masculine.

</details>

24. Put the words in order to ask a receptionist whether any rooms are available.

```text
Zimmer / noch / haben / frei / Sie
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Haben Sie noch Zimmer frei?**

**Explanation:** The verb comes first in a yes/no question, and *noch* sits before the thing that might have run out. *Haben Sie Zimmer frei?* is also correct German — it just drops the "any left" flavour.

</details>

25. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Er ist noch krank.**\
   New situation: he recovered, went back to work for a week, and has now fallen ill a second time.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Er ist wieder krank.**

**Explanation:** Change only the small word. *noch* claims one unbroken illness; *wieder* says it ended and returned. If you wanted to stress how long a single illness had lasted, *Er ist immer noch krank* would be the sentence — but that isn't this situation.

</details>

26. **Produce it:** You are at the bakery. You have asked for a loaf of bread, and the assistant says *Noch etwas?* You would also like two rolls.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ja, noch zwei Brötchen, bitte.**

**Explanation:** *noch zwei…* = two more, on top of what you have already ordered. *Ja, zwei Brötchen, bitte* is fine too; keeping *noch* just makes the link to the first order explicit, which is what a German speaker would normally do here.

</details>
