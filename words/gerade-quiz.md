# Quiz: gerade

[← Back to **gerade**](gerade.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

---

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. Your phone rings while you are standing over a hot pan. You want the caller to know this isn't a good moment.\
   a. Ich koche.\
   b. Ich bin kochen.\
   c. Ich koche schon.\
   d. Ich koche gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** German has no "am -ing" form, so *gerade* does that job. Option a could just as easily mean "I cook" as a general statement, and option b copies English into a sentence German doesn't have.

</details>

2. **Sie ist gerade gegangen.** When did she leave?\
   a. Sie geht in einer Minute.\
   b. Vor einer Minute.\
   c. Vor zwei Wochen.\
   d. Sie ist noch hier.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** With a past form, *gerade* means "just" — a moment ago. The word always points at the present moment; here it says the leaving happened right on the edge of it.

</details>

3. You ask a stranger the way and he says **Immer geradeaus.** What should you do?\
   a. Schneller gehen.\
   b. Weiter in dieselbe Richtung gehen.\
   c. Links abbiegen.\
   d. Warten.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *geradeaus* (one word) is "straight ahead," and *immer geradeaus* means keep going that way. The *immer* is about the route, not about hurrying.

</details>

4. Complete the dialogue. A wants to know what B is doing at this moment.

```text
A: Was machst du ___?
B: Ich lese.
```

   a. schon\
   b. wieder\
   c. gerade\
   d. immer

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Was machst du gerade?* is the everyday "what are you up to right now?" — very common in messages. *Was machst du immer?* would ask about habits instead.

</details>

5. A learner writes **Ich bin arbeiten**, meaning "I'm working right now." What is wrong?\
   a. *arbeiten* must be at the beginning of the sentence.\
   b. You need *kein* instead of *bin*.\
   c. You need *werden* instead of *sein*.\
   d. German has no *-ing* form — the correct sentence is *Ich arbeite gerade.*

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** There is no German equivalent of "am -ing." Use the plain present tense and add *gerade* when you need to pin it to this moment.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

6. What is the difference between **Ich arbeite** and **Ich arbeite gerade**?\
   a. The second sentence is more polite.\
   b. The second sentence is a question.\
   c. Both say exactly the same thing.\
   d. The first can describe your occupation; the second focuses on the current moment.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** On the phone this matters a lot: *Ich arbeite* tells someone what you do for a living, *Ich arbeite gerade* tells them to call back later.

</details>

7. You reach the platform half a minute after the train pulled out.\
   a. Der Zug ist gerade abgefahren.\
   b. Der Zug fährt gerade ab.\
   c. Der Zug ist noch nicht abgefahren.\
   d. Der Zug fährt erst ab.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The departure is finished, and only just — that's *gerade* with a past form. Option b would fit standing on the platform watching it move.

</details>

8. **Er ist gerade nicht da.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. Er kommt nie.\
   b. Er war noch nie da.\
   c. Im Moment ist er weg.\
   d. Er ist da, aber er schläft.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *gerade nicht da* is the standard office phrase for "not at his desk just now" — and it quietly implies he will be back. Without *gerade* the sentence would sound much more final.

</details>

9. **Das ist nicht gerade billig.** What does the speaker mean?\
   a. Es ist eher teuer.\
   b. Es ist sehr billig.\
   c. Der Preis ist genau richtig.\
   d. Der Preis ist unbekannt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *nicht gerade* = "not exactly," a polite way of saying the opposite. It's a set phrase and has nothing to do with the present moment.

</details>

10. You walked into the party sixty seconds ago and someone asks how long you've been there.\
   a. Ich bin gerade nicht da.\
   b. Ich bin schon lange hier.\
   c. Ich komme gleich.\
   d. Ich bin gerade erst angekommen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *gerade erst* stacks two ideas — "only just" — and is the natural way to say you've barely arrived. Both words are pushing in the same direction: very little time has passed.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

11. What is the difference between *jetzt* and *gerade*?\
   a. *jetzt* is more polite.\
   b. Both words are the same.\
   c. *gerade* focuses on this exact moment, often in the middle of an action.\
   d. *gerade* always refers to the future.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *jetzt* is "now" in a broader sense and can even mean "from now on." *gerade* zooms in on the exact moment, usually with something already in progress.

</details>

12. Which sentence tells someone what you do for a living?\
   a. Ich arbeite gerade.\
   b. Ich arbeite bei einer Bank.\
   c. Ich arbeite gerade nicht.\
   d. Ich arbeite gerade jetzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Every option containing *gerade* is about this moment rather than your career. Option c is the useful phrase for "I'm not working at the moment."

</details>

13. You want to say: **She's on the phone right now.** A learner writes **Sie hat gerade telefoniert.**\
   a. Correct German, but it means something different.\
   b. Correct — exactly that meaning.\
   c. Incorrect German.\
   d. Correct, but impolite.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The sentence is fine, but the past form makes it "she was on the phone a moment ago." For the call happening now: **Sie telefoniert gerade.**

</details>

14. In which sentence does *gerade* **not** pin something to this exact moment?\
   a. Der Weg ist gerade.\
   b. Wir essen gerade.\
   c. Er telefoniert gerade.\
   d. Ich arbeite gerade.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Here *gerade* is the adjective "straight" — the same word doing an entirely different job. It's the source of *geradeaus* in directions.

</details>

15. You are halfway through dinner when a friend calls. Which reply fits?\
   a. Ich esse schon — ich rufe später an.\
   b. Ich esse gerade — ich rufe später an.\
   c. Ich esse immer — ich rufe später an.\
   d. Ich esse wieder — ich rufe später an.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Only *gerade* says you are in the middle of it. *schon* would mean you've started earlier than expected, and *immer* would claim you're permanently eating.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

16. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does *gerade* mean "at this moment," rather than "a moment ago" or "straight"?\
   a. Ich koche gerade.\
   b. Sie ist gerade gegangen.\
   c. Er ist gerade nicht da.\
   d. Gehen Sie geradeaus.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c**

**Explanation:** Both describe the present state of affairs. In b the past form pushes *gerade* back to "just now," and in d the word has become part of *geradeaus*, the direction.

</details>

17. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A friend calls while you are driving on the motorway.

```text
Ich fahre ___ — ich rufe dich zurück.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **gerade**

**Explanation:** *Ich fahre gerade* makes it clear you're mid-journey. Plain *Ich fahre* would sound more like a general statement about how you travel.

</details>

18. Put the words in order to ask what someone is doing right now.

```text
du / machst / gerade / was
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Was machst du gerade?**

**Explanation:** Question word first, verb second, then subject, then *gerade* near the end. This is worth memorizing whole — it's one of the most-sent messages in German.

</details>
