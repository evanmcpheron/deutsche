# immer

**Type:** Adverb (frequency)
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

## Core Idea

*immer* = **always, every time, without exception.** This is one of the rare small words where the English equivalent really does hold up.

What's worth learning here is less the word itself than the **chunks** it forms — especially *immer noch* and *immer wieder*, which don't mean "always" at all.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Always" — habit

**Ich trinke morgens immer Kaffee.**
I always drink coffee in the morning.

**Der Bus kommt immer zu spät.**
The bus is always late.

**Sie ist immer freundlich.**
She's always friendly.

### 2. "Still, to this day" — in the chunk *immer noch*

**Er wartet immer noch.**
He's *still* waiting. *(and that's a long time)*

→ [immer noch](../patterns/immer-noch.md)

### 3. "Over and over" — in the chunk *immer wieder*

**Ich mache immer wieder denselben Fehler.**
I keep making the same mistake.

**Er kommt immer wieder zu spät.**
He's late again and again.

### 4. "Ever more" — with comparatives *(A2 — recognize)*

**Es wird immer kälter.**
It's getting colder and colder.

## Common Chunks

- **immer noch** — still, even now *(with impatience or surprise)* → [full page](../patterns/immer-noch.md)
- **immer wieder** — again and again, repeatedly
- **fast immer** — nearly always
- **wie immer** — as always: *Wie immer, bitte.* — The usual, please.
- **immer mehr** — more and more
- **nicht immer** — not always

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich gehe immer um acht ins Bett.**
**Wir essen immer zusammen.**
**Wie immer!**
**Er ist immer noch krank.** ← still, after all this time
**Sie ruft immer wieder an.** ← repeatedly

Notice how *noch* and *wieder* pull *immer* away from "always" and toward "still" and "repeatedly."

## What Changes Without It?

**Der Bus kommt zu spät.**
The bus is late. *(today)*

**Der Bus kommt immer zu spät.**
The bus is always late. *(a complaint about the bus in general)*

And the chunk contrast:

**Er ist noch krank.** — He's still ill. *(neutral)*
**Er ist immer noch krank.** — He's *still* ill. *(this is dragging on)*

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Reading *immer noch* as "always still."** It's one unit meaning "still, even now," usually with a note of impatience.
- **Reading *immer wieder* as "always again."** It means "over and over."
- **Word-order drift.** *immer* usually sits after the verb and subject: *Ich trinke immer Kaffee*, not *Immer ich trinke Kaffee*. Starting a sentence with *Immer* is possible but emphatic.

## Active or Passive?

**Active** for the plain "always" use, and for *immer noch*, which you'll want constantly. **Recognition First** for the *immer + comparative* pattern.

## You May Hear

- **Wie immer?** — The usual? *(in a café or bar where they know you)*
- **Immer geradeaus.** — Keep going straight. *(directions)*
- **Das sagst du immer.** — You always say that.
- **Er ist immer noch nicht da.** — He's still not here.

## Mini Review

1. Choose the natural word: *Ich stehe ___ um sechs Uhr auf.* (always)
   a. immer
   b. noch
   c. schon

2. **Er wartet immer noch.** What's the situation?
   a. He waits regularly.
   b. He's been waiting a long time and hasn't stopped.
   c. He waited once before.

3. What does **immer wieder** mean?
   a. always again
   b. over and over
   c. still

4. Contrast: **Er ist noch krank.** / **Er ist immer noch krank.**

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "the bus is always late" in German?

6. **Produce it:** How would you say "I always drink tea in the morning"?
