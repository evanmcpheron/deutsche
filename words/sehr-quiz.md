# Quiz: sehr

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

---

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. You take a spoonful of soup and it burns your mouth.\
   a. Die Suppe ist sehr heiß.\
   b. Die Suppe ist viel heiß.\
   c. Die Suppe ist sehr Hitze.\
   d. Die Suppe hat sehr heiß.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *heiß* is an adjective, so it takes *sehr*. Option b makes the classic swap — *viel* counts quantities, it doesn't turn up intensity.

</details>

2. You have a large amount of money in your account.\
   a. Ich habe sehr Geld.\
   b. Ich habe sehr viel Geld nicht.\
   c. Ich habe sehr Geld viel.\
   d. Ich habe viel Geld.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Geld* is a noun, and nouns take *viel*. If you wanted to stress it further you could say *sehr viel Geld* — but *sehr Geld* on its own is not German.

</details>

3. A shop assistant hands you your change and says **Bitte sehr.**\
   a. Er bittet um Geld.\
   b. Er entschuldigt sich.\
   c. Er gibt dir etwas — "here you are".\
   d. Er fragt nach dem Preis.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Bitte sehr* is a set phrase for handing something over, and also for "you're welcome." It has nothing to do with asking for anything.

</details>

4. Complete the dialogue. B would love to come.

```text
A: Kommst du mit ins Kino?
B: ___!
```

   a. Sehr gut\
   b. Sehr gern\
   c. Danke sehr\
   d. Bitte sehr

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Sehr gern* = "with pleasure, gladly" — the standard enthusiastic yes to an invitation. *Sehr gut* would judge the film rather than accept the invitation.

</details>

5. **Er arbeitet sehr viel.** What are the two words doing?\
   a. *sehr* counts the amount; *viel* intensifies.\
   b. Both words do the same job.\
   c. *viel* expresses the amount; *sehr* intensifies it.\
   d. The sentence is incorrect — the two words cannot be combined.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The two words do different jobs and stack happily: *viel* measures the amount of work, and *sehr* pushes that amount higher. *sehr viel* = "a great deal."

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

6. You want to say: **I have a lot of work.** A learner writes **Ich habe sehr Arbeit.**\
   a. Correct — exactly that meaning.\
   b. Incorrect — before a noun you need *viel*: *viel Arbeit*.\
   c. Correct German, but it means something different.\
   d. Correct, but impolite.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *sehr* never attaches to a noun. English "very" behaves the same way, which is why this error is so easy to spot once you look for the noun.

</details>

7. A learner writes **Das ist viel gut.** What is the fix?\
   a. Das ist sehr gut.\
   b. Das ist viele gut.\
   c. Das ist gut viel.\
   d. Das hat sehr gut.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** This is the error in the opposite direction — *viel* in front of an adjective. Adjectives take *sehr*, every time.

</details>

8. Which sentence puts *sehr* in the right place?\
   a. Das sehr freut mich.\
   b. Sehr das freut mich.\
   c. Das freut mich sehr.\
   d. Das freut sehr mich.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** With verbs like *freuen*, *sehr* comes after the verb and its object rather than in front. Compare *Es tut mir sehr leid*, which follows the same shape.

</details>

9. **Das ist nicht sehr teuer.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. The price is reasonable.\
   b. The price is very high.\
   c. It costs nothing.\
   d. The price is unknown.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *nicht sehr* turns the intensity down rather than reversing the adjective — "not very expensive," so affordable. It doesn't claim the thing is free.

</details>

10. You want to thank a shop assistant warmly as you leave.\
   a. Sehr danke.\
   b. Viel danke.\
   c. Danke viel.\
   d. Danke sehr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Danke sehr* is fixed in that order, with *sehr* second. *Vielen Dank* is the other everyday option — note that it uses a form of *viel*, not *sehr*.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

11. In which sentence is *sehr* **not** intensifying an adjective?\
   a. Ich bin sehr müde.\
   b. Das Hotel ist sehr klein.\
   c. Der Bahnhof ist sehr weit.\
   d. Es tut mir sehr leid.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Es tut mir leid* is a verb phrase, and *sehr* strengthens the whole expression from behind. The other three have a plain adjective sitting right after *sehr*.

</details>

12. Complete the dialogue. B enjoyed the film a lot.

```text
A: Wie war der Film?
B: ___ schön.
```

   a. Viel\
   b. Nur\
   c. Sehr\
   d. Immer

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *schön* is an adjective, so only *sehr* works here. Option b would play the film down rather than praise it.

</details>

13. Which pair is correct?\
   a. viel Geld / sehr gut\
   b. sehr Geld / viel gut\
   c. viel Geld / viel gut\
   d. sehr Geld / sehr gut

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Noun → *viel*; adjective → *sehr*. Getting this one pair straight fixes most of the confusion between the two words.

</details>

14. **Sie singt sehr schön.** What does *sehr* intensify here?\
   a. A noun.\
   b. An adverb.\
   c. A verb by itself.\
   d. A number.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *schön* here describes how she sings, so it is working as an adverb — and *sehr* intensifies adverbs just as readily as adjectives.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

15. **Select all that apply.** In which gaps does *sehr* belong rather than *viel*?\
   a. Das ist ___ interessant.\
   b. Ich habe ___ Geld.\
   c. Er spricht ___ schnell.\
   d. Wir haben ___ Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c**

**Explanation:** An adjective and an adverb, both *sehr*. Options b and d have nouns in the gap, so they need *viel Geld* and *viel Zeit*.

</details>

16. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You have been walking all day and can barely keep your eyes open.

```text
Ich bin ___ müde.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **sehr**

**Explanation:** *müde* is an adjective, so the intensifier is *sehr*. *Ich bin viel müde* is one of the most common early errors and is not German.

</details>

17. Correct this sentence: **Ich habe sehr Zeit.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe viel Zeit.**

**Explanation:** *Zeit* is a noun, so the quantity word *viel* is what you need. If you want to lay it on, *Ich habe sehr viel Zeit* is natural — *sehr* can intensify *viel*, but it can't replace it.

</details>
