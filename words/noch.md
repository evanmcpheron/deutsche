# noch

**Type:** Adverb / particle
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — very high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

[← auch](auch.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [Next → schon](schon.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

*noch* points at something that **hasn't run out yet**.

That one idea covers almost every A1 use. A situation that hasn't ended yet → "still." A result that hasn't arrived yet → "not yet." A supply that could take one more → "another." Time that hasn't been used up → "any time left."

Notice that these are not four unrelated dictionary meanings. They're one idea seen in four situations. English happens to use four different words for it, which is an English problem, not a German one.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. Continuing situation — "still"

**Ich bin noch hier.**
I'm still here.

**Er schläft noch.**
He's still asleep.

**Das Restaurant ist noch offen.**
The restaurant is still open.

### 2. With *nicht* or *kein* — "not yet"

**Ich bin noch nicht fertig.**
I'm not finished yet.

**Der Zug ist noch nicht da.**
The train isn't here yet.

**Wir haben noch keine Tickets.**
We don't have tickets yet.

The "yet" comes from *noch*: it says the thing is still pending, and implies it's expected. → [noch nicht](../patterns/noch-nicht.md)

### 3. An additional amount — "another, more"

**Noch einen Kaffee, bitte.**
Another coffee, please.

**Möchtest du noch etwas?**
Would you like anything else?

**Wir brauchen noch zwei Stühle.**
We need two more chairs.

→ [noch ein …](../patterns/noch-ein.md)

### 4. Remaining amount — "left"

**Hast du noch Zeit?**
Do you still have time? / Have you got any time left?

**Wir haben noch zehn Minuten.**
We have ten minutes left.

**Ist noch Milch da?**
Is there any milk left?

## Common Chunks

- **noch nicht** — not yet → [full page](../patterns/noch-nicht.md)
- **noch einmal** / **noch mal** — one more time, again: *Sag das noch mal.* — Say that again.
- **noch ein …** — another → [full page](../patterns/noch-ein.md)
- **immer noch** — still, even now (with a hint of "longer than expected") → [full page](../patterns/immer-noch.md)
- **noch etwas** — something else: *Noch etwas?* — Anything else?
- **noch nie** — never (yet): *Ich war noch nie in Berlin.* — I've never been to Berlin.

## Pattern Recognition

Read these as a group. The same word, the same underlying idea, four situations:

**Ich bin noch hier.** — not gone yet
**Ich bin noch nicht fertig.** — not finished yet
**Noch einen Kaffee, bitte.** — the coffee isn't finished either
**Hast du noch Zeit?** — the time isn't used up yet
**Ist noch Brot da?** — the bread isn't gone yet

Every one of them is "hasn't run out."

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich bin hier.**
I am here.

**Ich bin noch hier.**
I'm still here. *(you might have expected me to have left)*

The second sentence carries an expectation the first one doesn't. That's typical of these small words: they don't add facts, they add a **relationship between the fact and what was expected**.

Another pair:

**Ich möchte einen Kaffee.**
I'd like a coffee. *(a first one)*

**Ich möchte noch einen Kaffee.**
I'd like another coffee. *(there was already one)*

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Fixing it as "still."** *Noch einen Kaffee* is not "still a coffee." Let the situation choose the English, not the other way round.
- **Reaching for *immer noch* every time.** *immer noch* adds impatience or surprise. Plain *noch* is the neutral one — *Er schläft noch* is a fact; *Er schläft immer noch* is a comment.
- **Confusing it with [schon](schon.md).** *noch* looks backward at something continuing; *schon* looks at something that has already begun. See [noch vs. schon](../comparisons/noch-vs-schon.md).
- **Confusing it with [wieder](wieder.md).** *noch* means it never stopped; *wieder* means it stopped and started again. See [noch vs. wieder](../comparisons/noch-vs-wieder.md).
- **Expecting "not yet" to be one word.** It's the pair *noch nicht* — and word order matters: *noch nicht*, never *nicht noch*.

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** *noch* is one of the highest-value words on this whole list. Aim to produce at least: *noch nicht*, *noch ein…*, and *Ich bin noch hier*-type sentences.

## You May Hear

- **Noch etwas?** — Anything else? *(shops, cafés, bakeries — constantly)*
- **Sind Sie noch da?** — Are you still there? *(on the phone)*
- **Noch einen?** — Another one?
- **Haben Sie noch Zimmer frei?** — Do you still have rooms available?
- **Einen Moment noch, bitte.** — Just a moment more, please.
