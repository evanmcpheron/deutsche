## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Der Laden ist ___ offen.* (open again, after being closed)\
   a. noch\
   b. wieder\
   c. schon

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Der Laden ist wieder offen.*

</details>

2. **Er ist wieder krank.** What happened in between?\
   a. Nothing — he never got better.\
   b. He recovered, and is now ill again.\
   c. He's about to get ill.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** he recovered and has now relapsed.

</details>

3. Someone says **Schon wieder!** What's their mood?\
   a. Pleased\
   b. Neutral\
   c. Annoyed

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** annoyed. *schon wieder* carries irritation.

</details>

4. Contrast: **Er ist noch krank.** / **Er ist wieder krank.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *noch krank* = still ill, continuously. *wieder krank* = ill again, after being well.

**Explanation:** *wieder* means that something happens again after an interruption or earlier occurrence.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm back" in German, using *wieder*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin wieder da.**

**Explanation:** *wieder* means that something happens again after an interruption or earlier occurrence.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'll come again tomorrow"?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Ich komme morgen wieder.**

**Explanation:** *wieder* means that something happens again after an interruption or earlier occurrence.

</details>
