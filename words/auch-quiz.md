## Mini Review


1. Someone says *Ich habe keinen Hunger*. Which reply means "me neither"?\
   a. Ich auch.\
   b. Ich auch nicht.\
   c. Ich nicht auch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch nicht.*

</details>

2. What's the difference between **Ich trinke auch Kaffee** and **Auch ich trinke Kaffee**?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** The first adds coffee to the list of things *I* drink, or adds me to the list of coffee drinkers

**Explanation:** it's the neutral, everyday version. The second puts the emphasis firmly on *me* ("I, too, drink coffee") and sounds more marked or formal.

</details>

3. Choose the natural word: *Wir fahren ___ nach München.* (we're going too)\
   a. auch\
   b. noch\
   c. schon

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Wir fahren auch nach München.*

</details>

4. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'd like a coffee too" in German, using *auch*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Ich möchte auch einen Kaffee.**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds the sense of “also” or “too,” including another person, thing, or action.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** Someone says they're tired. How would you say "me too"?

<summary>Show solution</summary>

</details>
