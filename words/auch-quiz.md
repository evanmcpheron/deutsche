# Quiz: auch

[← Back to **auch**](auch.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

---

## Part A — Read the Situation

1. Your friend tells you she is going to the cinema this evening. You are going too.\
   a. Ich gehe auch ins Kino.\
   b. Ich gehe ins Kino auch.\
   c. Ich gehe noch ins Kino.\
   d. Auch gehe ich ins Kino.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *auch* stands directly in front of what is being added — here the cinema trip. Option b puts it at the end on the English pattern, which German doesn't do.

</details>

2. Complete the dialogue. B feels the same way.

```text
A: Ich habe Hunger.
B: ___
```

   a. Ich noch.\
   b. Auch ich.\
   c. Ich auch.\
   d. Ich auch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch* is a complete two-word answer and one of the most useful replies in German. Option b exists but is emphatic and slightly formal — it would draw attention to *you* rather than simply agreeing.

</details>

3. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Ich habe keinen Durst.
B: ___
```

   a. Ich auch.\
   b. Ich auch nicht.\
   c. Ich nicht auch.\
   d. Ich noch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** When you agree with a negative, German adds *nicht* after *auch* — in that order. *Ich auch* would agree with a positive statement, which A's sentence is not.

</details>

4. **Ich trinke auch Kaffee.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. Kaffee ist das Einzige, was ich trinke.\
   b. Ich trinke keinen Kaffee.\
   c. Ich trinke jeden Tag Kaffee.\
   d. Kaffee kommt zu etwas anderem dazu.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds — either to what other people drink, or to the other drinks I have. Option a is the *nur* sentence: *Ich trinke nur Kaffee.*

</details>

5. **Auch ich trinke Kaffee.** What does this sentence emphasize?\
   a. The drink.\
   b. The amount.\
   c. The person.\
   d. The time.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Whatever comes right after *auch* is what gets added — and here that's *ich*. Compare question 4: same words, and the emphasis moves with the particle.

</details>

6. In which sentence is *auch* **not** adding someone or something?\
   a. Wir haben auch Tee.\
   b. Du bist auch immer müde.\
   c. Ich komme auch mit.\
   d. Das weiß ich auch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** In b, *auch* is a light emphasis particle — roughly "you're always tired, aren't you." Nothing is being added to a list. Recognize this use; you don't need to produce it.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

7. You want to say: **Me neither.** A learner writes **Ich nicht auch.**\
   a. Incorrect — the normal order is *auch nicht*.\
   b. Correct — this expresses exactly that meaning.\
   c. Grammatically possible, but it means something different.\
   d. Correct, but impolite.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The pair is fixed: **Ich auch nicht.** Reversing the two words doesn't produce a different meaning — it produces a sentence German speakers don't say.

</details>

8. Two friends sit down. Nobody has ordered yet. Anna says *Ich nehme einen Tee.* Ben wants tea as well.\
   a. Ich nehme noch einen Tee.\
   b. Ich nehme nur einen Tee.\
   c. Ich nehme wieder einen Tee.\
   d. Ich nehme auch einen Tee.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *auch* adds a new person to what is already true. Option a would mean a second tea for Ben himself, which doesn't fit — he hasn't had a first one.

</details>

9. **Auch gut.** What does this mean?\
   a. That is very good.\
   b. That is not good.\
   c. That works for me too.\
   d. That turned out well.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Auch gut* accepts an alternative — "that works too, fine by me." It is agreement rather than praise, so option a overshoots.

</details>

10. A learner writes **Ich fahre nach Berlin auch.** What is wrong?\
   a. *nach* is incorrect here.\
   b. *auch* is at the end of the sentence — it should come before *nach Berlin*.\
   c. *noch* is needed instead of *auch*.\
   d. The verb is in the wrong position.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Correct: **Ich fahre auch nach Berlin.** Short sentences like *Ich komme auch* hide the problem because *auch* happens to land at the end anyway — in longer sentences the position matters.

</details>

11. Compare **Ich komme** and **Ich komme auch.** What extra fact does *auch* add?\
   a. That I am coming later.\
   b. That I might come.\
   c. That someone else is already coming.\
   d. That I am coming for a second time.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *auch* only makes sense if something is already true — that's why *Ich komme* can sound abrupt when someone has just told you their plans. Option d is the *wieder* reading.

</details>

12. What is the difference between *auch* and *noch* when you add something?\
   a. *auch* adds an amount; *noch* adds a person.\
   b. *noch* is the polite form of *auch*.\
   c. Both words do exactly the same thing.\
   d. *auch* adds something to a set; *noch* adds one more of something.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Ich möchte auch einen Kaffee* adds me to the coffee drinkers. *Ich möchte noch einen Kaffee* adds a second cup to the one I've already had. Different additions entirely.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

13. Complete the dialogue. B is not going to Vienna either.

```text
A: Ich fahre nicht nach Wien.
B: ___
```

   a. Ich auch.\
   b. Ich auch nicht.\
   c. Ich noch nicht auch.\
   d. Ich nicht auch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A's sentence is negative (*nicht*), so the agreement takes *auch nicht*. You could also say the fuller *Ich fahre auch nicht nach Wien.*

</details>

14. A bakery sells bread, and you discover they sell cake as well.\
   a. Sie haben auch Kuchen.\
   b. Sie haben Kuchen auch.\
   c. Sie haben noch Kuchen.\
   d. Auch sie haben Kuchen.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The cake is the addition, so *auch* goes in front of it. Option c would mean the cake hasn't sold out yet, and d would compare this bakery with other shops.

</details>

15. **Das weiß ich auch nicht.** Which statement is correct?\
   a. I know it exactly.\
   b. I do not want to say it.\
   c. I knew it before, but I do not know it now.\
   d. I do not know it either.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *auch nicht* joins your ignorance to someone else's. Option c is the *nicht mehr* reading — I used to know and have forgotten.

</details>

16. Which sentence puts the emphasis on the person rather than the drink?\
   a. Ich trinke auch Tee.\
   b. Ich trinke auch gern Tee.\
   c. Auch ich trinke Tee.\
   d. Ich trinke Tee.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Front position puts *ich* immediately after *auch*, so the person is what's added. It is emphatic and a little formal — normal conversation would use option a.

</details>

17. Someone looks out at the grey sky and says **Es ist auch kalt heute.** What is *auch* doing?\
   a. It adds a second person.\
   b. It asks about the weather.\
   c. It confirms that the situation makes sense.\
   d. It says that yesterday was warm.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** This is the light emphasis use — roughly "well, it *is* cold today," confirming that the grey sky makes sense. No list is being extended, which is why option a doesn't work.

</details>

18. You tell a friend you didn't like the film and he agrees. What is the normal everyday reply?\
   a. Ich nicht auch.\
   b. Ich auch.\
   c. Auch ich nicht.\
   d. Ich auch nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch nicht* is the standard two-word agreement with a negative. Option c is grammatical but emphatic — it stresses *you* specifically, which isn't what a casual "me neither" does.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. **Select all that apply.** Which replies agree with **Ich habe keine Zeit**?\
   a. Ich auch nicht.\
   b. Ich auch.\
   c. Ich habe auch keine Zeit.\
   d. Auch ich habe keine Zeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c, d**

**Explanation:** All three keep the negative in place, from the shortest reply to the most emphatic. Option b agrees with a positive statement, so after a negative it says the opposite of what you mean.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A colleague mentions she is taking the train tomorrow. You are taking the train too.

```text
Ich fahre ___ mit dem Zug.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **auch**

**Explanation:** *Ich fahre auch mit dem Zug.* The particle sits in front of *mit dem Zug*, the thing being added — not at the end of the sentence.

</details>

21. Put the words in order.

```text
nicht / weiß / auch / das / ich
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das weiß ich auch nicht.**

**Explanation:** *Das* comes first for emphasis, the verb stays in second position, and *auch nicht* holds together at the end. *Ich weiß das auch nicht* is equally correct and slightly more neutral.

</details>

22. **Produce it:** A friend says *Ich möchte ein Bier.* You'd like one as well.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich möchte auch ein Bier.**

**Explanation:** *Ich auch* on its own is just as natural in conversation. What you want to avoid is *Ich möchte ein Bier auch*, with the particle stranded at the end.

</details>
