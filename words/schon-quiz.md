## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Er ist ___ hier — wir können anfangen.* (he's already here)\
   a. noch\
   b. schon\
   c. erst

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Er ist schon hier.*

</details>

2. **Hast du schon gegessen?** What is the speaker asking?\
   a. Whether you eat at all.\
   b. Whether you have eaten by now.\
   c. Whether you're still eating.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** whether you've eaten by now.

</details>

3. A friend is nervous about an exam and says nothing. You say **Das schaffst du schon.** What are you doing?\
   a. Reassuring them.\
   b. Correcting them.\
   c. Asking a question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** reassuring them. This is *schon* as a comfort particle, not "already."

</details>

4. Contrast: **Ich bin noch hier.** / **Ich bin schon hier.** What's the difference?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *noch hier* = I haven't left yet. *schon hier* = I've already arrived, possibly earlier than expected.

**Explanation:** *schon* commonly marks something as already true or complete, and it can also add reassurance in context.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm already finished" in German?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin schon fertig.**

**Explanation:** *schon* commonly marks something as already true or complete, and it can also add reassurance in context.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** Ask a friend whether they're there yet, using *schon*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Bist du schon da?**

**Explanation:** *schon* commonly marks something as already true or complete, and it can also add reassurance in context.

</details>
