# wieder

**Type:** Adverb
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

## Core Idea

*wieder* = **again** — something stopped and is now happening a second time, or has returned to an earlier state.

The key contrast is with [noch](noch.md): *noch* means it never stopped, *wieder* means it stopped and restarted. See [noch vs. wieder](../comparisons/noch-vs-wieder.md).

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. "Again" — a repeated action

**Ich komme morgen wieder.**
I'll come again tomorrow.

**Wir treffen uns morgen wieder.**
We're meeting again tomorrow.

**Er ist wieder krank.**
He's ill again.

### 2. "Back" — returning to a previous state

**Ich bin wieder da.**
I'm back.

**Der Laden ist wieder offen.**
The shop is open again.

**Alles ist wieder gut.**
Everything's fine again.

### 3. In the chunk *schon wieder* — "yet again," usually annoyed

**Schon wieder Regen!**
Rain again!

**Du bist schon wieder zu spät.**
You're late yet again.

*schon wieder* is one of the most emotionally loaded chunks at A1 — it almost always signals mild irritation.

## Common Chunks

- **schon wieder** — yet again *(irritated)*
- **immer wieder** — over and over
- **wieder da** — back (here): *Ich bin gleich wieder da.* — I'll be right back.
- **nie wieder** — never again
- **noch einmal / noch mal** — one more time *(often where English says "again")* → [noch](noch.md)

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich bin wieder da.**
**Er ist wieder gesund.**
**Das Wetter ist wieder schön.**
**Wir sehen uns wieder.**
**Schon wieder du!**

The thread: something was true, stopped being true, and is true again.

## What Changes Without It?

**Er ist krank.**
He's ill.

**Er ist wieder krank.**
He's ill again. *(he got better in between)*

**Er ist noch krank.**
He's still ill. *(he never got better)*

Three sentences, one word of difference each, three quite different situations. This trio is worth memorizing as a set.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *wieder* where German prefers *noch einmal*.** For "say that again," German usually says **Sag das noch mal.** *wieder* fits a repeated event more than a repeated utterance.
- **Confusing *wieder* with *noch*.** See [noch vs. wieder](../comparisons/noch-vs-wieder.md) — this is the single most useful contrast on this page.
- **Confusing it with *wider*.** Different word, no *e*, means "against." Rare at A1, but it exists.
- **Missing the irritation in *schon wieder*.** If someone says *Schon wieder!*, they are not delighted.

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** *Ich bin wieder da* and *wieder + adjective* are everyday sentences.

## You May Hear

- **Bis bald! / Wir sehen uns wieder.** — See you again.
- **Ich bin gleich wieder da.** — I'll be right back.
- **Schon wieder?** — Again?
- **Es geht ihm wieder gut.** — He's doing well again.

## Mini Review

1. Choose the natural word: *Der Laden ist ___ offen.* (open again, after being closed)
   a. noch
   b. wieder
   c. schon

2. **Er ist wieder krank.** What happened in between?
   a. Nothing — he never got better.
   b. He recovered, and is now ill again.
   c. He's about to get ill.

3. Someone says **Schon wieder!** What's their mood?
   a. Pleased
   b. Neutral
   c. Annoyed

4. Contrast: **Er ist noch krank.** / **Er ist wieder krank.**

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm back" in German, using *wieder*?

6. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'll come again tomorrow"?
