## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Das kostet ___ fünf Euro.* (only five euros)\
   a. nur\
   b. noch\
   c. auch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Das kostet nur fünf Euro.*

</details>

2. What's the difference between **Ich trinke nur Wasser** and **Nur ich trinke Wasser**?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** The first limits what I drink (only water, nothing else). The second limits who drinks water (I'm the only one).

**Explanation:** *nur* limits the amount or scope: “only” or “just.”

</details>

3. **Wir haben nur noch zwei Karten.** What is the situation?\
   a. There are two tickets left, and that's all.\
   b. Two tickets have been sold.\
   c. Tickets cost two euros.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** only two are left.

</details>

4. Why does **Ich habe nur eine Frage** sound politer than **Ich habe eine Frage**?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *nur* shrinks the request, signalling that you won't take much of the listener's time.

**Explanation:** *nur* limits the amount or scope: “only” or “just.”

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I only have ten euros" in German, using *nur*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe nur zehn Euro.**

**Explanation:** *nur* limits the amount or scope: “only” or “just.”

</details>

6. **Produce it:** How would you say "That was just a joke"?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Das war nur ein Witz.** (*Das war nur Spaß* also works.)

**Explanation:** *nur* limits the amount or scope: “only” or “just.”

</details>
