# Quiz: kein

[← Back to **kein**](kein.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

---

## Part A — Recognize It

1. Negate **Ich habe ein Auto.**\
   a. Ich habe nicht ein Auto.\
   b. Ich habe kein Auto.\
   c. Ich habe keinen Auto.\
   d. Ich habe nicht Auto.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Put a *k* in front of *ein* and keep the ending exactly as it was: *ein Auto* → *kein Auto*. *Auto* is neuter, so there is no *-en*.

</details>

2. Someone offers you lunch and you are not hungry.\
   a. Ich habe nicht Hunger.\
   b. Ich habe kein Hunger.\
   c. Ich habe keine Hunger.\
   d. Ich habe keinen Hunger.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *der Hunger* is masculine, and *haben* takes the accusative — so *keinen*. Dropping the *-en* is the most frequent slip with this phrase; people will still understand you, but it's an easy fix.

</details>

3. **Das ist kein Problem.** Why is *kein* used instead of *nicht*?\
   a. Because the sentence is a question.\
   b. Because *Problem* is a noun.\
   c. Because *Problem* is neuter.\
   d. Because *nicht* only works with verbs.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The decision is about word class, not gender: a noun is being negated, so *kein* wins. Option d overstates the rule — *nicht* happily negates adjectives, adverbs and whole phrases too, just not bare nouns.

</details>

4. A colleague asks where the meeting room is. You genuinely don't know.\
   a. Ich habe nicht Ahnung.\
   b. Ich habe kein Ahnung.\
   c. Keine Ahnung.\
   d. Ich weiß keine.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Keine Ahnung* is the ready-made spoken phrase — extremely common, and no verb needed. *die Ahnung* is feminine, which is why it is *keine* and never *kein*.

</details>

5. You ask a hotel receptionist about restaurants in the village. There are none.\
   a. Hier gibt es nicht Restaurants.\
   b. Hier gibt es kein Restaurants.\
   c. Hier gibt es keine Restaurants.\
   d. Hier gibt es keinen Restaurants.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** In the plural, *kein* always takes *-e* in the nominative and accusative — *keine Restaurants*. This is the one form *ein* doesn't have at all, since *ein* has no plural.

</details>

6. Which gap needs *nicht* rather than *kein*?\
   a. Das ist ___ der Bahnhof.\
   b. Ich habe ___ Geld.\
   c. Wir haben ___ Kinder.\
   d. Er ist ___ Arzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *der Bahnhof* is a definite, specific thing, and *kein* can't negate that: *Das ist nicht der Bahnhof.* The other three have bare or indefinite nouns, which is exactly where *kein* belongs.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

7. You want to say: **That isn't a good idea.** A learner writes **Das ist nicht eine gute Idee.**\
   a. Correct — this expresses exactly that meaning.\
   b. Correct, but impolite.\
   c. Grammatically possible, but it means something different.\
   d. Unusual — the normal form is *Das ist keine gute Idee.*

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *nicht ein* is not flatly impossible, but German merges the two into *kein*, and that is what a speaker will say. Reach for *kein* whenever *ein* would have been there.

</details>

8. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Trinkst du Kaffee?
B: Nein, ich trinke ___ Kaffee.
```

   a. keinen\
   b. kein\
   c. keine\
   d. nicht

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *der Kaffee* is masculine and *trinken* takes the accusative, so *keinen Kaffee*. Notice that the German original had no article at all — a bare noun still pulls in *kein*.

</details>

9. What is the difference between **Ich trinke nicht** and **Ich trinke keinen Alkohol**?\
   a. The first sentence is incorrect.\
   b. Both sentences say exactly the same thing.\
   c. The first negates the action; the second names what is not being drunk.\
   d. The second sentence is a question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Ich trinke nicht* describes a habit and is usually understood as being about alcohol anyway. *Ich trinke keinen Alkohol* names the thing refused, and naming it is what brings in *kein*.

</details>

10. You are an only child and want to say you have no brother.\
   a. Ich habe kein Bruder.\
   b. Ich habe keine Bruder.\
   c. Ich habe nicht einen Bruder.\
   d. Ich habe keinen Bruder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *der Bruder*, accusative after *haben* → *keinen*. Compare *Er hat einen Bruder* — the ending on *kein* is simply the ending *ein* already had.

</details>

11. **Auf keinen Fall!** What does this mean?\
   a. Absolut nicht.\
   b. Vielleicht später.\
   c. Auf jeden Fall gern.\
   d. Ich weiß es nicht.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A fixed phrase: "no way, absolutely not." Its positive twin *auf jeden Fall* means "definitely" — worth learning as a pair, since only one small word separates them.

</details>

12. A learner writes **Ich habe nicht keine Zeit**, meaning "I don't have time." What went wrong?\
   a. *Zeit* needs the article *die*.\
   b. There are two negations — the correct sentence is *Ich habe keine Zeit.*\
   c. *keine* should be *keinen*.\
   d. *nicht* should be at the end of the sentence.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** German doesn't stack negations the way casual English does. One negation is enough, and for a noun that negation is *kein*.

</details>

## Part C — Apply It

13. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Ist das dein Auto?
B: Nein, ich habe ___ Auto.
```

   a. keinen\
   b. keine\
   c. kein\
   d. nicht

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *das Auto* is neuter, and neuter looks the same in the nominative and the accusative — so *kein Auto* either way. Only the masculine changes to *keinen*.

</details>

14. You arrive at the bakery in the afternoon. There is no bread in the shop at all today.\
   a. Wir haben kein Brot.\
   b. Wir haben nicht Brot.\
   c. Wir haben keine Brot.\
   d. Wir haben ein kein Brot.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Brot* is neuter, and *kein* takes no extra ending in the nominative/accusative neuter: **kein Brot**. *Nicht Brot* (b) wrongly negates a noun with *nicht* — the classic English-speaker mistake. *Keine Brot* (c) puts the feminine/plural ending on a neuter noun, and *ein kein Brot* (d) stacks two articles, which German never does.

</details>

15. Which sentence correctly uses **kein** with the masculine accusative noun *Bruder*?\
   a. Ich habe kein Bruder.\
   b. Ich habe keine Bruder.\
   c. Ich habe keinen Bruder.\
   d. Ich habe keins Bruder.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Bruder* is masculine, and the accusative ending for *kein* — just like *ein* — is *-en*: **keinen Bruder**, the same pattern shown in the lesson's Pattern Recognition section (*Er hat einen Bruder* → *Er hat keinen Bruder*). *Kein Bruder* (a) is missing the ending, *keine* (b) is the feminine/plural ending, and *keins* (d) isn't a form *kein* takes before a noun.

</details>

16. Someone asks whether the man at the door is a doctor. He is a nurse.\
   a. Er ist nicht Arzt.\
   b. Er ist kein Arzt.\
   c. Er ist keinen Arzt.\
   d. Er ist nicht ein Arzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Professions appear without an article in German (*Er ist Arzt*), and a bare noun still takes *kein*. And *sein* takes the nominative, so there is no *-en* here even though *Arzt* is masculine.

</details>

17. What is the natural everyday way to say "I have no idea"?\
   a. Ich habe nicht Idee.\
   b. Ich habe keine Idee.\
   c. Keine Ahnung.\
   d. Ich habe kein Ahnung.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Option b is grammatically correct but sounds like a translation; Germans say *Keine Ahnung.* This is a case where the textbook-possible sentence and the normal sentence are not the same thing.

</details>

18. In **Ich habe keine Kinder**, what is the form *keine*?\
   a. Plural — *ein* has no plural form, but *kein* does.\
   b. Dative singular.\
   c. Masculine accusative.\
   d. Incorrect; the correct form would be *keinen*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *kein* copies every ending of *ein* and adds one form *ein* lacks: a plural. That plural is *keine* in both nominative and accusative.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. **Select all that apply.** In which gaps does *kein* belong rather than *nicht*?\
   a. Ich habe ___ Auto.\
   b. Das ist ___ teuer.\
   c. Er hat ___ Bruder.\
   d. Hier gibt es ___ Hotels.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c, d**

**Explanation:** Three bare or indefinite nouns, all *kein* — *kein Auto*, *keinen Bruder*, *keine Hotels*. Only b has an adjective in the gap, and adjectives take *nicht*.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A friend asks whether you can come out this evening. You have to work late.

```text
Ich habe heute ___ Zeit.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **keine**

**Explanation:** *die Zeit* is feminine, so the accusative form is *keine* — identical to the nominative. *Ich habe keine Zeit* is one of the two or three *kein* phrases worth having completely automatic.

</details>

21. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   Your host offers you tea. You don't drink tea.

```text
Danke, ich trinke ___ Tee.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **keinen**

**Explanation:** *der Tee* is masculine and *trinken* takes the accusative — the same shape as *keinen Kaffee* and *keinen Hunger*. If you wrote *kein Tee*, you will be understood, but this is the one ending worth getting right.

</details>

22. Put the words in order.

```text
Hunger / habe / keinen / ich
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich habe keinen Hunger.**

**Explanation:** Literally "I have no hunger" — German states it as a possession rather than with an adjective. *Ich bin nicht hungrig* exists, but the *Hunger* version is what people actually say.

</details>

23. Negate this sentence: **Er hat einen Bruder.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Er hat keinen Bruder.**

**Explanation:** Nothing else moves — *ein* simply grows a *k* and keeps its ending. That is the whole trick with *kein*, and it works for every gender and case.

</details>

24. **Produce it:** A shopkeeper apologises because your order will take five more minutes. Tell her it's fine.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Kein Problem.**

**Explanation:** No verb needed — *Kein Problem* stands on its own, exactly like *Keine Ahnung*. The fuller *Das ist kein Problem* is also natural, and *Kein Problem, ich habe Zeit* would be a friendly extension.

</details>
