# eben

**Type:** Modal particle; also "just now" and the adjective "flat"
**Level:** A2
**Priority:** Tier 3
**Knowledge goal:** Passive

[← ja](ja.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [Next → halt](halt.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

As a particle, *eben* means **"that's just how it is"** — a shrug in word form. It marks something as an unchangeable fact that there's no point arguing with.

It also has an ordinary adverb use meaning **"just now, a moment ago"**, which is the one you're more likely to need first.

*halt* does almost exactly the same particle job — see [halt](halt.md) and the note below.

## The Situation, the Attitude, the Tone

**The situation:** someone has pointed out a problem or an annoyance, and you're accepting it rather than fighting it.

**The speaker's attitude:** resignation. It is what it is.

**The change in tone:** complaint → acceptance.

**What English does instead:** "just," "simply," "that's how it is," "oh well," or a shrug.

## Most Important Uses

### 1. Resignation — "that's just how it is"

**Das ist eben so.**
That's just how it is.

**Dann gehen wir eben zu Fuß.**
Then we'll just walk, I suppose.

**Er ist eben noch jung.**
He's young, that's all.

### 2. Agreement — "exactly"

**Eben!**
Exactly! / That's just what I said.

A one-word answer expressing strong agreement, very common in conversation.

### 3. "Just now" — a moment ago *(not a particle)*

**Er war eben hier.**
He was here a moment ago.

**Ich habe eben angerufen.**
I just called.

This overlaps with [gerade](../words/gerade.md), which is the more common word for it.

## *eben* or *halt*?

They're near-synonyms as particles. The differences are regional and stylistic rather than logical:

- ***eben*** — used everywhere; slightly more neutral, a touch more standard
- ***halt*** — more southern (Bavaria, Austria, Switzerland) and more casual; increasingly common everywhere in speech

*Das ist eben so* and *Das ist halt so* mean the same thing and would both be normal.

## Common Chunks

- **Eben!** — exactly!
- **Das ist eben so.** — that's just how it is
- **eben nicht** — precisely not: *Das ist eben nicht so einfach.*
- **eben erst** — only just: *Ich bin eben erst angekommen.*

## Pattern Recognition

**Das ist eben teuer.**
**Dann bleiben wir eben zu Hause.**
**So ist das eben.**
**Eben!**
**Er hat eben angerufen.** ← "just now", not the particle

## What Changes Without It?

**Das ist so.**
That's how it is. *(a plain statement)*

**Das ist eben so.**
That's just how it is. *(and there's no point complaining)*

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Confusing it with "even."** *eben* is not English "even." That's *sogar* (*sogar er* — even he) or *gerade* in some contexts.
- **Mixing it up with *eben* the adjective.** *Der Weg ist eben* means the path is flat.
- **Trying to produce it.** This is a word to recognize; used wrongly, resignation particles can read as dismissive.

## Active or Passive?

**Passive at A1.** You'll hear it constantly in real conversation, and understanding the shrug is enough. One exception: **Eben!** as a one-word agreement is safe and useful.

## You May Hear

- **Eben!** — Exactly!
- **Das ist eben Deutschland.** — Well, that's Germany for you.
- **Dann eben morgen.** — Tomorrow, then.
- **Er ist eben so.** — That's just how he is.
