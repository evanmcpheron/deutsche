## Mini Review


1. **Das ist ja teuer!** What is *ja* doing?\
   a. Answering "yes."\
   b. Marking surprise or shared observation.\
   c. Negating the sentence.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** surprise or shared observation, not "yes."

</details>

2. **Du kennst ihn ja.** What is the speaker assuming?\
   a. You've never met him.\
   b. You already know him.\
   c. You want to meet him.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** that you already know him.

</details>

3. Someone asks **Hast du keine Zeit?** and you *do* have time. Which word do you answer with?\
   a. Ja\
   b. Doch\
   c. Nein

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Doch.*

</details>

4. Is particle *ja* stressed or unstressed?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Unstressed. The stress falls on the word after it.

**Explanation:** Unstressed *ja* often points to information the speaker treats as known, obvious, or shared.

</details>

5. **Recognition:** Should you start inserting *ja* into your own sentences at A1?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** No

**Explanation:** recognize it for now. It depends on intonation and shared context, and inserting it too early tends to sound odd.

</details>
