## Part A — Recognize the Attitude

1. A café is expensive, but there is no cheaper place nearby. Your friend says **Das ist eben so.** What attitude does **eben** add?\
   a. The speaker accepts the situation as something that is not worth arguing about. \
   b. The speaker presents the price as a shared observation the listener already knows. \
   c. The speaker pushes back against someone who says the café is cheap. \
   d. The speaker encourages the listener to reconsider a refusal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Particle *eben* often marks resigned acceptance: this is simply the situation, and there is little point fighting it.

</details>

2. Compare:

```text
Das ist so.
Das ist eben so.
```

What does the second sentence add?\
   a. The facts stay the same, but the speaker presents them with a “that is just how it is” attitude. \
   b. The speaker marks the fact as something both people already know. \
   c. The speaker pushes against a different expectation. \
   d. The speaker expresses surprise at discovering the fact.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Removing *eben* leaves a plain statement. Adding it contributes the resigned, accepting perspective taught in the lesson.

</details>

3. You explain a problem, and your friend answers only **Eben!** What does that mean here?\
   a. “Stop!” \
   b. “Yes.” \
   c. “Well ...” \
   d. “Exactly!”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Eben!* on its own is a common agreement word meaning “Exactly!” This is the safest active use of *eben* at this stage.

</details>

4. The museum is closed today. You wanted to go there, but you accept the change of plan and say **Dann bleiben wir eben zu Hause.** What does **eben** signal?\
   a. Staying home was the preferred plan from the beginning. \
   b. Staying home is an accepted alternative because the first plan does not work. \
   c. You are reminding everyone that staying home was already agreed. \
   d. You are trying to persuade someone who refuses to stay home.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Dann ... eben ...* often accepts an inconvenient alternative: the preferred plan is unavailable, so this is what you will do instead.

</details>

5. Your friend is too tired to go out. Someone says **Er ist eben müde.** Which interpretation best fits the particle?\
   a. He is tired again after feeling better. \
   b. He is simply tired; that fact explains the situation and is being accepted. \
   c. The speaker points out tiredness as shared information the listener already knows. \
   d. The speaker protests against someone who says he is not tired.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Here *eben* presents being tired as the simple fact that explains the situation: that is just how things are.

</details>

6. Compare:

```text
Das ist teuer.
Das ist eben teuer.
```

What is mainly lost if **eben** is removed?\
   a. The adjective *teuer*. \
   b. The present tense. \
   c. The statement that the thing is expensive. \
   d. The resigned “there is no point arguing with this fact” tone.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Both sentences state that something is expensive. *Eben* changes the speaker’s attitude toward that fact, not the basic factual content.

</details>

## Part B — Particle or Another Meaning?

7. The lesson says **eben** also has an ordinary adverb use that is not a modal particle. Which meaning is that?\
   a. “already” \
   b. “again” \
   c. “still” \
   d. “just now / a moment ago”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Outside the particle use, *eben* can mean “just now” or “a moment ago.” The lesson notes that *gerade* is more common for that meaning.

</details>

8. Read **Der Weg ist eben.** What does **eben** mean in this sentence?\
   a. resigned \
   b. flat \
   c. exactly \
   d. a moment ago

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Here *eben* is the adjective “flat.” It is not the modal particle and does not express resignation.

</details>

9. Which English meaning should you **not** automatically assign to German **eben**?\
   a. “exactly,” when *Eben!* is a one-word agreement \
   b. “just now,” in its ordinary adverb use \
   c. English “even,” as in “even Anna” \
   d. “that is just how it is,” as a rough description of the particle attitude

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** German *eben* is not the English word “even.” The lesson identifies that as a false-friend mistake.

</details>

10. The intended effect is resigned acceptance. Judge the sentence:

```text
Das ist teuer.
```

Which description is best?\
   a. Incorrect German. \
   b. Correct German, but it is a plain statement and does not itself add the resigned *eben* perspective. \
   c. Correct only in southern Germany. \
   d. It means “That was expensive a moment ago.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Das ist teuer* is fully correct. It simply lacks the extra “that is just the fact we have to accept” attitude supplied by particle *eben*.

</details>

11. Which statement best matches the lesson’s comparison of particle **eben** and **halt**?\
   a. They are near-synonyms; *eben* is slightly more neutral/standard and is used everywhere. \
   b. They have opposite meanings. \
   c. *Eben* is only used in Austria. \
   d. *Halt* is the formal written version of *eben*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The lesson treats them as near-synonyms whose main differences are regional and stylistic, not logical. *Eben* is the somewhat more neutral choice.

</details>

12. The bus does not come, and walking is the only practical option. Someone says **Dann gehen wir eben zu Fuß.** Which reading fits best?\
   a. The speaker reminds everyone that walking was always the agreed plan. \
   b. The speaker accepts walking as the available alternative. \
   c. The speaker is trying to persuade someone who refuses to walk. \
   d. The speaker presents walking as surprising new information.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Dann ... eben ...* marks acceptance of the alternative. Walking may not be the first choice, but the speaker accepts it.

</details>

## Part C — Distinguish Tone, Style, and Function

13. Someone says a task is easy. You answer **Das ist eben nicht einfach.** What does **eben nicht** contribute here?\
   a. The task is not easy yet, but the speaker expects that to change. \
   b. The task was easy before but is no longer easy. \
   c. The task becomes easy again. \
   d. The speaker emphasizes that the expected positive description is simply or precisely not true.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Eben nicht* can underline the negative point as the decisive reality: it is precisely not easy. It does not mean “not yet” or “not anymore.”

</details>

14. Which statement is accurate when comparing **Das ist eben so** and **Das ist halt so**?\
   a. Only the *eben* sentence can express resignation. \
   b. Only the *halt* sentence is correct German. \
   c. Both can naturally express the same resigned acceptance; the difference is mainly style and region. \
   d. The *halt* sentence means “Stop doing that.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The lesson explicitly warns against inventing a sharp meaning difference. Both sentences are normal and can express the same basic attitude.

</details>

15. Which use does the lesson specifically identify as a safe, useful active exception for an A1 learner?\
   a. **Eben!** as a one-word agreement \
   b. putting particle *eben* into every complaint \
   c. using *eben* to mean English “even” \
   d. using the adjective *eben* as a conversational filler

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The lesson recommends passive recognition of particle *eben* at A1, with *Eben!* = “Exactly!” as the clear active exception.

</details>

16. Which sentence uses **eben** as the resignation particle rather than as the adjective “flat” or the separate “just now” meaning?\
   a. Das ist eben so. \
   b. Der Weg ist eben. \
   c. *Eben* kann auch „gerade eben“ bedeuten. \
   d. Eben! means “exactly” as a one-word response.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Das ist eben so* is the core modal-particle pattern. *Der Weg ist eben* uses the adjective “flat,” while the other choices describe separate uses rather than showing this particle in a sentence.

</details>

17. The intended meaning is “The path is flat.” Judge **Der Weg ist eben.**\
   a. Incorrect because *eben* can only be a particle. \
   b. Correct, but it means the path is expensive. \
   c. Correct German; this is the adjective **eben**, not the resignation particle. \
   d. Correct only if *eben* means “a moment ago.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** This is a correct but different grammatical use. The same spelling *eben* can be an adjective meaning “flat.”

</details>

18. A person first sounds annoyed about a problem, then says **Das ist eben so.** What shift does the lesson associate with the particle?\
   a. plain statement → shared observation \
   b. refusal → encouragement \
   c. complaint → acceptance \
   d. direct question → casual aside

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The lesson summarizes the tone shift as complaint to acceptance. *Eben* tells you how the speaker is framing the unchanged fact.

</details>

19. **Select all that apply.** Which uses of **eben** are taught in the lesson?\
   a. a resignation particle meaning roughly “that is just how it is” \
   b. **Eben!** as strong agreement: “Exactly!” \
   c. an ordinary adverb meaning “just now / a moment ago” \
   d. the normal German equivalent of English “even”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** All three are taught uses. English “even” is specifically a false friend and should not be mapped to *eben*.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

20. Give the one-word lesson-taught response that means **“Exactly!”**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Eben!**

**Explanation:** This is the safest active use of *eben* for an A1 learner.

</details>

21. Put the words in order to make the fixed resignation pattern from the lesson.

```text
ist / eben / das / so
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das ist eben so.**

**Explanation:** The particle sits inside the statement: *Das ist eben so.* The sentence presents the situation as something to accept.

</details>

22. Add only **eben** to the sentence so that the fact sounds like something the speaker is resigned to accepting.

```text
Das ist teuer.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das ist eben teuer.**

**Explanation:** The factual meaning remains “It is expensive.” *Eben* adds the lesson-taught accepting “that is simply the situation” perspective.

</details>
