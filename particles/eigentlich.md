## Core Idea

*eigentlich* opens a small gap between **what's on the surface** and **what's really the case**.

In statements that gives you "actually, in fact, originally." In questions it's softer than that — closer to "by the way" or "come to think of it," a signal that you're changing tack or asking something slightly off-topic.

## The Situation, the Attitude, the Tone

**The situation:** either you're correcting a picture ("actually, …") or you're introducing a new question in a conversation.

**The speaker's attitude:** the obvious version isn't quite the true one / this just occurred to me.

**The change in tone:** in questions, direct → casual and slightly curious. It makes a question sound less like it was planned.

**What English does instead:** "actually," "really," "by the way," "so tell me," "come to think of it."

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. In questions — "by the way, so tell me"

**Wie alt bist du eigentlich?**
How old are you, actually?

**Was machst du eigentlich beruflich?**
So what do you do for a living?

**Wo wohnst du eigentlich?**
Where do you live, by the way?

This is the most common conversational use, and it's how new topics get raised politely.

### 2. In statements — "actually, in fact"

**Eigentlich wollte ich nicht kommen.**
I actually didn't want to come. *(but I did)*

**Das ist eigentlich ganz einfach.**
That's actually quite simple.

**Ich habe eigentlich keine Zeit.**
I don't really have time. *(but I might make some)*

Notice the implied "but" hanging in the air. *eigentlich* very often sets up an exception that follows.

### 3. "Originally / strictly speaking"

**Eigentlich komme ich aus Hamburg, aber ich wohne in Berlin.**
I'm originally from Hamburg, but I live in Berlin.

**Eigentlich ist das verboten.**
Strictly speaking, that's not allowed.

## Common Chunks

- **Was machst du eigentlich?** — so what do you do?
- **Eigentlich schon.** — well, yes, in a way.
- **Eigentlich nicht.** — not really.
- **eigentlich nichts** — nothing much

*Eigentlich nicht* and *eigentlich schon* are complete, natural answers on their own — worth having ready.

## Pattern Recognition

**Wie heißt du eigentlich?**
**Wo bist du eigentlich?**
**Eigentlich habe ich keine Lust.**
**Das ist eigentlich nicht schlimm.**
**Eigentlich ja, aber …**

In questions, *eigentlich* sits inside, after the subject. In statements, it often starts the sentence — and then the verb comes straight after: *Eigentlich **wollte** ich …*

## What Changes Without It?

**Wo wohnst du?**
Where do you live? *(direct question)*

**Wo wohnst du eigentlich?**
Where do you live, actually? *(a friendly aside; we were talking about something else)*

**Ich habe keine Zeit.**
I don't have time. *(final)*

**Ich habe eigentlich keine Zeit.**
I don't really have time. *(… but ask me anyway)*

That second pair is worth noticing: *eigentlich* leaves a door open.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Reading it as English "eventually."** Unrelated. *eigentlich* = actually; "eventually" is *schließlich* or *irgendwann*.
- **Hearing it as a hard "actually."** In questions it's much lighter than English "actually," which can sound corrective.
- **Missing the implied "but."** *Eigentlich wollte ich nicht kommen* almost always continues — the speaker came anyway.
- **Overusing it.** It's frequent in speech but not in every sentence.

## Active or Passive?

**Recognition First.** The two short answers — *eigentlich schon* and *eigentlich nicht* — are safe to start using early. Building it into your own sentences can wait.

## You May Hear

- **Was machst du eigentlich so?** — So what are you up to these days?
- **Eigentlich nicht.** — Not really.
- **Wie spät ist es eigentlich?** — What time is it, actually?
- **Das wollte ich eigentlich fragen.** — That's what I was actually going to ask.
