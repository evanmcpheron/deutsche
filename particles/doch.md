# doch

## Core Idea

*doch* pushes **against something** — against an expectation, against a refusal, against what was just said.

That single idea covers both of its lives:

- as a **particle**, it nudges, encourages, or reminds ("come on, do it," "but you know this")
- as a **word on its own**, it's the special German "yes" that contradicts a negative question

There is no English equivalent for the particle. English does the same work with "do" ("do come along"), "come on," "but," or tone alone.

## The Situation, the Attitude, the Tone

**The situation:** something has been said, assumed, or refused, and you're pushing back gently.

**The speaker's attitude:** I think you've got this slightly wrong / I'd like you to reconsider / surely you remember.

**The change in tone:** neutral → encouraging, insistent, or reassuring, depending on the sentence type.

**What English does instead:** "come on," "do," "but," "surely," "you know" — or nothing but emphasis.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. *doch* as a contradicting "yes"

This is the one to learn first, because it's a rule, not a feeling. When someone asks a **negative** question or makes a negative statement, and you want to contradict it, German uses *doch*, not *ja*.

**Kommst du nicht mit? — Doch!**
Aren't you coming? — Yes, I am!

**Du magst keinen Kaffee. — Doch, ich mag Kaffee!**
You don't like coffee. — Yes I do!

**Hast du keine Zeit? — Doch, ich habe Zeit.**
Don't you have time? — Yes, I do.

Answering *ja* here would be genuinely confusing. English has no equivalent and has to say "yes I am" with emphasis.

### 2. Encouraging an invitation or request

**Komm doch mit!**
Come on, come with us.

The basic request is **Komm mit!** — "Come along!" Adding *doch* makes it warmer and more persuasive, the way English might say "oh, do come" or "come on, come with us."

**Setzen Sie sich doch.**
Do have a seat. *(formal, welcoming)*

**Frag doch Anna.**
Why don't you ask Anna.

### 3. Reminding — "but you know this"

**Das weißt du doch.**
But you know that.

**Wir haben doch morgen frei.**
We've got tomorrow off, remember.

**Du bist doch müde.**
But you're tired *(aren't you?)*.

Here *doch* appeals to shared knowledge: I'm not telling you something new, I'm reminding you.

### 4. Mild surprise or protest

**Das ist doch nicht möglich!**
That can't be right!

**Das ist doch teuer!**
But that's expensive!

## Common Chunks

- **Doch!** — yes I am / yes it is *(contradicting)*
- **Komm doch mit!** — do come along
- **Das ist doch …!** — but that's …!
- **Das weißt du doch.** — you know that, though
- **doch nicht** — surely not: *Das ist doch nicht dein Ernst!* — You can't be serious!
- **doch mal** — the two stack happily: *Komm doch mal vorbei.* — Do drop by sometime.

## Pattern Recognition

**Komm doch mit!**
**Setz dich doch.**
**Das weißt du doch.**
**Das ist doch nicht schwer.**
**Frag ihn doch.**

Each one pushes: come along after all, sit down after all, you knew this after all.

## What Changes Without It?

**Komm mit!**
Come along! *(a plain invitation, or an order)*

**Komm doch mit!**
Oh, come with us. *(warmer, persuasive — you're overcoming a hesitation)*

And:

**Das ist nicht so schwer.** — That isn't so hard. *(a statement)*
**Das ist doch nicht so schwer.** — Come on, it isn't that hard. *(reassuring, or slightly impatient)*

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Answering *ja* to a negative question.** *Kommst du nicht? — Ja* is ambiguous at best. Use **Doch** when you mean "yes, I am."
- **Hunting for one English word.** *doch* is a direction of push, not a meaning.
- **Producing it too early.** Inserted at random, *doch* can make a sentence sound argumentative. Recognize first.
- **Missing it entirely in speech.** *doch* is unstressed and easy to skip — but it often carries the whole emotional message of the sentence.

## Active or Passive?

**Recognition First** for the particle. **Active for the contradicting "yes"** — that one is a discrete, learnable rule and you'll need it within your first weeks of real conversation.

## You May Hear

- **Doch, doch!** — Yes, honestly! *(insisting)*
- **Das ist doch klar.** — That's obvious, surely.
- **Komm doch rein!** — Do come in!
- **Das kann doch nicht sein.** — That can't be.
