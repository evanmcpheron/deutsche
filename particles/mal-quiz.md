## Part A — Recognize the Tone Change

1. Compare:

```text
Warte!
Warte mal.
```

What does **mal** mainly change?\
   a. The action stays the same, but the request sounds smaller and more casual. \
   b. The verb changes from “wait” to “come.” \
   c. The sentence becomes a past-tense statement. \
   d. The request becomes more urgent.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Particle *mal* usually changes the social tone, not the factual request. *Warte mal* is a relaxed “hang on a moment” rather than a bare command.

</details>

2. A friend begins **Sag mal, hast du Zeit?** What is **Sag mal** doing here?\
   a. It opens a casual question and makes it feel less abrupt. \
   b. It tells the listener to repeat a sentence exactly. \
   c. It means “say this three times.” \
   d. It makes the question formal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Sag mal ...* is a common conversational opener. It flags that a question is coming and gives it a relaxed, curious tone.

</details>

3. You are speaking formally with **Sie** and want to ask someone to look here. Which sentence matches the lesson?\
   a. Schau mal hier. \
   b. Sie schauen mal hier. \
   c. Mal schauen Sie hier. \
   d. Schauen Sie mal hier.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Schauen Sie mal hier.* keeps the formal *Sie* imperative and uses *mal* to soften the request. The particle does not make the relationship informal.

</details>

4. Your friend is across the room. You say **Komm mal her.** Which description fits best?\
   a. You are asking whether your friend is here. \
   b. You are casually asking your friend to come over for a moment. \
   c. You are saying your friend comes here every day. \
   d. You are multiplying two numbers.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** In this imperative, *mal* makes the request feel small and routine: roughly “come here a moment.”

</details>

5. Which sentence uses **mal** in a clearly different, non-particle meaning?\
   a. Warte mal. \
   b. Schau mal. \
   c. Zwei mal drei ist sechs. \
   d. Sag mal, hast du Hunger?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** In arithmetic, *mal* means “times.” The other examples use conversational particle *mal* with a request or imperative.

</details>

6. Someone says **Komm mal vorbei!** Which lesson-taught interpretation can **mal** have here?\
   a. “Come by three times.” \
   b. “Come by sometime.” \
   c. “Do not come by.” \
   d. “You are already here.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Here *mal* can be understood as shortened *einmal*: “once / sometime.” This is a recognition use distinct from simple request-softening.

</details>

7. Which sentence most clearly shows **mal** softening a short imperative?\
   a. Ich zeige das Buch. \
   b. Das Buch ist hier. \
   c. Zeigst du das Buch? \
   d. Zeig mal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Zeig mal.* is the lesson pattern: a short imperative made more casual by *mal*.

</details>

## Part B — With or Without mal?

8. Compare:

```text
Gib mir das Buch.
Gib mir mal das Buch.
```

What changes in the second sentence?\
   a. The listener should give two books. \
   b. The sentence becomes a question. \
   c. The request is still the same, but it sounds more routine and less pointed. \
   d. The speaker says the book belongs to the listener.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Mal* does not change what object is requested. It takes the edge off the imperative and makes the request feel smaller.

</details>

9. You are writing a formal complaint to an office. What is the best lesson-based choice?\
   a. Do not add conversational particle *mal* just to make the writing sound friendly. \
   b. Put *mal* in every request. \
   c. Use *mal* only after every noun. \
   d. Use *mal* because it makes writing more official.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The lesson treats particle *mal* as spoken, conversational German. It is not a general politeness marker for formal writing.

</details>

10. The intended request is casual: **Warte mal.** Judge the sentence **Warte!**\
   a. Incorrect German. \
   b. It means “do not wait.” \
   c. Correct German, but more direct and brisk. \
   d. Exactly the same in social tone.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Warte!* is a valid imperative. Adding *mal* does not fix grammar; it changes tone from bare/direct toward casual and relaxed.

</details>

11. Which fixed chunk is the natural lesson-taught way to say something like **“Hold on a moment!”**?\
   a. Mal Moment! \
   b. Moment mal! \
   c. Moment ist mal! \
   d. Mal ist Moment!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Moment mal!* is a common fixed conversational chunk. It is worth learning as a whole expression.

</details>

12. Which sentence most naturally opens a casual question to a friend?\
   a. Sag mal, wo ist Anna? \
   b. Mal sag, wo ist Anna? \
   c. Wo mal ist Anna? \
   d. Sag wo, mal ist Anna?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Sag mal, ...* is the standard chunk taught in the lesson for introducing a casual question.

</details>

13. Which sentence correctly uses **mal** with a formal **Sie** imperative?\
   a. Warten mal Sie. \
   b. Warte Sie mal. \
   c. Warten Sie mal. \
   d. Sie mal warten.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Formal imperative word order is *Warten Sie ...*; adding *mal* gives *Warten Sie mal.*

</details>

## Part C — Particle, Other Meaning, and Speaker Attitude

14. Read:

```text
Zwei mal fünf ist zehn.
```

What is **mal** doing?\
   a. Making a request friendlier. \
   b. Introducing a question. \
   c. Meaning “sometime.” \
   d. Meaning “times” in arithmetic.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** This is not the modal particle. In arithmetic, *mal* is the multiplication word “times.”

</details>

15. You want a friend to look at something on your phone. Which sentence is the most natural lesson-taught casual request?\
   a. Ich schaue. \
   b. Schaust du immer? \
   c. Schau mal! \
   d. Mal ist schauen!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Schau mal!* is one of the safest fixed chunks for active use. The particle makes the short imperative feel casual.

</details>

16. Which statement best describes the lesson’s guidance about particle **mal**?\
   a. Translate it as “times” in every sentence. \
   b. Add it to all statements to sound natural. \
   c. Use it mainly in formal written German. \
   d. Recognize it especially with short imperatives and casual requests; do not hunt for one literal English translation.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Particle *mal* often has no separate English word. Its main A1 value is recognizing how it softens short spoken requests.

</details>

17. Compare:

```text
Kannst du helfen?
Kannst du mal helfen?
```

What does **mal** mainly contribute in the second question?\
   a. It asks whether the person helped yesterday. \
   b. It makes the request feel smaller and more casual. \
   c. It changes *helfen* into a noun. \
   d. It means “three times.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The requested action stays “help.” *Mal* reduces the social weight of the request, similar to an English “give me a hand” or “just help for a moment.”

</details>

18. Which sentence does **not** use **mal** as a conversational softening particle?\
   a. Hör mal. \
   b. Drei mal vier ist zwölf. \
   c. Zeig mal. \
   d. Warte mal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Drei mal vier* is arithmetic. In the other sentences, *mal* accompanies a short imperative and changes its conversational tone.

</details>

19. A clerk says **Warten Sie mal.** What should you understand about the tone?\
   a. The grammar remains formal because of *Sie*, while *mal* makes the request less sharp. \
   b. *Mal* changes *Sie* into informal *du*. \
   c. The clerk is talking about multiplication. \
   d. The clerk is describing a completed action.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Mal* can soften formal *Sie* imperatives too. It changes the conversational edge, not the formal pronoun relationship.

</details>

20. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does **mal** primarily make a request or imperative sound more casual?\
   a. Warte mal. \
   b. Schau mal. \
   c. Warten Sie mal. \
   d. Zwei mal drei ist sechs.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** In a, b, and c, *mal* softens a spoken request or imperative. In d, it simply means “times” in arithmetic.

</details>
