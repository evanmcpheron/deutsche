## Mini Review


1. **Warte mal.** How does this differ from **Warte!**?\
   a. It's a different verb.\
   b. It's softer and more casual.\
   c. It's more urgent.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** softer and more casual. Same request, friendlier delivery.

</details>

2. Someone begins with **Sag mal, …**. What are they about to do?\
   a. Tell you a story.\
   b. Ask you something.\
   c. Correct you.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** asking you something. *Sag mal* flags an incoming question.

</details>

3. Is *mal* appropriate in a formal written complaint to a landlord?\
   a. Yes, it's neutral.\
   b. No, it's spoken and casual.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** leave it out of formal writing.

</details>

4. What does *mal* add to **Kannst du mir helfen?**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** It makes the request smaller and more casual

**Explanation:** closer to "could you give me a hand?" than "can you help me?"

</details>

5. **Recognition:** In **Ich war schon mal in Wien**, is *mal* softening a request?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** No. Here *mal* is short for *einmal* and means "at some point / ever"

**Explanation:** the sentence says "I've been to Vienna before."

</details>
