## Mini Review


1. **Wo wohnst du eigentlich?** What does *eigentlich* signal?\
   a. Suspicion.\
   b. A friendly, slightly off-topic question.\
   c. That the previous answer was wrong.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** a friendly aside, often introducing a new topic.

</details>

2. **Ich habe eigentlich keine Zeit.** What's likely to come next?\
   a. A firm refusal.\
   b. "… but go on, tell me."\
   c. A change of subject.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *eigentlich* leaves a door open; an exception usually follows.

</details>

3. Someone answers your question with **Eigentlich nicht.** What do they mean?\
   a. Definitely not.\
   b. Not really.\
   c. Yes.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** not really. It's softer than a flat *nein*.

</details>

4. Why is "eventually" a false friend here?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Because *eigentlich* means "actually," not "eventually." The English word for that is *schließlich* or *irgendwann*.

**Explanation:** *eigentlich* often signals “actually” or a shift to what the speaker really wants to ask or say.

</details>

5. **Recognition:** In **Eigentlich komme ich aus Köln**, what is the speaker signalling about where they live now?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** That they live somewhere else now. *eigentlich* marks Cologne as the original, not the current, situation.

**Explanation:** *eigentlich* often signals “actually” or a shift to what the speaker really wants to ask or say.

</details>
