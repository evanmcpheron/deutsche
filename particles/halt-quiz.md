## Mini Review


1. **Das ist halt so.** What's the speaker doing?\
   a. Complaining bitterly.\
   b. Accepting something unchangeable.\
   c. Asking a question.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** accepting it.

</details>

2. What's the difference between *halt* and [eben](eben.md) as particles?\
   a. Opposite meanings.\
   b. Almost none — *halt* is more southern and casual.\
   c. *halt* is formal.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** near-synonyms; *halt* is more southern and casual.

</details>

3. **Nächster Halt: Hauptbahnhof.** What does *Halt* mean here?\
   a. Stop (station).\
   b. Just.\
   c. Hold on.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** a stop or station. That's the noun, not the particle.

</details>

4. Should *halt* appear in a formal email?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** No. It's casual spoken German.

**Explanation:** *halt* is a casual spoken particle that often signals resigned acceptance: “that is just how it is.”

</details>

5. **Recognition:** In **Dann nehmen wir halt den Bus**, how does the speaker feel about taking the bus?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** It's not their first choice, but they're fine with it

**Explanation:** resigned, not annoyed.

</details>
