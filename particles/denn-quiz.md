## Mini Review


1. **Wo warst du denn?** compared with **Wo warst du?** — what does *denn* add?\
   a. A demand for an explanation.\
   b. Interest and warmth.\
   c. Nothing; they're identical.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** interest and warmth; it makes the question conversational.

</details>

2. Someone says **Ich komme nicht mit.** You reply **Warum denn nicht?** What are you doing?\
   a. Asking, with interest, why not.\
   b. Telling them off.\
   c. Agreeing.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** asking why not, with genuine curiosity.

</details>

3. In **Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde**, what is *denn* doing?\
   a. Softening a question.\
   b. Meaning "because".\
   c. Meaning "then".

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** it's the conjunction "because" (and note the verb stays in second position).

</details>

4. Where does particle *denn* sit in a question — at the start, or inside?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Inside

**Explanation:** after the verb and subject: *Wo warst du **denn**?*

</details>

5. **Recognition:** Does adding *denn* change what a question is asking, or how it sounds?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** How it sounds. The information requested is identical.

**Explanation:** In questions, unstressed *denn* adds conversational interest or warmth without changing the basic information being requested.

</details>
