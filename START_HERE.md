# Start Here

[← Back to README](README.md) | [↑ Master Index](MASTER_INDEX.md) | [Study Guide →](STUDY_GUIDE.md)

This is a reading order for someone with no instructor. Follow it top to bottom.

**About the paces below:** they are approximate starting points, not deadlines. "Roughly one week" means "this is about one week's worth of material for someone studying most days" — not "you have failed if it takes twelve days." What matters far more than speed is the **rhythm**: a little new material, then review, then a little more, with old material cycling back in. Review is built *between* the stages here, not saved for the end.

---

## Before Stage 1 (about 20 minutes)

Read these two short pages. They frame everything else:

1. [How to Read Without Translating](reference/how-to-read-without-translating.md)
2. [Active vs. Passive Vocabulary](reference/active-vs-passive-vocabulary.md)

Then skim [The A1 Priority System](reference/a1-priority-system.md) so you know which words are worth real effort now and which ones you only need to recognize.

## Stage 1 — roughly 1 week
### The most common small words

These appear in almost every conversation. Learn them **actively** — you should be able to produce them.

- [nicht](words/nicht.md)
- [kein](words/kein.md)
- [auch](words/auch.md)
- [noch](words/noch.md)
- [schon](words/schon.md)
- [nur](words/nur.md)

**Pace:** one word page per day is plenty. Reread the previous day's page for two minutes before starting a new one.

**Before moving to Stage 2, complete:**
- [Recognition 01](review/recognition-01.md) — do you understand what the sentence is describing?
- [Contrast 01](review/contrast-01.md) — can you tell the near-identical sentences apart?

If more than a third of your answers were wrong, don't push on. Reread the two or three pages that gave you trouble and take the review again in a day or two.

## Stage 2 — roughly 1–2 weeks
### Essential preposition patterns

- [mit](prepositions/mit.md)
- [zu](prepositions/zu.md)
- [nach](prepositions/nach.md)
- [bei](prepositions/bei.md)
- [aus](prepositions/aus.md)
- [von](prepositions/von.md)
- [für](prepositions/fuer.md)
- [um](prepositions/um.md)

Then, more slowly:

- [in](prepositions/in.md)
- [auf](prepositions/auf.md)
- [an](prepositions/an.md)

**Slow down here.** *mit, zu, nach, bei, aus, von, für* always take the same case, so once you know it you know it. But *in*, *auf*, and *an* are **two-way prepositions**: they switch case depending on whether you're describing where something *is* or where something is *going*. That distinction takes far more repetition than the earlier prepositions did — expect it to feel unstable for a while, and read [Cases at a Glance](reference/cases-at-a-glance.md) alongside those three pages.

**Pace:** two fixed-case prepositions per day is comfortable; give *in*, *auf*, and *an* a day each at minimum.

**Before moving to Stage 3, complete:**
- [Sentence Completion 01](review/sentence-completion-01.md) — the preposition-focused review
- **and revisit [Recognition 01](review/recognition-01.md)** from Stage 1, so those words don't go stale while your attention is on prepositions

## Stage 4 — ongoing; revisit rather than rush
### Conversational particles

- [mal](particles/mal.md)
- [denn](particles/denn.md)
- [doch](particles/doch.md)
- [eigentlich](particles/eigentlich.md)
- [ja](particles/ja.md)
- [eben](particles/eben.md) and [halt](particles/halt.md) — recognition only for now

Read [Modal Particles Overview](reference/modal-particles-overview.md) before the individual pages.

These are the **slowest words to internalize**, and that is normal. They carry tone rather than information, so there is nothing to memorize in the usual sense — you are waiting for a feel to develop. **Recognition first, production much later.** A learner who says *Komm doch mal mit!* correctly after six months is doing well; a learner who forces particles into sentences at week three usually sounds strange.

Stage 4 is not a finish line. While you work through it:

- Cycle back to [Recognition 01](review/recognition-01.md), [Contrast 01](review/contrast-01.md), and [Sentence Completion 01](review/sentence-completion-01.md) about once a week
- Add [Recognition 02](review/recognition-02.md) — the particle-focused review
- Read the [phrases](phrases/README.md) pages, especially [Conversation Patterns](phrases/conversation-patterns.md), whenever you want low-effort exposure
