# aus

**Type:** Preposition (**always dative**); also an adjective meaning "off"
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

[← bei](bei.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [Next → von](von.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

*aus* means **out of the inside of something** — you were in it, now you're coming out.

That covers both the physical sense (*aus dem Haus* — out of the house) and the one you'll use first: **where you're originally from**. Your home country is something you came out of.

**Case: always dative.**

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. Origin — where you're from

**Ich komme aus Amerika.**
I'm from America.

**Sie kommt aus Berlin.**
She's from Berlin.

**Woher kommen Sie? — Ich komme aus Kanada.**
Where are you from? — I'm from Canada.

This is the standard answer to *Woher kommst du?*, and one of the first full sentences worth owning.

### 2. Out of a physical space

**Er kommt aus dem Haus.**
He's coming out of the house.

**Ich steige aus dem Zug.**
I'm getting off the train.

**Nimm das Brot aus der Tasche.**
Take the bread out of the bag.

### 3. Made of

**Der Tisch ist aus Holz.**
The table is made of wood.

**Das Glas ist aus Plastik.**
The glass is made of plastic.

### 4. "Off" — as an adjective *(no case, not a preposition)*

**Das Licht ist aus.**
The light is off.

**Der Film ist aus.**
The film is over.

## Common Chunks

- **aus Deutschland / aus den USA** — from Germany / from the US
- **Ich komme aus …** — I'm from …
- **aus dem Fenster** — out of the window
- **aus Holz / aus Glas / aus Papier** — made of wood/glass/paper
- **Aus!** — Stop! / That's enough! *(also shouted at dogs)*

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich komme aus Irland.**
**Wir kommen aus einer kleinen Stadt.**
**Sie steigt aus dem Bus.**
**Das Hemd ist aus Baumwolle.**
**Das Licht ist aus.**

The first four all have something emerging from inside something else — a country, a town, a bus, a material.

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich komme.**
I'm coming.

**Ich komme aus Berlin.**
I'm from Berlin. *(origin — my roots)*

**Ich komme von der Arbeit.**
I'm coming from work. *(starting point of this trip)*

That last contrast is the one to get right: *aus* for where you're **from** in life, *von* for where you're coming **from right now**. See [aus vs. von](../comparisons/aus-vs-von.md).

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *von* for nationality.** *Ich komme von Amerika* → **aus Amerika**. English "from" covers both; German splits them.
- **Using *aus* for a person.** "I got it from my sister" is *von meiner Schwester*, not *aus*.
- **Forgetting the dative.** *aus die Schweiz* → **aus der Schweiz**.
- **Missing the "off" use.** *Der Film ist aus* doesn't mean the film is out — it means it's finished.

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** *Ich komme aus …* will be one of your most-used sentences as a beginner, and *aussteigen* ("get off") matters on public transport.

## You May Hear

- **Woher kommen Sie?** — Where are you from?
- **Bitte aussteigen!** — Please get off / alight.
- **Der Zug kommt aus München.** — The train is coming from Munich.
- **Ist das aus Deutschland?** — Is that from Germany?
