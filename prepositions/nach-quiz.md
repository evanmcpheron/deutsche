## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Ich fliege ___ Deutschland.*\
   a. zu\
   b. nach\
   c. in

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Ich fliege nach Deutschland.* (*Deutschland* takes no article.)

</details>

2. Fill in the pattern: *Ich gehe ___ Hause.* (I'm going home)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **nach**

**Explanation:** *Ich gehe nach Hause.*

</details>

3. **Nach dem Essen gehen wir spazieren.** What does *nach* mean here?\
   a. To\
   b. After\
   c. Toward

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** after.

</details>

4. Why is **Ich gehe nach dem Arzt** wrong for "I'm going to the doctor"? What should it be?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *nach* + a person means "after" them in time or sequence, and doctors are people. Destinations that are people take *zu*: **Ich gehe zum Arzt.**

**Explanation:** *nach* takes the dative and is used with cities, most countries without articles, directions, and “after.”

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm going to Munich" in German?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich fahre nach München.**

**Explanation:** *Ich fliege nach München* if flying.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** Tell someone to go left.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Gehen Sie nach links.**

**Explanation:** informal: *Geh nach links.*

</details>
