## Part A — Clock Time First

1. Your train leaves at 8:00. Which sentence gives the departure time?\
   a. Der Zug fährt um acht Uhr. \
   b. Der Zug fährt am acht Uhr. \
   c. Der Zug fährt im acht Uhr. \
   d. Der Zug fährt nach acht Uhr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Exact clock times take *um*: *um acht Uhr*. This is the most important A1 use of *um*.

</details>

2. Your appointment is on Monday at 9:00. Which sentence uses the time expressions correctly?\
   a. Der Termin ist um Montag am neun Uhr. \
   b. Der Termin ist im Montag um neun Uhr. \
   c. Der Termin ist am Montag um neun Uhr. \
   d. Der Termin ist am Montag im neun Uhr.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Days take *am* and clock times take *um*: *am Montag um neun Uhr*.

</details>

3. Someone asks when the film starts. Which question means “At what time?”\
   a. An wie viel Uhr? \
   b. Um wie viel Uhr? \
   c. Im wie viel Uhr? \
   d. Bei wie viel Uhr?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Um wie viel Uhr?* is the standard question chunk for asking at what clock time something happens.

</details>

4. You meet a friend at half past six. Which sentence is correct?\
   a. Wir treffen uns am halb sieben. \
   b. Wir treffen uns um halb sieben. \
   c. Wir treffen uns im halb sieben. \
   d. Wir treffen uns bei halb sieben.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Clock times use *um*: the correct German is **Wir treffen uns um halb sieben.**

</details>

5. Which sentence correctly says that a shop opens at nine?\
   a. Das Geschäft öffnet am neun. \
   b. Das Geschäft öffnet nach neun. \
   c. Das Geschäft öffnet um neun. \
   d. Das Geschäft öffnet im neun.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A clock time takes *um*: *Das Geschäft öffnet um neun.*

</details>

6. Your class is in July, not at a clock time. Which sentence is correct?\
   a. Der Kurs ist im Juli. \
   b. Der Kurs ist um Juli. \
   c. Der Kurs ist am Juli. \
   d. Der Kurs ist bei Juli.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Months take *im*: *im Juli*. *Um* is for clock times, not months.

</details>

7. A friend says **Ich komme so um acht.** What does the time phrase tell you?\
   a. They will come exactly at 8:00. \
   b. They will come after 8:00 only. \
   c. They will come before 8:00 only. \
   d. They will come around 8:00, approximately.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *so um acht* means approximately around eight. The exact minute is not fixed.

</details>

8. A small item costs **um die 20 Euro**. What does that price expression mean?\
   a. exactly 20 euros \
   b. less than 20 euros in every case \
   c. 20 euros per person \
   d. approximately 20 euros

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *um die 20 Euro* means “around 20 euros,” an approximate amount. The price is not necessarily exactly 20 euros.

</details>

## Part B — Around, Accusative, and Changing Trains

9. Four people are sitting around a table. Which sentence fits?\
   a. Wir sitzen um den Tisch. \
   b. Wir sitzen um dem Tisch. \
   c. Wir sitzen bei den Tisch. \
   d. Wir sitzen nach den Tisch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Physical “around” uses *um*, and *um* always takes accusative. Masculine *der Tisch* becomes *um den Tisch*.

</details>

10. A bus turns around the corner. Which phrase is the lesson-taught expression?\
   a. bei die Ecke \
   b. um die Ecke \
   c. nach die Ecke \
   d. von die Ecke

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *um die Ecke* means “around the corner.” This is the physical “around” use of *um*.

</details>

11. A learner writes **Wir sitzen um dem Tisch.** What is the best correction?\
   a. Wir sitzen bei dem Tisch. \
   b. Wir sitzen um der Tisch. \
   c. Wir sitzen nach dem Tisch. \
   d. Wir sitzen um den Tisch.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Because *um* always takes accusative, *der Tisch* becomes **den Tisch**: **Wir sitzen um den Tisch.**

</details>

12. Which statement about prepositional *um* is correct?\
   a. It always takes accusative. \
   b. It always takes dative. \
   c. It takes dative for time and accusative for place. \
   d. It never changes article forms.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** Prepositional *um* always takes accusative, whether it marks clock time or physical “around.”

</details>

13. At a train station you hear **In Köln müssen Sie umsteigen.** What must you do in Cologne?\
   a. Leave the station and end the trip. \
   b. Change trains. \
   c. Wait until eight o'clock. \
   d. Walk around the station.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *umsteigen* is a separable verb meaning “to change trains/buses.” In this sentence it means you need to transfer in Cologne.

</details>

14. Which sentence correctly combines a part of the day with a clock time?\
   a. Wir essen um Abend am sieben. \
   b. Wir essen am Abend um sieben. \
   c. Wir essen im Abend um sieben. \
   d. Wir essen am Abend im sieben.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A part of the day takes *am* (*am Abend*), while the exact clock time takes *um* (*um sieben*).

</details>

15. **Select all that apply.** Which gaps correctly take *um*?\
   a. Der Film beginnt ___ acht Uhr. \
   b. Wir sitzen ___ den Tisch. \
   c. Das kostet ___ die 20 Euro. \
   d. Wir treffen uns ___ Montag.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** *um* works with a clock time, physical “around,” and an approximate amount. A day takes *am*: *am Montag*.

</details>

## Part C — Travel and Everyday Interpretation

16. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Um wie viel Uhr beginnt der Film?
B: ___ acht Uhr.
```

   a. Im \
   b. Am \
   c. Um \
   d. Bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The answer gives an exact clock time, so it uses *um*: *um acht Uhr*.

</details>

17. At the station, an employee says **In Frankfurt bitte umsteigen.** Which action matches the instruction?\
   a. Stay on the same train to the end. \
   b. Buy a ticket at eight. \
   c. Change to another train in Frankfurt. \
   d. Walk around Frankfurt station.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *umsteigen* means to change trains or buses. The prefix *um-* here is part of the separable verb, not the clock-time preposition.

</details>

18. A learner wants to say “We meet on Tuesday” and writes **Wir treffen uns um Dienstag.** Which correction is best?\
   a. Wir treffen uns im Dienstag. \
   b. Wir treffen uns bei Dienstag. \
   c. Wir treffen uns nach Dienstag. \
   d. Wir treffen uns am Dienstag.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Days take *am*, not *um*: *am Dienstag*. Save *um* for an exact clock time such as *um neun Uhr*.

</details>

19. Your friend says **Treffen wir uns so um zehn?** Which reply shows the right interpretation?\
   a. “Okay, around ten; the exact minute is flexible.” \
   b. “Okay, exactly at 10:00 and not one minute later.” \
   c. “Okay, on the tenth day of the month.” \
   d. “Okay, for ten hours.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *so um zehn* gives an approximate clock time — around ten — rather than an exact minute.

</details>

20. Which sentence does **not** use the correct time expression?\
   a. Der Termin ist um elf Uhr. \
   b. Wir treffen uns am Freitag. \
   c. Der Kurs beginnt um Montag. \
   d. Ich bin im August in Berlin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** **Der Kurs beginnt um Montag** is the incorrect one; a day takes *am*: *am Montag*. The other sentences correctly use *um* for a clock time, *am* for a day, and *im* for a month.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

21. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

```text
Der Zug fährt ___ neun Uhr.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **um**

**Explanation:** Exact clock times take *um*: *um neun Uhr*.

</details>

22. Put the words in order.

```text
um halb sieben / wir / uns / treffen
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Wir treffen uns um halb sieben.**

**Explanation:** *um halb sieben* gives the clock time of the meeting.

</details>

23. Correct the sentence for the intended meaning: “We meet on Monday.”

```text
Wir treffen uns um Montag.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Wir treffen uns am Montag.**

**Explanation:** Days take *am*. *Um* is used for clock times: for example, *am Montag um acht Uhr*.

</details>

24. Correct the case error.

```text
Wir sitzen um dem Tisch.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Wir sitzen um den Tisch.**

**Explanation:** Prepositional *um* always takes accusative. Masculine *der Tisch* therefore becomes *den Tisch*.

</details>
