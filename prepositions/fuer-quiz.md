## Mini Review


1. Which is correct?\
   a. für dir\
   b. für dich\
   c. für du

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *für dich* (accusative).

</details>

2. Choose the natural word: *Ein Tisch ___ zwei Personen, bitte.*\
   a. für\
   b. zu\
   c. mit

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *für zwei Personen.*

</details>

3. **Für hier oder zum Mitnehmen?** Where would you hear this?\
   a. At a hotel reception.\
   b. At a café counter.\
   c. On a train.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** at a café or bakery counter.

</details>

4. Why do learners so often say *für mir*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Because most of the other high-frequency prepositions (*mit, zu, bei, von, aus, nach*) take dative, so the dative pronoun *mir* becomes a habit. *für* is one of the accusative ones: **für mich**.

**Explanation:** *für* always takes the accusative and commonly marks a recipient, purpose, or duration.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "that's for you" (informal) in German?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das ist für dich.**

**Explanation:** *für* always takes the accusative and commonly marks a recipient, purpose, or duration.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** Ask for a table for two.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Ein Tisch für zwei, bitte.**

**Explanation:** *für* always takes the accusative and commonly marks a recipient, purpose, or duration.

</details>
