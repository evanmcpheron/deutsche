## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Ich habe ___ Montag frei.*\
   a. um\
   b. am\
   c. in

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *am Montag.*

</details>

2. Choose the natural form: *Ich hänge das Bild an ___ Wand.*\
   a. der\
   b. die\
   c. dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *an die Wand* (movement → accusative).

</details>

3. **Wann kommt der Zug an?** Why is *an* at the end?\
   a. It's a preposition with no noun.\
   b. It's the prefix of the separable verb *ankommen*.\
   c. It's a typo.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** it belongs to *ankommen* ("to arrive"), which splits in a main clause.

</details>

4. Contrast: **Wir sitzen am Tisch.** / **Wir setzen uns an den Tisch.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** The first describes where we are (location, dative). The second describes the act of sitting down at the table (movement, accusative).

**Explanation:** *an* is a two-way preposition: location uses dative, while movement toward an edge, surface, or destination uses accusative.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'll call you tomorrow" (informal) in German?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich rufe dich morgen an.**

**Explanation:** *an* is a two-way preposition: location uses dative, while movement toward an edge, surface, or destination uses accusative.

</details>
