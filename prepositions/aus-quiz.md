# Quiz: aus

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

## Part A — Origin, Inside, or Material?

1. You meet someone in Germany and they ask about your home country. You grew up in Canada.\
   *Ich komme ___ Kanada.*\
   a. von\
   b. vom\
   c. aus\
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A lasting place of origin takes *aus*: *Ich komme aus Kanada.* Option a is the strongest trap because English uses “from” for both origin and a current starting point; German uses *von* for the latter.

</details>

2. The train stops, the doors open, and you step onto the platform.\
   *Ich steige ___ Zug.*\
   a. vom\
   b. von der\
   c. aus der\
   d. aus dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** You are coming out of the inside of *der Zug*, so German uses *aus* plus dative: *aus dem Zug*. Option a marks a starting point with *von*; it does not express coming out of the vehicle.

</details>

3. A shop label says the bowl is made of glass.\
   *Die Schale ist ___ Glas.* (die Schale = bowl)\
   a. von\
   b. mit\
   c. aus\
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Materials use *aus*: *aus Glas*, *aus Holz*, *aus Papier*. Option a is tempting because English may say “made of,” but possessive/source *von* is not the material construction.

</details>

4. You press the switch, but the room stays dark. Someone says **Das Licht ist aus.** What does *aus* mean here?\
   a. The light comes from outside.\
   b. The light is made of glass.\
   c. The light is out of the room.\
   d. The light is off.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Here *aus* is an adjective meaning “off,” not a preposition. Option c tries to force the physical “out of” idea onto a sentence where *aus* has no noun after it.

</details>

5. You tell someone where your friend is from. She grew up in Switzerland.\
   *Sie kommt ___ Schweiz.*\
   a. aus der\
   b. von der\
   c. aus die\
   d. aus den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *die Schweiz* keeps its article, and *aus* always takes dative, so *die* becomes *der*: *aus der Schweiz*. Option c keeps the accusative form and is the common case error.

</details>

6. Your bread is inside your bag. You ask your child to take it out.\
   *Nimm das Brot ___ Tasche.*\
   a. von der\
   b. aus die\
   c. bei der\
   d. aus der

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The bread moves from inside the bag to outside it, so this is *aus der Tasche*. Option a gives only a source or starting point; it loses the important “from inside” relationship.

</details>

7. At an international event, someone asks where your family comes from.\
   *Wir kommen ___ USA.*\
   a. aus den\
   b. von den\
   c. aus der\
   d. aus die

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** German treats *die USA* as plural, and dative plural after *aus* is *den*: *aus den USA*. Option b has the right dative article but the wrong preposition for lasting origin.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

8. A learner wants to say “The email is from Anna” and writes **Die E-Mail ist aus Anna.** Which judgment is best?\
   a. Correct and natural.\
   b. Incorrect — a person as the source takes *von*.\
   c. Correct, but only in formal German.\
   d. Correct, but it means Anna is inside the email.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A person who sends or gives something is a source marked by *von*: *Die E-Mail ist von Anna.* *Aus* is for origin, movement out of an interior, or material here; it cannot replace *von* before a person.

</details>

9. Which sentence is the normal standard-German way to say that a man is coming out of the house?\
   a. Er kommt vom Haus.\
   b. Er kommt aus Haus.\
   c. Er kommt aus dem Haus.\
   d. Er kommt zum Haus.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Coming from inside *das Haus* requires *aus dem Haus*. Option a means roughly “from the house” as a starting point and does not say he was inside; option b is missing the dative article.

</details>

10. You are introducing yourself and want to say that Berlin is your home city. A learner instead says **Ich fahre von Berlin nach Hamburg.** Which judgment is best?\
   a. Correct German, but it describes a journey from Berlin to Hamburg.\
   b. Correct and it expresses the intended home-city meaning.\
   c. Incorrect because *von* can never appear before a city.\
   d. Incorrect because a city always needs an article after *von*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Von Berlin nach Hamburg* is correct for the start and destination of a journey, but it does not tell where you are from in life. For the intended home-city meaning, use *Ich komme aus Berlin.*

</details>

11. In which sentence is *aus* **not** a preposition?\
   a. Ich komme aus Spanien.\
   b. Der Film ist aus.\
   c. Das Glas ist aus Plastik.\
   d. Er kommt aus dem Hotel.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** In *Der Film ist aus*, *aus* is an adjective meaning the film is over. In the other three sentences, *aus* introduces a place or material and therefore acts as a preposition.

</details>

12. A child is inside a hotel room and now comes into the hall. Which completion is correct?\
   *Das Kind kommt ___ Zimmer.*\
   a. aus das\
   b. von dem\
   c. aus dem\
   d. aus den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Das Zimmer* is neuter, and *aus* always takes dative: *aus dem Zimmer*. Option a uses the accusative article *das*; option b changes the meaning from “out of the inside” to a general starting point.

</details>

13. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does the gap need *aus*?\
   a. Der Ring ist ___ Gold.\
   b. Ich komme ___ Polen.\
   c. Die Nachricht ist ___ meinem Bruder. (die Nachricht = message)\
   d. Sie steigt ___ dem Auto.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** Material, lasting origin, and movement out of a vehicle all use *aus*. Option c has a person as the source of a message, so it needs *von meinem Bruder*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

14. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Woher kommen Sie?
B: Ich komme ___ Spanien.
```

   a. von\
   b. bei\
   c. aus\
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Woher kommen Sie?* asks for origin, and the standard answer is *Ich komme aus …*. Option a is the English-influenced mistake of using one German word for every kind of “from.”

</details>

15. Complete the instruction. Your keys are inside your jacket pocket.\
   *Nimm den Schlüssel ___ Tasche.*\
   a. aus der\
   b. von der\
   c. aus die\
   d. bei der

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The key comes from inside *die Tasche*, so *aus der Tasche*. Option b marks a source but not an interior, while option c forgets that *aus* requires dative.

</details>

16. A customer asks **Ist der Tisch aus Holz?** What are they asking about?\
   a. Where the table is now.\
   b. What material the table is made from.\
   c. Who owns the table.\
   d. Where the table's journey starts.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Aus Holz* identifies the material. A source person or owner would normally be expressed with *von*, so option c belongs to a different relationship.

</details>

17. You arrive late to a movie. Your friend says **Der Film ist aus.** What has happened?\
   a. The film comes from another country.\
   b. The film is over.\
   c. The film is made of paper.\
   d. The film is outside.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Der Film ist aus* means the film is finished. This is the adjective use of *aus*; it does not describe origin or movement out of a place.

</details>

18. Someone asks where you are from. You answer **Ich komme gerade von der Arbeit.** Why does the answer not fit the question?\
   a. *Arbeit* can never follow *von*.\
   b. *Gerade* cannot appear with *kommen*.\
   c. The sentence says where you live.\
   d. It gives today's starting point, not your lasting origin.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Von der Arbeit* tells where your current trip starts. For the biographical question “Where are you from?”, German uses *aus* plus a city or country, such as *Ich komme aus Köln.*

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You are introducing yourself. America is your home country.

```text
Ich komme ___ Amerika.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **aus**

**Explanation:** Lasting origin uses *aus*: *Ich komme aus Amerika.* A current departure point would use *von* instead.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing words. No options given.

   Your phone is inside your bag, and you take it out.

```text
Ich nehme das Handy ___ Tasche.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **aus der**

**Explanation:** The phone moves from inside *die Tasche* to outside it. *Aus* always takes dative, so feminine *die* becomes *der*.

</details>

21. Put the words in order.

```text
Holz / der Tisch / aus / ist
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Der Tisch ist aus Holz.**

**Explanation:** *Aus* plus a material tells what something is made of. No article is needed before *Holz* in this common pattern.

</details>

22. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich komme von der Arbeit.**\
   New situation: You are introducing yourself, and Berlin is your home city.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich komme aus Berlin.**

**Explanation:** The original sentence gives the starting point of today's trip. The new situation asks for lasting origin, so the preposition changes from *von* to *aus*.

</details>
