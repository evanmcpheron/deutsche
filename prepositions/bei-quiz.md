# Quiz: bei

## Part A — At Someone's Place, Work, or Nearby?

1. You are spending the afternoon at your mother's home.\
   *Ich bin ___ meiner Mutter.*\
   a. zu\
   b. bei\
   c. mit\
   d. von

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Being at someone's home uses *bei*: *bei meiner Mutter*. Option a is the movement word you would use while going there: *Ich gehe zu meiner Mutter.*

</details>

2. A form asks where you work. Your employer is a bank.\
   *Ich arbeite ___ einer Bank.*\
   a. mit\
   b. von\
   c. bei\
   d. zu

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** An employer or workplace takes *bei*: *bei einer Bank*. Option a would mean you work together with the bank as a partner, not that the bank employs you.

</details>

3. You are sitting in the waiting room and the doctor is about to see you.\
   *Ich bin ___ Arzt.*\
   a. zum\
   b. beim\
   c. mit dem\
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** You are already there, so use *bei dem Arzt* → *beim Arzt*. Option a, *zum Arzt*, is for movement toward the doctor's office.

</details>

4. Your hotel is only a short walk from the train station.\
   *Das Hotel ist ___ Bahnhof.*\
   a. zum\
   b. mit dem\
   c. beim\
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Bei* can mean “near/by,” and *bei dem Bahnhof* contracts to *beim Bahnhof*. Option d marks a source or starting point rather than a nearby location.

</details>

5. You read the newspaper while eating breakfast.\
   *___ Frühstück lese ich Zeitung.*\
   a. Zum\
   b. Mit dem\
   c. Vom\
   d. Beim

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Beim Frühstück* means “at/during breakfast.” Option b would make breakfast sound like an instrument or companion, which is not the relationship here.

</details>

6. A friend asks where the group should meet. You suggest your home.\
   *Wir treffen uns ___ mir.*\
   a. mit\
   b. zu\
   c. bei\
   d. von

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Bei mir* means “at my place.” Option a means “with me” and says who accompanies the group, not where the meeting happens.

</details>

7. You and a friend agree to meet at the bakery before walking downtown.\
   *Wir treffen uns ___ Bäcker.*\
   a. beim\
   b. mit dem\
   c. zum\
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A meeting location connected with a business takes *bei*: *beim Bäcker*. Option c, *zum Bäcker*, would describe going to the bakery rather than meeting there.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

8. You put on your coat and start walking to your mother's house. Which sentence fits now?\
   a. Ich bin bei meiner Mutter.\
   b. Ich gehe bei meiner Mutter.\
   c. Ich gehe mit meiner Mutter.\
   d. Ich gehe zu meiner Mutter.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Bei* is static, so movement toward a person needs *zu*: *Ich gehe zu meiner Mutter.* Option a becomes correct after you arrive.

</details>

9. You and your friend walk to the cinema together. Which sentence expresses the companion relationship?\
   a. Ich gehe mit meinem Freund ins Kino.\
   b. Ich bin bei meinem Freund.\
   c. Ich gehe zu meinem Freund.\
   d. Ich komme von meinem Freund.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A person who accompanies you takes *mit*: *mit meinem Freund*. *Bei* places you at someone's home or in their orbit; it does not mean “together with.”

</details>

10. You are already in the doctor's office. Which sentence is the most natural everyday German?\
   a. Ich bin bei Arzt.\
   b. Ich bin mit dem Arzt.\
   c. Ich bin zum Arzt.\
   d. Ich bin beim Arzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The normal expression is *beim Arzt*, the contraction of *bei dem Arzt*. Option c uses *zum*, which belongs to the journey there, not the location after arrival.

</details>

11. You mean that Siemens is your employer. Which sentence says that?\
   a. Ich arbeite mit Siemens.\
   b. Ich arbeite bei Siemens.\
   c. Ich arbeite von Siemens.\
   d. Ich arbeite zu Siemens.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Employers take *bei*: *Ich arbeite bei Siemens.* Option a is grammatical but different: it makes Siemens a partner you work together with.

</details>

12. Which sentence uses *bei* to mean **during an activity**?\
   a. Beim Kochen höre ich Musik.\
   b. Ich bin bei meiner Schwester.\
   c. Das Café ist beim Bahnhof.\
   d. Ich arbeite bei einer Firma.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Beim Kochen* means “while cooking.” The other three are location or association uses: at someone's place, near a station, or at an employer.

</details>

13. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does the gap need *bei*?\
   a. Im Sommer bin ich ___ meinen Großeltern.\
   b. Sie arbeitet ___ einer kleinen Firma.\
   c. Ich fahre ___ dem Bus zur Arbeit.\
   d. Wir treffen uns ___ mir.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** Someone's home, an employer, and “at my place” all take *bei*. Option c asks how you travel, so it needs *mit dem Bus*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

14. Complete the dialogue. Anna is at home and you are visiting her.

```text
A: Wo bist du?
B: Ich bin ___ Anna.
```

   a. mit\
   b. zu\
   c. von\
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The question asks *Wo?* — where you are — so *bei Anna* gives the location at her place. *Zu Anna* would answer *Wohin?* instead.

</details>

15. Complete the dialogue. B is leaving home now for a medical appointment.

```text
A: Wohin gehst du?
B: Ich gehe ___ Arzt.
```

   a. beim\
   b. mit dem\
   c. zum\
   d. vom

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Movement toward the doctor takes *zu dem* → *zum Arzt*. *Beim Arzt* is the matching location phrase for after B arrives.

</details>

16. Two friends are deciding where to spend the evening. One asks **Bei mir oder bei dir?** What are they choosing?\
   a. Which bus to take.\
   b. Who pays for dinner.\
   c. Who travels with whom.\
   d. Whose home they will be at.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Bei mir* and *bei dir* mean “at my place” and “at your place.” Option c would use *mit mir / mit dir* and would be about companionship, not location.

</details>

17. A train announcement says **Vorsicht beim Aussteigen!** What relationship does *beim* express?\
   a. during the activity of getting off\
   b. movement toward a person\
   c. travelling with another person\
   d. a starting point

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Beim Aussteigen* means “while getting off.” This is the activity/during use of *bei*, not the location-at-a-person use.

</details>

18. Someone says **Das Hotel ist beim Bahnhof.** What does that tell you?\
   a. The hotel is inside the station.\
   b. The hotel is near the station.\
   c. The hotel travels with the station.\
   d. The hotel is leaving from the station.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Beim Bahnhof* can mean “near/by the station.” Option a would require a relationship of being inside, which *bei* does not express.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. Fill in the missing words. No options given.

   You are at your friend's home.

```text
Ich bin ___ Freund.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **bei meinem**

**Explanation:** Someone's home takes *bei*, and *der Freund* becomes dative *meinem Freund* after the preposition.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You listen to music while eating.

```text
___ Essen höre ich Musik.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Beim**

**Explanation:** *Beim Essen* means “while eating / at the meal.” It is the contraction of *bei dem Essen*.

</details>

21. Put the words in order.

```text
bei einer kleinen Firma / arbeitet / sie
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Sie arbeitet bei einer kleinen Firma.**

**Explanation:** *Bei* marks the company as her employer, and the feminine dative phrase is *bei einer kleinen Firma*.

</details>

22. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich gehe zu meiner Schwester.**\
   New situation: You have arrived and are now at your sister's home.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin bei meiner Schwester.**

**Explanation:** The original uses *zu* for movement toward her place. Once the situation becomes static location, the preposition changes to *bei*.

</details>
