## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Ich bin ___ meiner Mutter.* (I'm at my mother's)\
   a. zu\
   b. bei\
   c. nach

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *bei* for location.

</details>

2. Choose the natural word: *Ich gehe ___ meiner Mutter.* (I'm going to my mother's)\
   a. zu\
   b. bei\
   c. nach

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *zu* for movement toward a person.

</details>

3. **Er ist beim Arzt.** What's the situation?\
   a. He's on his way to the doctor.\
   b. He's at the doctor's now.\
   c. He's a doctor.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** he's there now. (*zum Arzt* would be on the way.)

</details>

4. What's wrong with **Ich gehe bei meinem Freund** if you mean "I'm going with my friend"?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *bei* means "at someone's place," not "together with." For accompaniment use *mit*: **Ich gehe mit meinem Freund.**

**Explanation:** *bei* takes the dative and commonly means at someone’s place, near, or during a situation.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I work at a bank" in German, using *bei*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Ich arbeite bei einer Bank.**

**Explanation:** *bei* takes the dative and commonly means at someone’s place, near, or during a situation.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** How would you say "we're meeting at my place"?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Wir treffen uns bei mir.**

**Explanation:** *bei* takes the dative and commonly means at someone’s place, near, or during a situation.

</details>
