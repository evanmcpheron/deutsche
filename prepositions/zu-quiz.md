## Part A — Destination, Fixed Phrase, or "Too"?

1. Your friend Lena has invited you to her apartment after lunch. Which sentence fits?\
   a. Ich gehe nach Lena. \
   b. Ich gehe zu Lena. \
   c. Ich gehe in Lena. \
   d. Ich gehe bei Lena.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** People take *zu*: *zu Lena*. *Nach Lena* would not mean “to Lena.”

</details>

2. You are at a hotel and ask how to get to the train station. Which question is most natural?\
   a. Wie komme ich nach dem Bahnhof? \
   b. Wie komme ich bei dem Bahnhof? \
   c. Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof? \
   d. Wie komme ich aus dem Bahnhof?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A normal everyday destination such as *der Bahnhof* takes *zu*. *Zu dem* contracts to *zum*: *zum Bahnhof*.

</details>

3. A jacket costs much more than you want to pay. You say **Die Jacke ist zu teuer.** What does *zu* do here?\
   a. It marks the destination. \
   b. It means the jacket is at home. \
   c. It makes *teuer* excessive: “too expensive.” \
   d. It shows that the jacket belongs to someone.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Before an adjective, *zu* can mean “too”: *zu teuer*. This is a separate job from the preposition *zu*.

</details>

4. You do not have a car, and the bakery is close. Which sentence says that you are going on foot?\
   a. Ich gehe zum Fuß. \
   b. Ich gehe nach Fuß. \
   c. Ich gehe mit Fuß. \
   d. Ich gehe zu Fuß.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *zu Fuß* is a fixed expression meaning “on foot.” Do not add an article.

</details>

5. A delivery driver asks whether you will be home this afternoon. Which answer fits?\
   a. Ja, ich bin nach Hause. \
   b. Ja, ich bin zum Haus. \
   c. Ja, ich bin bei Hause. \
   d. Ja, ich bin zu Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *zu Hause* means “at home” and describes location. *Nach Hause* is used for movement toward home.

</details>

6. You arrive at the station at 9:02. Your train left at 9:00. Which sentence fits?\
   a. Es ist zu spät. \
   b. Es ist zum spät. \
   c. Es ist nach spät. \
   d. Es ist bei spät.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *zu spät* means “too late.” Here *zu* comes before an adjective and expresses excess.

</details>

7. Your child leaves home each morning for school. Which sentence is the natural everyday German choice?\
   a. Mein Kind geht nach der Schule. \
   b. Mein Kind geht bei der Schule. \
   c. Mein Kind geht zur Schule. \
   d. Mein Kind geht aus der Schule.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *die Schule* is a normal destination, so use *zu*. *Zu der* contracts to *zur*: *zur Schule*.

</details>

## Part B — Tell the Jobs Apart

8. A learner wants to say “I am going to my brother’s” and writes **Ich gehe nach meinem Bruder.** Which judgment is best?\
   a. Correct and natural for the intended meaning. \
   b. German can use this wording for “after my brother,” but it does not mean “to my brother’s.” \
   c. Correct only if the brother is in another city. \
   d. Correct only with *fahren* instead of *gehen*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** For a person, the destination form is *zu meinem Bruder*. *Nach meinem Bruder* can express sequence — “after my brother” — so it is not the intended meaning.

</details>

9. You are leaving now because you need to go to your doctor. Which sentence directly expresses that destination?\
   a. Ich gehe zum Arzt. \
   b. Ich komme vom Arzt. \
   c. Ich bin beim Arzt. \
   d. Ich gehe nach dem Arzt.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *zum Arzt* is the destination: *zu dem Arzt* contracts to *zum Arzt*. The other choices mean “from the doctor,” “at the doctor,” or “after the doctor.”

</details>

10. In which sentence is *zu* **not** a preposition?\
   a. Ich fahre zu meiner Schwester. \
   b. Wir gehen zum Museum. \
   c. Sie geht zur Arbeit. \
   d. Der Kaffee ist zu heiß.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** In *zu heiß*, *zu* means “too” and modifies the adjective. In the other sentences, it introduces a destination.

</details>

11. You are visiting your sister. Which phrase has the correct form after *zu*?\
   a. zu meine Schwester \
   b. zu meiner Schwester \
   c. zu meinen Schwester \
   d. zu meinem Schwester

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Prepositional *zu* always takes dative. With feminine *meine Schwester*, dative is *meiner Schwester*.

</details>

12. A learner writes **Ich gehe zum Arbeit.** Which correction is best?\
   a. Ich gehe nach Arbeit. \
   b. Ich gehe zu Arbeit. \
   c. Ich gehe beim Arbeit. \
   d. Ich gehe zur Arbeit.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *die Arbeit* is feminine: *zu der Arbeit* contracts to *zur Arbeit*. *Zum* is used with *zu dem*.

</details>

13. Your friend puts six spoonfuls of sugar in one cup of coffee. You say **Das ist zu viel Zucker.** What does *zu viel* mean?\
   a. More than is wanted or appropriate. \
   b. Sugar from a particular place. \
   c. Sugar going toward someone. \
   d. Exactly enough sugar.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *zu viel* means “too much.” This is the same excessive *zu* as in *zu teuer* and *zu heiß*.

</details>

14. Which sentence is the natural fixed expression for “I am at home”?\
   a. Ich bin beim Haus. \
   b. Ich bin im Haus. \
   c. Ich bin zu Hause. \
   d. Ich bin nach Hause.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *zu Hause* is a fixed expression with no article. *Nach Hause* is the corresponding movement expression: “homeward / going home.”

</details>

15. **Select all that apply.** Which gaps need a form of *zu*?\
   a. Ich fahre ___ meinem Vater. \
   b. Wir fliegen ___ Berlin. \
   c. Ich gehe ___ Supermarkt. \
   d. Sie geht ___ Post.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, c, d**

**Explanation:** People and ordinary daily destinations take *zu*: *zu meinem Vater*, *zum Supermarkt*, *zur Post*. A city takes *nach*: *nach Berlin*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

16. Complete the dialogue. Your friend asks whether you are coming to her place tonight.

```text
A: Kommst du heute Abend ___ mir?
B: Ja, gern.
```

   a. nach \
   b. zu \
   c. bei \
   d. aus

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A person as the destination takes *zu*: *zu mir* means “to my place / to me.”

</details>

17. Complete the dialogue. You take one sip and cannot drink the coffee yet.

```text
A: Ist der Kaffee gut?
B: Ja, aber er ist ___ heiß.
```

   a. zum \
   b. zu \
   c. nach \
   d. zur

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Before the adjective *heiß*, bare *zu* means “too”: *zu heiß*. *Zum* and *zur* are destination contractions, so they do not fit here.

</details>

18. You are on the street and need the museum. Which question is most natural?\
   a. Wie komme ich zum Museum? \
   b. Wie komme ich nach dem Museum? \
   c. Wie komme ich beim Museum? \
   d. Wie komme ich vom Museum?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *das Museum* is a normal destination: *zu dem Museum* → *zum Museum*.

</details>

19. A friend says **Ich gehe jeden Morgen zu Fuß zur Arbeit.** What does *zu Fuß* tell you?\
   a. The friend works at home. \
   b. The friend is going to a foot doctor. \
   c. The friend walks instead of using transport. \
   d. The friend arrives too early.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *zu Fuß* is the fixed expression “on foot.” The second *zu* in *zur Arbeit* has the separate destination job.

</details>

20. Your train is delayed, but you still catch your connection. You say **Zum Glück!** What are you expressing?\
   a. Relief: “Luckily / fortunately.” \
   b. A destination: “to happiness.” \
   c. That the train is too fast. \
   d. That you are going home.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *zum Glück* is a fixed expression meaning “luckily / fortunately.” Learn it as a chunk.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

21. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You have a medical appointment this afternoon.

```text
Ich muss heute Nachmittag ___ Arzt.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **zum**

**Explanation:** A person/professional destination takes *zu*, and *der Arzt* gives *zu dem* → *zum*.

</details>

22. Put the words in order.

```text
am Samstag / zu / fahre / meinen Eltern / ich
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich fahre am Samstag zu meinen Eltern.**

**Explanation:** *zu* is required for people, and it takes dative: *meinen Eltern*.

</details>

23. Rewrite the sentence so that the price is more than you are willing to pay.

   Original: **Das Hotel ist teuer.**

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das Hotel ist zu teuer.**

**Explanation:** Adding *zu* before the adjective changes “expensive” to “too expensive.”

</details>

24. Correct the sentence for the intended meaning: “I am at home.”

```text
Ich bin nach Hause.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich bin zu Hause.**

**Explanation:** *zu Hause* is location. *Nach Hause* is used when someone is moving home: *Ich gehe nach Hause.*

</details>
