# Quiz: auf

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

## Part A — On It, or Onto It?

1. You come into the kitchen in the morning and see your keys exactly where you dropped them last night.\
   *Der Schlüssel liegt ___ Tisch.*\
   a. auf den\
   b. auf die\
   c. auf das\
   d. auf dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *liegen* is a resting verb and the keys aren't going anywhere → dative, and *der Tisch* is masculine → *dem*. Option a is the form you'd need the moment somebody picks them up and puts them down again.

</details>

2. You're clearing the sofa so a visitor can sit down, and the book in your hand has to go somewhere.\
   *Ich lege das Buch ___ Tisch.*\
   a. auf den\
   b. auf dem\
   c. auf der\
   d. in den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *legen* moves the book → accusative *den*. Option b is the single most common error with this preposition; option d would have the book disappearing inside the table.

</details>

3. The cat jumped up ten minutes ago and hasn't moved since.\
   *Die Katze sitzt ___ Sofa.*\
   a. auf das\
   b. auf dem\
   c. auf der\
   d. auf den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *sitzen* belongs to the resting group, so dative — and *das Sofa* is neuter → *dem*. Option a is what the jump itself needed: *Die Katze springt auf das Sofa.*

</details>

4. You're in a shop in Hamburg and you don't know what the thing you want is called.\
   *Wie sagt man das ___?*\
   a. auf Deutsch\
   b. in Deutsch\
   c. im Deutsch\
   d. auf dem Deutsch

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A fixed pattern with no article and no case decision to make. Option b is the word-for-word rendering of English "in German" and the mistake to expect from yourself for a while.

</details>

5. You need stamps and the counter closes at five, so you set off with your coat half on.\
   *Ich gehe schnell ___ Post.*\
   a. in die\
   b. auf der\
   c. auf die\
   d. zu die

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The post office is one of the destinations that idiomatically take *auf*, and going there is accusative → *auf die Post*. Option b is the same errand once you've arrived. There's no rule to derive this group from; learn the phrases whole.

</details>

6. The meeting room is stuffy and you ask the colleague nearest the glass to do something about it.\
   *Mach bitte das Fenster ___.*\
   a. an\
   b. in\
   c. aus\
   d. auf

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *aufmachen* is a separable verb and its prefix lands at the end of the sentence. There's no noun for *auf* to govern here, so no case question arises. Option c would belong to *ausmachen*, which switches things off.

</details>

7. It's Sunday and you walk past a shop with its lights on and customers going in and out.\
   *Die Bäckerei ist ___.* (die Bäckerei = bakery)\
   a. an\
   b. in\
   c. auf\
   d. auf dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Everyday German uses *auf* as a plain adjective for "open" — *geöffnet* is the version you'll see written on the door. Nothing follows it, which rules out option d before you even consider the meaning.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

8. A learner writes **Ich lege das Buch auf dem Tisch.**\
   a. Correct.\
   b. Correct, but unusual.\
   c. Correct German, but it means something different.\
   d. Incorrect German — here you need *auf den Tisch*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** This isn't a shade of meaning. *legen* always moves its object, so the dative simply isn't available and the sentence has nowhere to land. Swap the verb to *liegt* and *auf dem Tisch* becomes right immediately.

</details>

9. Compare **Das Buch ist im Regal** and **Das Buch ist auf dem Regal.** What is the difference?\
   a. There is no difference.\
   b. In one sentence the book is inside; in the other it is on top.\
   c. One describes location; the other describes movement there.\
   d. The first sentence is incorrect.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Both are dative and both are positions — the preposition alone does the work. Option c is the distinction you'd get from changing the case, not the preposition. English says "on the shelf" for both, which is exactly why the pair blurs.

</details>

10. You're texting a friend from your seat on the way to work.\
   a. Ich bin im Bus.\
   b. Ich bin auf dem Bus.\
   c. Ich bin an dem Bus.\
   d. Ich bin auf den Bus.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** English "on the bus" is *im Bus* in German — you're inside it. Option b puts you on the roof. English "on" spreads across several German prepositions: *am Montag*, *im Fernsehen*, *auf dem Tisch*.

</details>

11. Which sentence does **not** describe something resting on a surface?\
   a. Die Tasse steht auf dem Tisch.\
   b. Der Schlüssel liegt auf dem Regal.\
   c. Wir warten auf dich.\
   d. Die Katze sitzt auf dem Stuhl.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *warten auf* is a fixed verb-plus-preposition pair, and nothing is on top of anything. You'll meet it as a whole chunk — *Warten Sie auf mich!* — rather than as a case decision.

</details>

12. A friend holds up a novel and says **Das Buch ist auf Deutsch.** What does that tell you?\
   a. Das Buch kommt aus Deutschland.\
   b. Man liest das Buch auf Deutsch, nicht auf Englisch.\
   c. Das Buch liegt oben.\
   d. Das Buch ist geöffnet.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *auf* plus a language says what language something is in. Option d reaches for the other *auf* — the "open" one — which never has a noun after it, and option c reads the preposition literally when it isn't being used literally.

</details>

13. **Ich stehe um sechs auf.** Why is *auf* at the end?\
   a. It belongs with *sechs*.\
   b. It is an error.\
   c. It is the separated prefix of *aufstehen*.\
   d. It is a preposition without a noun.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *aufstehen* splits in a main clause and the prefix goes to the very end. Option d describes what it looks like but not what it is: a preposition would still be pointing at something, and this one isn't.

</details>

14. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences is there no surface involved at all?\
   a. Wie heißt das auf Deutsch?\
   b. Der Laden ist auf.\
   c. Die Tasse steht auf dem Tisch.\
   d. Auf jeden Fall!

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, d**

**Explanation:** Three separate jobs — naming a language, saying "open", and a fixed phrase meaning "definitely". Only option c is the literal *auf* with something sitting on top of something else.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

15. Complete the dialogue. B can see them from where she's standing.

```text
A: Wo ist meine Brille? (die Brille = glasses)
B: Sie liegt ___ Tisch.
```

   a. auf dem\
   b. auf den\
   c. auf die\
   d. in dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A asked *Wo?* and *liegen* points the same way — dative. Option b would be right if B were putting them there rather than spotting them, and option d would have them inside the table.

</details>

16. Complete the dialogue. B is setting the table for dinner.

```text
A: Wohin soll ich die Teller stellen? (der Teller = plate)
B: ___ Tisch, bitte.
```

   a. Auf dem\
   b. Im\
   c. In den\
   d. Auf den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *wohin* and *stellen* both force the accusative, and *der Tisch* is masculine → *den*. Option c gets the case right and the preposition wrong: plates go on top of a table, not inside it.

</details>

17. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Was machst du am Samstag?
B: Ich gehe ___ Markt. Da gibt es frisches Obst.
```

   a. auf dem\
   b. in den\
   c. zu den\
   d. auf den

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The market belongs to the same idiomatic group as the post office, and B is going there → accusative *auf den Markt*. Option a is where you already are once you've arrived — *auf dem Markt* — which wouldn't answer A's question.

</details>

18. Complete the dialogue. B is free that evening and likes the idea.

```text
A: Kommst du mit ins Kino?
B: ___!
```

   a. Auf Wiedersehen\
   b. Auf jeden Fall\
   c. Auf Deutsch\
   d. Auf der Straße

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A fixed chunk meaning "definitely", and one more place where *auf* carries no spatial sense whatever. Learn these as whole phrases; taking them apart word by word tells you nothing useful.

</details>

19. A neighbour says **Der Laden ist auf.** Which description fits?\
   a. Man kann jetzt einkaufen.\
   b. Der Laden ist geschlossen.\
   c. Der Laden liegt oben.\
   d. Der Laden ist auf der Straße.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *auf* as an adjective means open — the formal equivalent on signs is *geöffnet*. Option c takes the spatial reading, which needs a noun after the preposition, and there isn't one.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

20. A learner writes **Ich stelle die Tasse auf dem Regal.** What has gone wrong?\
   a. *stellen* does not work with *auf*.\
   b. *das Regal* does not need an article.\
   c. *stellen* moves the cup to a new location — here you need *auf das Regal*.\
   d. The sentence is correct.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** The verb decides before the meaning does: *stellen* is in the movement group, so the article has to be accusative. Change the verb to *steht* and the dative in the original becomes correct without touching anything else.

</details>

21. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   Your friend can't find her phone. You can see it from the doorway, sitting next to the books.

```text
Das Handy liegt ___ Regal.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **auf dem**

**Explanation:** Neuter noun, resting verb, position → dative *dem*. *im Regal* is perfectly good German too, but it would put the phone inside the shelf unit rather than on top of it.

</details>

22. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   A visitor points at something in your kitchen and you want to know the German word for it.

```text
Wie heißt das ___?
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **auf Deutsch**

**Explanation:** No article, no case, nothing to decide — the whole phrase is frozen. *in Deutsch* is the version your English ear will offer you, and it's the one to keep catching.

</details>

23. Put the words in order.

```text
auf den / die Katze / springt / Stuhl
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Die Katze springt auf den Stuhl.**

**Explanation:** Verb in second position. *springen* is a movement, so the accusative *den Stuhl* is the only option; the same sentence with *sitzt* would need *auf dem Stuhl* instead.

</details>

24. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Die Tasse steht auf dem Tisch.**\
   New situation: you're wiping the table down, so you pick the cup up and put it on the shelf.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich stelle die Tasse auf das Regal.**

**Explanation:** Two things move together: the resting verb *stehen* becomes the movement verb *stellen*, and the dative becomes accusative. *Ich stelle die Tasse ins Regal* is equally good German if it goes inside the shelf. Leaving *auf dem* in place is the classic version of this mistake.

</details>
