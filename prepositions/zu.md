# zu

**Type:** Preposition (**always dative**); also an adverb meaning "too"
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — very high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

[← mit](mit.md) | [Category Index](./README.md) | [Next → nach](nach.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

As a preposition, *zu* means **heading toward a person or a place you'll enter or arrive at**. It is the default "to" for people and for most named places in your daily life.

As a separate word entirely, *zu* also means **"too"** in the sense of excessive: *zu teuer*, *zu spät*. Same spelling, unrelated job — and both are extremely common.

**Case: always dative.** No exceptions.

**Contractions you'll see constantly:**
- *zu dem* → **zum** (masculine and neuter): **zum Arzt**, **zum Bahnhof**
- *zu der* → **zur** (feminine): **zur Arbeit**, **zur Schule**

These aren't slang. Writing *zu dem Arzt* sounds stilted.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. To a person

**Ich gehe zu meiner Mutter.**
I'm going to my mother's.

**Kommst du zu mir?**
Are you coming to my place?

**Ich muss zum Arzt.**
I have to go to the doctor.

People always take *zu* — never *nach*. This is the single most reliable preposition rule at A1.

### 2. To a place or destination

**Ich fahre zum Bahnhof.**
I'm going to the station.

**Wie komme ich zum Museum?**
How do I get to the museum?

**Sie geht zur Arbeit.**
She's going to work.

### 3. In fixed expressions

**Ich bin zu Hause.**
I'm at home. *(note: no article — a fixed expression)* → [zu Hause vs. nach Hause](../comparisons/zu-hause-vs-nach-hause.md)

**zum Beispiel**
for example

**zum Glück**
luckily, fortunately

### 4. As "too" — excessive

**Das ist zu teuer.**
That's too expensive.

**Es ist zu spät.**
It's too late.

**Der Kaffee ist zu heiß.**
The coffee is too hot.

Here *zu* has nothing to do with direction. Judge by what follows: *zu* + adjective = "too"; *zu* + person or place = "to."

## Common Chunks

- **zu Hause** — at home
- **zum Arzt, zum Bahnhof, zum Supermarkt** — to the doctor/station/supermarket
- **zur Arbeit, zur Schule, zur Post** — to work/school/the post office
- **zum Beispiel** *(abbreviated **z. B.**)* — for example
- **zum Glück** — luckily
- **zu Fuß** — on foot: *Ich gehe zu Fuß.*
- **zu viel / zu wenig** — too much / too little

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich gehe zum Arzt.**
**Ich gehe zur Bank.**
**Ich fahre zu meinem Bruder.**
**Wie komme ich zum Hotel?**
**Kommst du zu uns?**

Then the other *zu*:

**Das ist zu laut.**
**Du fährst zu schnell.**
**Es ist zu früh.**

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich gehe.**
I'm going / I'm leaving.

**Ich gehe zum Arzt.**
I'm going to the doctor.

And the pair that matters most:

**Ich bin zu Hause.** — I'm at home. *(location)*
**Ich gehe nach Hause.** — I'm going home. *(movement)*

Two different prepositions, two different situations, one fixed pair worth memorizing whole.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Assuming *zu* always means "to."** *zu teuer* means "too expensive." Look at what follows.
- **Using *nach* for people.** *Ich gehe nach meiner Mutter* is wrong — **zu meiner Mutter**. See [zu vs. nach](../comparisons/zu-vs-nach.md).
- **Avoiding contractions.** *zu dem Bahnhof* is grammatical but sounds odd; say **zum Bahnhof**.
- **Saying *nach Hause* when you mean *zu Hause*.** Being home vs. going home — [full page](../comparisons/zu-hause-vs-nach-hause.md).
- **Adding an article to *zu Hause*.** It's a frozen expression; there's no *zu dem Hause*.

## Active or Passive?

**Active** in every use above. *zum Bahnhof*, *zu Hause*, and *zu teuer* are among the highest-frequency things you'll say.

## You May Hear

- **Wie komme ich zum Bahnhof?** — How do I get to the station?
- **Zum Mitnehmen oder zum Hieressen?** — To take away or eat in?
- **Das ist mir zu teuer.** — That's too expensive for me.
- **Zum Wohl!** — Cheers!
