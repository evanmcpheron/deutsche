# an

**Type:** Preposition — **two-way** (dative *or* accusative)
**Level:** A1–A2
**Priority:** Tier 2
**Knowledge goal:** Recognition First

[← auf](auf.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

*an* means **right up against something** — touching a vertical surface, sitting at an edge, or directed at a person.

Roughly: *auf* is **on top of**, *in* is **inside**, *an* is **up against or at the edge of**.

Like [in](in.md) and [auf](auf.md), *an* is a **two-way preposition**: dative for a location, accusative for a movement toward.

*an* is also how German marks **days and parts of the day**: *am Montag*, *am Abend*.

## Location vs. Movement

**The test: ask a question.**

- If the sentence answers **Wo?** ("where is it?") → **dative**
- If the sentence answers **Wohin?** ("where is it going?") → **accusative**

### Pair 1 — the wall

**Location (dative):**
**Das Bild hängt an der Wand.**
The picture is hanging on the wall.

**Movement (accusative):**
**Ich hänge das Bild an die Wand.**
I'm hanging the picture on the wall.

### Pair 2 — the table

**Location (dative):**
**Der Stuhl steht am Tisch.**
The chair is standing at the table. *(am = an dem)*

**Movement (accusative):**
**Ich stelle den Stuhl an den Tisch.**
I'm putting the chair at the table.

### Pair 3 — the window

**Location (dative):**
**Wir sitzen am Fenster.**
We're sitting by the window.

**Movement (accusative):**
**Wir setzen uns ans Fenster.**
We're sitting down by the window. *(ans = an das)*

**Contractions:** *an dem* → **am**, *an das* → **ans**.

See [Cases at a Glance](../reference/cases-at-a-glance.md) for the article tables and the same test explained on its own.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. Days and parts of the day — always dative, always *am*

**am Montag** — on Monday
**am Wochenende** — at the weekend
**am Abend** — in the evening
**am Morgen** — in the morning

This is the *an* you'll use most as a beginner, and it needs no case decision at all — it's frozen as *am*.

### 2. Up against a surface or edge

**Das Bild hängt an der Wand.**
The picture is on the wall.

**Wir sitzen am Tisch.**
We're sitting at the table.

### 3. Directed at a person *(accusative)*

**Ich schreibe eine E-Mail an meine Schwester.**
I'm writing an email to my sister.

**Eine Frage an Sie:**
A question for you:

### 4. As a separable prefix — *anfangen, ankommen, anrufen*

**Der Film fängt um acht an.**
The film starts at eight.

**Wann kommt der Zug an?**
When does the train arrive?

**Ich rufe dich an.**
I'll call you.

These three verbs are extremely common, and in all of them *an* is part of the verb rather than a preposition.

## Common Chunks

- **am Montag, am Wochenende, am Abend** — time expressions
- **am Tisch / am Fenster** — at the table / by the window
- **an der Wand** — on the wall
- **am Bahnhof** — at the station
- **anrufen, ankommen, anfangen** — call, arrive, begin

## Pattern Recognition

| Wo? (dative) | Wohin? (accusative) |
|---|---|
| Das Bild hängt **an der** Wand. | Ich hänge das Bild **an die** Wand. |
| Wir sitzen **am** Tisch. | Wir setzen uns **an den** Tisch. |
| Er steht **am** Fenster. | Er geht **ans** Fenster. |

## What Changes Without It?

**Das Bild ist an der Wand.** — against the wall *(hanging on it)*
**Das Bild ist auf dem Tisch.** — on top of the table
**Das Bild ist in der Tasche.** — inside the bag

Three prepositions, three positions. English uses "on" for the first two, which is exactly why they blur together for English speakers.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *auf* for walls.** A picture on a wall is *an der Wand*, not *auf der Wand*.
- **Using *um* for days.** *um Montag* → **am Montag**. Clock times take *um*; days take *am*.
- **Defaulting to dative for movement.** *Ich hänge das Bild an der Wand* → **an die Wand**.
- **Missing the separable verbs.** *Der Film fängt um acht an* has *an* at the end because of *anfangen* — nothing to do with prepositions.

## Active or Passive?

**Recognition First** overall, with one exception: **am + day/time** is fully Active and should be automatic (*am Montag*, *am Abend*). The location/movement contrast for *an* can settle later, once [in](in.md) and [auf](auf.md) feel stable.

## You May Hear

- **Am Montag habe ich Zeit.** — I have time on Monday.
- **Ich rufe Sie morgen an.** — I'll call you tomorrow.
- **Der Zug kommt um zehn an.** — The train arrives at ten.
- **Am besten fragen Sie dort.** — You'd best ask over there.
