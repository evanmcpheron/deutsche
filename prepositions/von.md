# von

**Type:** Preposition (**always dative**)
**Level:** A1
**Priority:** Tier 1 — high
**Knowledge goal:** Active

[← aus](aus.md) | [Category Index](README.md) | [Next → für](fuer.md) | [↑ Master Index](../MASTER_INDEX.md)

## Core Idea

*von* marks a **starting point or a source** — where something comes from, who it's from, when it starts.

It's also how German usually expresses **"of"** in the possessive sense: *ein Freund von mir* — a friend of mine.

**Case: always dative.** Contraction: *von dem* → **vom**.

## Most Important A1 Uses

### 1. Starting point of a journey or period

**Ich komme von der Arbeit.**
I'm coming from work.

**Der Zug fährt von Gleis 3.**
The train leaves from platform 3.

**von Montag bis Freitag**
from Monday to Friday → [von … bis …](../patterns/von-bis.md)

### 2. From a person — who gave, sent, or said it

**Das ist ein Geschenk von meiner Mutter.**
That's a present from my mother.

**Ich habe eine E-Mail von Anna.**
I have an email from Anna.

**Grüße von Tom!**
Greetings from Tom!

### 3. "Of" — belonging

**das Auto von meinem Bruder**
my brother's car

**ein Freund von mir**
a friend of mine

**die Mutter von Anna**
Anna's mother

German has a possessive case (genitive) for this, but in everyday speech *von* + dative is the normal choice, and it's what you should use at A1.

### 4. Time — "from … on"

**von acht bis fünf**
from eight to five

**ab morgen** — from tomorrow *(here German prefers **ab**, not *von*)*

## Common Chunks

- **von … bis …** — from … to … → [full page](../patterns/von-bis.md)
- **von mir / von dir / von uns** — from me/you/us
- **ein Freund von mir** — a friend of mine
- **von hier** — from here: *Wie weit ist es von hier?*
- **vom Bahnhof** — from the station
- **von Anfang an** — from the start *(A2 — recognize)*

## Pattern Recognition

**Ich komme von der Uni.**
**Das ist von meinem Vater.**
**Wir fahren von Berlin nach Hamburg.**
**Der Bus fährt vom Hauptbahnhof.**
**Ich habe es von einem Freund.**

Every one has a source: a place you've left, a person who gave you something, a point a journey starts from.

## What Changes Without It?

**Ich komme aus Berlin.**
I'm from Berlin. *(my home city — origin)*

**Ich komme von Berlin.**
Not what a German would say. For where you're **from** in life, German wants *aus*; and even for a journey, a bare *von* + city sounds unfinished — you'd say *von Berlin nach Hamburg*.

**Ich komme vom Bahnhof.**
I'm coming from the station. *(starting point today — natural)*

See [aus vs. von](../comparisons/aus-vs-von.md); this pair causes more errors than almost anything else in this folder.

## Common English-Speaker Mistakes

- **Using *von* for nationality.** *Ich bin von Amerika* → **aus Amerika**.
- **Building the genitive when you don't need to.** *das Auto meines Bruders* is correct but advanced. *das Auto von meinem Bruder* is normal, everyday German.
- **Forgetting *vom*.** *von dem Bahnhof* → **vom Bahnhof**.
- **Using *von* for "by train."** That's *mit dem Zug*.

## Active or Passive?

**Active.** *von … bis …*, *ein Freund von mir*, and *von der Arbeit* are all high-frequency. The genitive can wait indefinitely.

## You May Hear

- **Von wem ist das?** — Who's that from?
- **Der Zug von Hamburg hat Verspätung.** — The train from Hamburg is delayed.
- **Wir haben von Montag bis Freitag geöffnet.** — We're open Monday to Friday.
- **Das hängt von dir ab.** — That depends on you.
