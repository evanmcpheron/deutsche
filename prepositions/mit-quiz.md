## Mini Review


1. Choose the natural word: *Ich fahre ___ dem Bus.*\
   a. mit\
   b. bei\
   c. nach

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Ich fahre mit dem Bus.*

</details>

2. Which is correct?\
   a. mit mein Bruder\
   b. mit meinem Bruder\
   c. mit meinen Bruder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *mit meinem Bruder* (dative).

</details>

3. **Kommst du mit?** Why is there no noun after *mit*?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** Because *mit* here is the prefix of the separable verb *mitkommen*, not a preposition. The sentence means "are you coming along?"

**Explanation:** *mit* takes the dative and commonly expresses accompaniment, means/instrument, or transportation.

</details>

4. What's the difference between **Ich wohne mit meiner Schwester zusammen** and **Ich wohne bei meiner Schwester**?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** *mit meiner Schwester zusammen* = we share a flat as equals. *bei meiner Schwester* = I'm staying at her place; it's her home. See [bei vs. mit](../comparisons/bei-vs-mit.md).

**Explanation:** *mit* takes the dative and commonly expresses accompaniment, means/instrument, or transportation.

</details>

5. **Produce it:** How would you say "I'm going to Berlin by train" in German?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich fahre mit dem Zug nach Berlin.**

**Explanation:** *mit* takes the dative and commonly expresses accompaniment, means/instrument, or transportation.

</details>

6. **Produce it:** Order a coffee with milk.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** A natural way to say this is **Einen Kaffee mit Milch, bitte.**

**Explanation:** *mit* takes the dative and commonly expresses accompaniment, means/instrument, or transportation.

</details>
