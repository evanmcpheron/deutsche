# Quiz: mit

Try each question before opening its solution. Each solution is directly below the question so you can check your understanding as you go.

## Part A — Companion, Transport, or Tool?

1. You travel to Berlin by train.\
   *Ich fahre ___ Zug nach Berlin.*\
   a. beim\
   b. mit dem\
   c. vom\
   d. zum

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Means of transport use *mit* plus dative: *mit dem Zug*. Option a, *beim Zug*, would place you near the train rather than tell how you travel.

</details>

2. You and your sister walk to the market together.\
   *Ich gehe ___ Schwester zum Markt.*\
   a. mit meiner\
   b. bei meiner\
   c. von meiner\
   d. zu meiner

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** A person who accompanies you takes *mit*: *mit meiner Schwester*. Option b would place you at your sister's home; it does not mean that she comes along.

</details>

3. At a café, you want coffee with milk.\
   *Ein Kaffee ___ Milch, bitte.*\
   a. bei\
   b. von\
   c. mit\
   d. zu

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** An ingredient or added thing can take *mit*: *Kaffee mit Milch*. *Von* would mark a source, not something included in the drink.

</details>

4. You write a note using a pen.\
   *Ich schreibe ___ Stift.* (der Stift = pen)\
   a. beim\
   b. vom\
   c. zum\
   d. mit einem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The tool you use takes *mit*: *mit einem Stift*. Because *mit* always takes dative, masculine *ein Stift* becomes *einem Stift*.

</details>

5. You go to a concert together with your brother. Which form is correct?\
   a. mit mein Bruder\
   b. mit meinem Bruder\
   c. mit meinen Bruder\
   d. bei meinem Bruder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Mit* always takes dative, so *mein Bruder* becomes *meinem Bruder*. Option d is grammatical as a location phrase — “at my brother's” — but it does not express accompaniment.

</details>

6. You are walking through the park together with several children.\
   *Ich gehe ___ Kindern.*\
   a. mit die\
   b. mit den\
   c. bei den\
   d. mit der

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Dative plural after *mit* uses *den*: *mit den Kindern*. Option c has the right article but the wrong preposition; it would describe a location associated with the children rather than walking together.

</details>

7. A friend is leaving for the cinema and asks **Kommst du mit?** What is *mit* doing here?\
   a. It marks a destination.\
   b. It introduces a dative noun.\
   c. It means “at someone's place.”\
   d. It is the separated prefix of *mitkommen*.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** There is no noun after *mit* because it belongs to the separable verb *mitkommen*: *Kommst du mit?* Option b describes the preposition use, which always has a following noun or pronoun.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

8. You and your sister share the same flat as equals. Which sentence fits?\
   a. Ich wohne mit meiner Schwester zusammen.\
   b. Ich wohne bei meiner Schwester.\
   c. Ich gehe zu meiner Schwester.\
   d. Ich komme von meiner Schwester.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Mit meiner Schwester zusammen wohnen* says you live together. Option b is also grammatical, but it presents the home as your sister's place and you as staying with her.

</details>

9. Which sentence correctly shows that *mit* has **no contraction** with *dem*?\
   a. Ich fahre beim Bus.\
   b. Ich fahre zum Bus.\
   c. Ich fahre vom Bus.\
   d. Ich fahre mit dem Bus.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** German keeps *mit dem* as two words: *mit dem Bus*. The other choices are real contractions of different prepositions and therefore express different relationships.

</details>

10. Your company and Siemens are working on one project together. Which sentence expresses that partnership?\
   a. Wir arbeiten bei Siemens.\
   b. Wir arbeiten von Siemens.\
   c. Wir arbeiten mit Siemens.\
   d. Wir arbeiten zu Siemens.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Mit Siemens* makes Siemens the partner you work together with. Option a is correct German but different: *bei Siemens* says Siemens is your employer.

</details>

11. Which sentence uses *mit* for an **ingredient or included thing**?\
   a. Ich gehe mit Anna.\
   b. Ich fahre mit dem Auto.\
   c. Ein Tee mit Zitrone, bitte.\
   d. Ich arbeite bei Anna.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Tee mit Zitrone* uses *mit* for something included with the drink. Options a and b also use *mit* correctly, but for a companion and a means of transport instead.

</details>

12. Which sentence does **not** work for its intended meaning?\
   a. Ich gehe mit meiner Freundin.\
   b. Ich schreibe mit einem Stift.\
   c. Ein Kaffee mit Milch, bitte.\
   d. Ich fahre bei dem Zug. — intended: “I travel by train.”

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Means of transport require *mit*: *Ich fahre mit dem Zug.* *Bei dem Zug* places someone near the train and cannot mean “by train.”

</details>

13. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does the gap need *mit*?\
   a. Ich fahre ___ dem Auto.\
   b. Ich gehe ___ meinem Freund ins Kino.\
   c. Ein Kaffee ___ Milch, bitte.\
   d. Ich bin ___ meiner Schwester. Es ist ihre Wohnung.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** Transport, companionship, and an ingredient all use *mit*. Option d describes being at your sister's home, so it needs *bei meiner Schwester*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

14. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Mit wem gehst du ins Kino?
B: Ich gehe ___ Tom.
```

   a. bei\
   b. mit\
   c. von\
   d. zu

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** *Mit wem?* asks for a companion, so the answer is *mit Tom*. *Bei Tom* would answer a location question about being at Tom's place.

</details>

15. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Wie kommst du zur Arbeit?
B: Ich fahre ___ Bus.
```

   a. mit dem\
   b. beim\
   c. vom\
   d. zum

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Wie?* asks how B travels, and transport takes *mit dem Bus*. *Zum Bus* would describe movement toward the bus, not the means used for the whole trip.

</details>

16. Your friend asks **Kommst du mit?** What is the natural meaning?\
   a. Are you coming along?\
   b. Are you coming from somewhere?\
   c. Are you at my place?\
   d. Are you coming by train?

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** This is the separable verb *mitkommen*: “come along.” Option d is a different *mit* construction and would need a transport noun, such as *mit dem Zug*.

</details>

17. You order at a café. Which sentence naturally asks for coffee with milk?\
   a. Ein Kaffee bei Milch, bitte.\
   b. Ein Kaffee von Milch, bitte.\
   c. Ein Kaffee zu Milch, bitte.\
   d. Ein Kaffee mit Milch, bitte.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Mit Milch* is the normal ingredient phrase. The other prepositions do not express that the milk is included with the coffee.

</details>

18. Someone says **Bring bitte Brot mit.** Why is *mit* at the end?\
   a. Every *mit* phrase goes at the end.\
   b. *Brot* requires dative here.\
   c. *Mit* is the separated prefix of *mitbringen*.\
   d. *Mit* means “near” in this sentence.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Mitbringen* is separable, so the prefix *mit* moves to the end of the main clause. This is not the preposition *mit*, so there is no dative noun after it.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. Fill in the missing words. No options given.

   You take the underground to work.

```text
Ich fahre ___ U-Bahn zur Arbeit. (die U-Bahn = underground)
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **mit der**

**Explanation:** Means of transport use *mit* plus dative. *Die U-Bahn* therefore becomes *mit der U-Bahn*.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing words. No options given.

   Your brother goes to the concert together with you.

```text
Ich gehe ___ Bruder ins Konzert.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **mit meinem**

**Explanation:** A companion takes *mit*, and masculine *mein Bruder* becomes dative *meinem Bruder*.

</details>

21. Put the words in order.

```text
mit einem Stift / ich / schreibe
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich schreibe mit einem Stift.**

**Explanation:** The pen is the tool used to write, so the phrase is *mit einem Stift* with dative after *mit*.

</details>

22. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich bin bei meiner Schwester.**\
   New situation: The flat belongs to both of you, and you live there together.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich wohne mit meiner Schwester zusammen.**

**Explanation:** *Bei meiner Schwester* presents the home as hers. When you share the home as equals, the lesson uses *mit meiner Schwester zusammen wohnen*.

</details>
