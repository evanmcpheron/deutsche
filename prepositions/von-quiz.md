## Part A — Source, Start, or Belonging?

1. It is your birthday. Your mother gives you a book.\
   *Das Geschenk ist ___ meiner Mutter.*\
   a. aus\
   b. von\
   c. mit\
   d. bei

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A person who gives or sends something is a source, so German uses *von*: *von meiner Mutter*. Option a is for origin, material, or movement out of an interior, not a person who gives you something.

</details>

2. The station display tells you that your train departs from platform 3.\
   *Der Zug fährt ___ Gleis 3.* (das Gleis = platform/track)\
   a. aus\
   b. bei\
   c. von\
   d. mit

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** A journey's starting point takes *von*: *von Gleis 3*. Option a would suggest movement out of the inside of something, which is not the relationship between a train and its platform.

</details>

3. Your brother owns the red car outside. Which everyday German phrase identifies it?\
   a. das Auto aus meinem Bruder\
   b. das Auto mit meinem Bruder\
   c. das Auto bei meinem Bruder\
   d. das Auto von meinem Bruder

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** Everyday German commonly uses *von* plus dative for belonging: *das Auto von meinem Bruder*. Option b says the car is together with your brother, not that it belongs to him.

</details>

4. You are talking about one person in your group of friends. Which phrase means “a friend of mine”?\
   a. ein Freund aus mir\
   b. ein Freund bei mir\
   c. ein Freund mit mir\
   d. ein Freund von mir

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Ein Freund von mir* is the common A1 chunk for “a friend of mine.” *Von* marks the relationship; option c would only say that a friend is together with you.

</details>

5. You leave the office and call your spouse while walking home.\
   *Ich komme gerade ___ Arbeit.*\
   a. von der\
   b. aus der\
   c. bei der\
   d. mit der

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Die Arbeit* is the starting point of the trip you are making now, so the natural chunk is *von der Arbeit*. Option b treats work like an interior you physically emerge from and is not the normal expression here.

</details>

6. A shop is open every weekday and closed on Saturday and Sunday.\
   *Wir haben ___ Montag bis Freitag geöffnet.*\
   a. aus\
   b. von\
   c. bei\
   d. mit

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** A time range uses the pattern *von … bis …*: *von Montag bis Freitag*. Option a is not used to mark the starting edge of a time range.

</details>

7. You are walking home after meeting a friend at the station.\
   *Ich komme ___ Bahnhof.*\
   a. vom\
   b. aus dem\
   c. bei dem\
   d. mit dem

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** The station is today's starting point, so use *von dem Bahnhof*, normally contracted to *vom Bahnhof*. Option b would specifically say you are coming out of the inside of the station building.

</details>

## Part B — Tell It Apart

8. Canada is your home country. Which sentence best answers **Woher kommst du?**\
   a. Ich komme von Kanada.\
   b. Ich komme vom Kanada.\
   c. Ich komme aus Kanada.\
   d. Ich komme bei Kanada.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Lasting origin takes *aus*: *Ich komme aus Kanada.* *Von* is for a current source or starting point, so option a does not give the normal biographical answer the question asks for.

</details>

9. Which sentence uses *von* to identify a **person as the source**?\
   a. Die E-Mail ist von Anna.\
   b. Der Tisch ist aus Holz.\
   c. Ich fahre mit Anna.\
   d. Ich bin bei Anna.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** *Von Anna* tells who the email comes from. Option d places you at Anna's home, while option c makes Anna your companion; neither identifies her as the source of the email.

</details>

10. At a station you hear **Der Zug von Hamburg hat Verspätung.** What does *von Hamburg* tell you?\
   a. Hamburg is where every passenger lives.\
   b. The train is made in Hamburg.\
   c. Hamburg is the train's source or starting point.\
   d. The train is travelling with Hamburg.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** Here *von* identifies where the train comes from on this route. Option a confuses a journey's starting point with the lasting-origin use that normally takes *aus* for people.

</details>

11. Which sentence is the most natural everyday German?\
   a. Der Bus fährt von der Hauptbahnhof.\
   b. Der Bus fährt aus dem Hauptbahnhof.\
   c. Der Bus fährt bei dem Hauptbahnhof.\
   d. Der Bus fährt vom Hauptbahnhof.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** *Der Hauptbahnhof* is masculine, so *von dem* contracts to *vom*: *vom Hauptbahnhof*. Option a uses the feminine dative article *der* with a masculine noun; option b changes the relation to coming out of an interior.

</details>

12. Which sentence expresses a relationship that does **not** use *von*?\
   a. Das ist ein Geschenk von meinem Vater.\
   b. Der Tisch ist aus Holz.\
   c. Das ist das Auto von meiner Schwester.\
   d. Der Bus kommt vom Bahnhof.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** Materials use *aus*: *aus Holz*. The other three are core *von* uses — a person as source, belonging, and a journey's starting point.

</details>

13. **Select all that apply.** In which sentences does the gap need *von*?\
   a. Das Foto ist ___ meinem Bruder.\
   b. Das ist das Fahrrad ___ meiner Schwester.\
   c. Ich komme gerade ___ der Arbeit.\
   d. Ich komme ___ Spanien. Das ist mein Heimatland. (das Heimatland = home country)

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a, b, c**

**Explanation:** A person as source, belonging, and today's starting point all use *von*. Option d is lasting origin, so it needs *aus Spanien*.

</details>

## Part C — Conversation and Interpretation

14. Complete the dialogue.

```text
A: Von wem ist das Geschenk?
B: Es ist ___ meinem Vater.
```

   a. aus\
   b. von\
   c. bei\
   d. mit

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **b**

**Explanation:** The question *Von wem?* asks who the source is, and the answer repeats the same relationship: *von meinem Vater*. *Aus* cannot mark a person who gives the gift.

</details>

15. You are on a train. Berlin is where this trip begins and Hamburg is where it ends.\
   a. Ich komme aus Berlin.\
   b. Ich bin bei Berlin und Hamburg.\
   c. Ich fahre mit Berlin nach Hamburg.\
   d. Ich fahre von Berlin nach Hamburg.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **d**

**Explanation:** The complete route pattern is *von Berlin nach Hamburg*: starting point plus destination. Option a instead tells people Berlin is your lasting place of origin.

</details>

16. Someone says **Das ist ein Freund von mir.** What does *von mir* express?\
   a. He is one of my friends.\
   b. He is travelling from me.\
   c. He is staying at my home.\
   d. He is travelling with me.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **a**

**Explanation:** This is the everyday belonging/relationship use of *von*: “a friend of mine.” Option d would need *mit mir* and would describe companionship rather than the relationship between you.

</details>

17. A café sign says **von acht bis fünf**. What does that describe?\
   a. The café is at number eight.\
   b. The café opens only at five.\
   c. A time range beginning at eight and ending at five.\
   d. The café is made of eight parts.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** *Von … bis …* marks a range from one point to another. Here those points are clock times. Option b ignores the starting point introduced by *von*.

</details>

18. Your new work schedule starts tomorrow and continues after that. Which phrase is the natural one from the lesson?\
   a. von morgen\
   b. vom morgen\
   c. ab morgen\
   d. aus morgen

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **c**

**Explanation:** German normally says *ab morgen* for “from tomorrow on.” The lesson specifically contrasts this with time ranges such as *von acht bis fünf*; option a translates English “from” too mechanically.

</details>

## Part D — Retrieve and Produce

19. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   Anna sends you an email.

```text
Ich habe eine E-Mail ___ Anna.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **von**

**Explanation:** A person who sends something is its source, so use *von Anna*.

</details>

20. Fill in the missing word. No options given.

   You leave the station and walk home.

```text
Ich komme ___ Bahnhof.
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **vom**

**Explanation:** The station is the starting point of the current trip. *Von dem Bahnhof* contracts to the normal everyday form *vom Bahnhof*.

</details>

21. Put the words in order.

```text
von meinem Bruder / das Auto / ist / das
```

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Das ist das Auto von meinem Bruder.**

**Explanation:** *Von* plus dative is the A1 everyday way to show belonging here: the car belongs to your brother.

</details>

22. Rewrite the sentence for the new situation.

   Original: **Ich komme aus Berlin.**\
   New situation: Berlin is only the start of today's train trip, and Hamburg is the destination.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer:** **Ich fahre von Berlin nach Hamburg.**

**Explanation:** The original describes lasting origin. The new situation is a route, so German changes to the starting-point pattern *von Berlin nach Hamburg*.

</details>
