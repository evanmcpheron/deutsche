# Grading Instructions for a Future AI

**Assessment Version:** `A1-WRITTEN-20260817-F2F2`  
**Blueprint Version:** `A1-Written-1.0`

Use these instructions when grading a completed attempt. The learner should not need to explain the assessment system again.

## Inputs

Required:

1. the completed learner-facing `assessment/` files;
2. all files in `grader-only/`.

Optional:

3. one or more previous standardized `German A1 Progress Report` files.

## Non-Negotiable Scope

This assessment does **not** assess listening, speaking, pronunciation, phonetic discrimination, or oral interaction. Never penalize those skills and never claim full four-skill CEFR A1 proficiency.

The highest permitted positive conclusion is:

**A1 Achieved in Assessed Domains**

## Grading Procedure

### Step 1 — Validate the Administration

Read `assessment/00-administration.md` and `assessment/99-submission-checklist.md`.

Note:

- version;
- date and attempt number;
- active testing time;
- number of sittings;
- excessive timing;
- prohibited assistance;
- unusual conditions;
- whether grader-only material was consulted early.

If prohibited assistance was used or the answer key was consulted, still grade if requested, but clearly mark the attempt as **not valid as an unaided progress measurement**.

Do not secretly discard an attempt because it took longer than suggested. Record the timing limitation.

### Step 2 — Read the Entire Attempt

Read every learner-facing assessment file, including all writing responses and the A2 ceiling. Do not grade from excerpts only.

### Step 3 — Load the Grading System

Read:

- `assessment-blueprint.md`;
- `answer-key.md`;
- `scoring-rubric.md`;
- `report-template.md`;
- `source-notes.md`;
- `version-metadata.md`.

Use the blueprint's primary domains to avoid double counting.

### Step 4 — Score Every A1 Core Item

For every A1 core item:

- assign earned points;
- preserve the item's primary scoring domain;
- preserve R / CP / IP mode;
- note secondary diagnostic evidence where useful;
- apply documented partial-credit rules;
- treat blank and `IDK` as 0, never as a harsher penalty;
- do not award arbitrary partial credit to wrong selected-response answers.

If an answer is not listed in the key but is valid contemporary Standard German and fulfills the task, accept it. Document substantive key expansions in the report.

### Step 5 — Grade Independent Writing Analytically

Score W001–W004 using `scoring-rubric.md`.

For each task, record criterion scores and total /20.

Do not count every repeated manifestation of one underlying error as a separate unlimited deduction. Instead, lower the relevant control criterion to the level supported by the pattern and record representative examples.

Evaluate whether errors:

- block comprehension;
- change meaning;
- recur;
- affect a core A1 target;
- are minor form errors;
- are ordinary developmental imperfections.

### Step 6 — Calculate Domain Scores

Raw maxima:

- Reading Comprehension: 34
- Written Production: 80
- Grammar & Sentence Structure: 38
- Vocabulary & Lexical Control: 30
- Functional Communication & Pragmatics: 28
- Orthography & Written Conventions: 10

Normalize each to 0–100 before weighting.

Use full precision internally, but report integer headline percentages.

### Step 7 — Calculate Weighted Overall

Use:

`Reading×0.25 + Writing×0.25 + Grammar×0.15 + Vocabulary×0.15 + Functional×0.15 + Orthography×0.05`

Round the final headline overall score to a whole percent only after the calculation.

### Step 8 — Apply Classification and Floors

Preliminary overall bands:

- 0–39: Pre-A1
- 40–54: Early A1
- 55–69: Developing A1
- 70–79: Strong A1
- 80–100: potentially A1 Achieved in Assessed Domains

To award **A1 Achieved in Assessed Domains**, verify all floors:

- Overall ≥ 80
- Reading ≥ 70
- Written Production ≥ 70
- Functional Communication & Pragmatics ≥ 70
- Grammar & Sentence Structure ≥ 65
- Vocabulary & Lexical Control ≥ 65
- Orthography & Written Conventions ≥ 60

Also verify:

- no major foundational A1 communicative capability is clearly absent;
- the learner demonstrates meaningful independent production and is not succeeding almost entirely by recognition.

If overall is 80+ but any floor is missed, final classification is **Strong A1**. Name each blocking floor or capability.

Do not inflate a result for encouragement.

### Step 9 — Calculate Ability-Mode Indices

Core A1 maxima:

- Recognition: 70
- Controlled Production: 70
- Independent Production: 80

Calculate each as earned/max ×100.

These are diagnostic only and must not be added to the overall score.

Explicitly investigate recognition-production gaps. A pattern such as strong R with much weaker CP/IP is important and should be prominent in the report.

### Step 10 — Calculate Vocabulary Diagnostics

Passive vocabulary:

- score `V001–V012` only;
- maximum 12;
- use passive-tagged reading items as corroborating observations only.

Active vocabulary:

- score `V013–V024`;
- score `P001–P006`;
- add the 3-point **Vocabulary & lexical adequacy** criterion from each W001–W004 task;
- maximum 30.

Report both percentages as diagnostics, not as additional weighted scores.

Also identify topic areas with enough evidence to support a conclusion.

### Step 11 — Analyze Reading

Report patterns in:

- gist;
- explicit detail;
- practical information;
- time/date/number interpretation;
- short-message comprehension;
- signs/notices;
- schedules/timetables;
- any reasonable A1-level inference tested.

Do not describe one isolated miss as a broad weakness unless corroborated.

### Step 12 — Analyze Grammar and Sentence Structure

Use item tags and writing evidence to inspect, when supported:

- present-tense verb forms;
- sein/haben;
- V2 and fronting;
- yes/no and W-questions;
- nicht/kein;
- articles, gender-sensitive forms, nominative/accusative;
- limited source-supported dative structures;
- possessives;
- plurals;
- modal constructions;
- separable verbs;
- common prepositions;
- time expressions;
- coordination;
- imperative/request forms;
- pronoun reference;
- selected A1 past forms.

Use a numeric subtopic score only when evidence density is sufficient. Otherwise use **Insufficient evidence**.

### Step 13 — Analyze Function and Pragmatics

Report whether the learner can use or recognize simple German appropriately for:

- greetings/closings;
- du/Sie;
- polite requests;
- ordering/purchasing;
- invitations and replies;
- apologies and thanks;
- asking for clarification;
- appointments/rescheduling;
- practical travel/service needs.

Functional success can deserve substantial credit despite minor grammar errors, but not full credit when meaning or appropriateness is materially wrong.

### Step 14 — Analyze Orthography

Report:

- spelling;
- noun and sentence capitalization;
- umlauts;
- ß;
- basic punctuation;
- repeated patterns.

Do not automatically treat `ae/oe/ue/ss` as acceptable keyboard substitutes when the standard spelling requires a German character.

### Step 15 — Analyze Confidence

For all confidence-rated items, compare accuracy and stated confidence.

Highlight patterns such as:

- several correct answers marked Guess;
- repeated incorrect answers marked Certain;
- stable correct-and-Certain performance.

Confidence never changes item points.

### Step 16 — Score the A2 Ceiling Separately

Maximum 20.

Classify:

- 0–6: No meaningful A2 signal
- 7–12: Emerging A2 signal
- 13–20: Consistent A2 indicators

Do not let this section lower or raise the A1 weighted score.

Never report **A2 achieved** from this ceiling.

### Step 17 — Build the A1 Capability Matrix

Use:

- Not demonstrated
- Emerging
- Developing
- Strong
- Secure
- Insufficient evidence

Base each status on multiple evidence points where possible. Do not manufacture precision from a single item.

Suggested matrix capabilities include:

- understand short personal messages;
- locate practical information in notices/ads;
- interpret schedules, dates, prices, and times;
- understand common everyday vocabulary;
- retrieve common everyday vocabulary;
- produce simple main clauses;
- form basic questions;
- use basic negation;
- use common article/case forms;
- use common modal/separable-verb patterns;
- make basic requests and respond appropriately;
- manage simple invitations/appointments;
- write short practical messages;
- write simple familiar descriptions;
- use basic standard orthography.

Add or remove rows when evidence justifies it.

### Step 18 — Analyze Error Patterns

Report only patterns supported by actual responses.

Potential categories include, but are not limited to:

- verb conjugation;
- word order;
- article/gender/case;
- negation;
- plural formation;
- possessives;
- separable verbs;
- modal constructions;
- prepositions;
- lexical choice;
- false friends;
- capitalization;
- umlauts;
- ß;
- spelling;
- question formation;
- time interpretation;
- formality/pragmatics;
- active-retrieval failures;
- recognition-production gap.

Use representative examples rather than reproducing every error.

Format representative examples like:

You wrote:
> ...

Expected:
> ...

Pattern:
...

### Step 19 — Build Priorities

Use exactly these levels:

- **Priority 1 — Blocking weaknesses**
- **Priority 2 — Important weaknesses**
- **Priority 3 — Consolidation**

For every recommended area state:

- the problem;
- assessment evidence;
- what the learner should be able to do;
- a concrete practice type.

Do not say only “practice grammar.”

### Step 20 — Compare Prior Reports if Supplied

Compare:

- normalized domain scores;
- R / CP / IP indices;
- active/passive vocabulary;
- sufficiently comparable subtopics;
- recurring/resolved/new error patterns.

Do not describe 1–2 percentage-point movement as meaningful unless the item evidence supports it. Distinguish likely change from measurement noise and version effects.

### Step 21 — Produce the Standardized Report

Follow `report-template.md` exactly in overall structure.

The bottom line must answer where the learner stands in German A1 **in the assessed written/non-oral domains** and explicitly repeat that speaking/listening were not assessed.

## Key Integrity Rule

The answer key is authoritative for intended answers but is not allowed to override actual German correctness.

If an unanticipated response is valid Standard German and fulfills the task:

1. score it correctly;
2. note any material key adjustment;
3. do not penalize the learner for the key's omission.

## Invalidating or Limiting Conditions

Do not silently ignore validity issues.

Examples:

- prohibited outside assistance;
- answer-key exposure;
- unusually long or fragmented administration;
- missing sections;
- missing responses caused by file loss.

Grade what is available, identify the limitation, and avoid stronger claims than the evidence supports.
