# Source Notes

**Source Research Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Access Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Blueprint Version:** `A1-Written-1.0`

This assessment was built from current official source material available on the research date. The hierarchy was Council of Europe first, then Goethe-Institut, then telc.

## 1. Council of Europe — CEFR Companion Volume (2020)

**Source:** Council of Europe, *Common European Framework of Reference for Languages: Learning, teaching, assessment — Companion volume* (2020).  
**Official source:** https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4

### Contribution to the blueprint

The CEFR was used as the capability-level foundation, especially for:

- overall reading comprehension;
- reading correspondence;
- reading for orientation and practical information;
- written production;
- written interaction;
- general linguistic range;
- vocabulary control/range;
- grammatical accuracy;
- orthographic control;
- sociolinguistic appropriateness;
- the distinction between A1 and A2 communicative demand.

Relevant A1 ideas used in the blueprint include the ability to understand very short, simple texts with familiar words and phrases; understand short simple messages; give basic personal information in writing; produce simple isolated phrases and sentences; ask for or pass on personal details; spell personal details; and use basic punctuation.

The CEFR does **not** function as a German grammar syllabus. Grammar coverage was therefore resolved through the German-specific official inventories below rather than by treating CEFR descriptors as chapter lists.

## 3. Goethe-Institut — Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1

**Official current exam page:**  
https://www.goethe.de/ins/us/en/spr/prf/gzsd1.cfm

**Official current A1 practice page:**  
https://www.goethe.de/de/spr/prf/ueb/pa1.html

### Contribution

The current Goethe material confirms the adult A1 exam context and the everyday written text types used at A1. The official exam description identifies short notes, advertisements, information signs/notices, simple forms, and a short personal text as appropriate written material.

This assessment is deliberately broader and more diagnostic than the certification exam, but these official task types informed the realism of reading and functional-writing tasks.

## 4. Goethe-Institut — Prüfungsziele / Testbeschreibung A1: Start Deutsch 1

**Official PDF:**  
https://www.goethe.de/pro/relaunch/prf/de/Pruefungsziele_Testbeschreibung_A1_SD1.pdf

**Edition visible in the current official PDF:** 4th updated edition, © 2022.

### Contribution

This document supplied the main German-specific A1 inventories:

- communicative language actions/functions;
- strategies;
- notions;
- themes;
- vocabulary;
- grammar/morphosyntactic structures;
- adult domains: private life, work, education/training, and public/semi-public life.

The inventory explicitly supports broad A1 sampling across personal information, family, home, travel/transport, food/drink, shopping, services, education, work, and leisure.

It also explicitly frames grammar as subordinate to task-oriented communicative ability and notes that comprehensibility has high importance at A1.

### Grammar scope decisions derived from this source

The current official inventory includes, among other areas:

- verb-second main-clause order;
- sentence brackets;
- negation;
- questions;
- sentence connections;
- present tense;
- selected Perfekt forms;
- selected Präteritum forms of `haben` and `sein`;
- modal verbs;
- separable-prefix verbs;
- noun gender, number, and case;
- article and pronoun forms;
- temporal, local, and modal prepositions;
- common imperative forms.

The assessment samples these selectively in context rather than treating them as isolated grammar chapters.

The source also notes that some formally complex expressions needed for polite requests can be learned lexically at A1 rather than as a systematic paradigm. The assessment therefore accepts formulaic A1 requests such as `Ich hätte gern ...` without requiring systematic Konjunktiv-II mastery.

### Contribution

The official list provides:

- A1 topic areas;
- word groups;
- an adult A1 reference vocabulary;
- gender/plural information;
- example contexts.

The current official materials describe the list as a reference for the A1 level rather than a drill list. The accompanying Goethe test-description material states that learners should passively understand approximately the listed inventory and have roughly half of the approximately 650 entries available actively.

This assessment therefore distinguishes passive and active vocabulary and avoids turning the test into a large translation list.

### Additional contribution from the official telc sample

The official A1.2 material explicitly states that A1.1 plus A1.2 leads to A1 and prepares learners for Start Deutsch 1 / telc Deutsch A1. The telc material was used as corroborating evidence for the scope boundaries already established from CEFR and Goethe, not as a reason to expand the assessment into a course-syllabus test.

## Scope Decisions and A1/A2 Boundaries

### Included in A1 core

- practical reading of messages, notices, schedules, prices, opening hours, ads, and short everyday information;
- basic personal-information writing;
- short practical written messages;
- present-tense control;
- basic V2/main-clause order;
- questions;
- `nicht` / `kein`;
- common article/case forms;
- source-supported high-frequency dative contexts;
- possessives;
- plurals;
- modal constructions;
- separable verbs;
- common prepositions;
- selected source-supported A1 past forms;
- polite requests as ordinary formulaic language;
- basic standard orthography.

### Deliberately limited in A1 core

- systematic adjective-declension paradigms;
- systematic Konjunktiv-II paradigms;
- broad subordinate-clause production;
- extended connected narrative;
- abstract opinion or argument;
- dense idiomatic language.

### A2 ceiling

The ceiling increases discourse length, connection of ideas, reason-giving, and past-event production beyond what is required for the A1 core. It is intentionally separate and never affects A1 scoring.

Some individual forms can occur at the upper edge of A1 in particular inventories; the A2 ceiling is interpreted as a **pattern of above-A1 performance**, not as proof that every ceiling form belongs exclusively to A2.

## Variety and Orthography

The assessment uses contemporary Standard German as used in Germany and normal adult everyday contexts. It avoids dialect and regional vocabulary as testing targets.

The learner has explicitly requested strict standard spelling. Therefore required `ä`, `ö`, `ü`, and `ß` are not automatically replaced by `ae`, `oe`, `ue`, or `ss`, even though the CEFR A1 orthographic descriptor itself tolerates limited early-stage spelling control. Orthography is weighted only 5% so that this stricter local standard does not dominate communicative evidence.

## No Listening/Speaking Substitution

No transcript is used as simulated listening. Written dialogue appears only as reading material. No scoring claim is made about speaking, listening, pronunciation, or oral interaction.
