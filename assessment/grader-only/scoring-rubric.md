# Scoring Rubric

**Assessment Version:** `A1-WRITTEN-20260817-F2F2`  
**Blueprint Version:** `A1-Written-1.0`

## 1. Scoring Architecture

Each A1 core item has exactly one **primary scoring domain**. Secondary tags are diagnostic only and do not add points to the weighted overall score.

For each domain:

`Domain % = (points earned in that primary domain / raw maximum for that domain) × 100`

Round headline domain scores to the nearest whole percent **after** calculating with full precision.

### Raw Maximums and Weights

| Domain | Raw max | Weight |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reading Comprehension | 34 | 25% |
| Written Production | 80 | 25% |
| Grammar & Sentence Structure | 38 | 15% |
| Vocabulary & Lexical Control | 30 | 15% |
| Functional Communication & Pragmatics | 28 | 15% |
| Orthography & Written Conventions | 10 | 5% |

Weighted overall:

`Overall = Reading×0.25 + Writing×0.25 + Grammar×0.15 + Vocabulary×0.15 + Functional×0.15 + Orthography×0.05`

Use full-precision domain percentages in the weighted calculation, then round the final headline score to the nearest integer.

## 2. Overall Classification

| Overall | Classification |
|---:|---|
| 0–39 | Pre-A1 |
| 40–54 | Early A1 |
| 55–69 | Developing A1 |
| 70–79 | Strong A1 |
| 80–100 | Candidate for A1 Achieved in Assessed Domains, subject to floors |

### Required Floors for `A1 Achieved in Assessed Domains`

All must be met:

- Overall ≥ 80
- Reading Comprehension ≥ 70
- Written Production ≥ 70
- Functional Communication & Pragmatics ≥ 70
- Grammar & Sentence Structure ≥ 65
- Vocabulary & Lexical Control ≥ 65
- Orthography & Written Conventions ≥ 60
- no major foundational A1 communicative capability is clearly absent;
- meaningful independent production is demonstrated.

If overall is 80+ but any floor or the independent-production requirement is not met, classification is **Strong A1**. State the exact blocking floor(s) or capability.

Never report “A1 certified,” “officially A1,” or equivalent.

## 4. Selected-Response Items

- Correct: full point.
- Incorrect, blank, or `IDK`: 0.
- No partial credit for ordinary MCQ or true/false.
- If an item is genuinely defective or has more than one valid answer, document the adjustment and either accept all valid answers or remove the item from numerator and denominator consistently.

## 5. Controlled Production

Unless a specific item note says otherwise:

- **1.0** — target form/function is correct and the response fulfills the task.
- **0.5** — target knowledge is substantially demonstrated but one limited form error remains that does not change the intended answer; use only when the item permits partial credit in the key or when a valid answer is clearly near-complete.
- **0** — target is absent, wrong, or meaning materially changes.

Do not invent partial credit for a clearly wrong selected response.

A minor orthographic slip on an item whose primary domain is grammar or vocabulary may justify limited partial credit when the intended target is unmistakable. Do not award full credit if the spelling produces a different word or obscures the answer.

## 6. Orthography Items P019–P028

Each has a 1-point maximum.

- **1.0** — all targeted spelling/capitalization/punctuation conventions are correct.
- **0.5** — exactly one minor targeted convention remains wrong, while the rest is corrected and meaning is preserved.
- **0** — two or more targeted errors remain, the learner uses a forbidden keyboard substitute for a required German character without otherwise correcting it, or the sentence is materially changed instead of corrected.

`ae`, `oe`, `ue`, and `ss` are not full-credit substitutes when standard spelling requires `ä`, `ö`, `ü`, or `ß`.

## 7. Independent Writing

Writing is scored analytically. Do not mechanically subtract one point per error. Repeated instances of the same underlying error should lower the relevant control criterion, be documented as a recurring pattern, and not create unlimited deductions.

### W001 — Short Personal Answers — 20 points

| Criterion | Max | Guidance |
|---|---:|---|
| Task fulfillment | 5 | 1 point for each prompt answered with relevant German content. |
| Comprehensibility | 3 | 3 clear throughout; 2 generally clear with some repair needed; 1 meaning often uncertain; 0 largely unintelligible. |
| Appropriateness | 1 | Responses fit ordinary personal-information prompts. |
| Grammar & sentence structure | 3 | Basic present-tense/simple-sentence control appropriate to A1. |
| Vocabulary & lexical adequacy | 3 | Sufficient familiar vocabulary to answer the prompts without pervasive English substitution. |
| Word order | 2 | Main-clause/question-relevant word order is sufficiently controlled. |
| Spelling & capitalization | 3 | Standard spelling, noun capitalization, German characters, and basic punctuation. |

### W002–W004 — Standard 20-Point Analytic Rubric

| Criterion | Max | Guidance |
|---|---:|---|
| Task fulfillment | 4 | Required content points substantially communicated. Allocate proportionally; one omitted point should not erase the whole task. |
| Comprehensibility | 3 | 3 clear throughout; 2 generally clear; 1 repeated ambiguity; 0 largely unintelligible. |
| Appropriateness | 2 | Register, opening/closing, request/response conventions appropriate to the situation. |
| Grammar & sentence structure | 3 | A1 structures generally usable; errors evaluated by frequency and effect on meaning. |
| Vocabulary & lexical adequacy | 3 | Enough appropriate everyday vocabulary to fulfill the task. |
| Word order | 2 | Main-clause order and sentence bracket sufficiently controlled for A1. |
| Spelling & capitalization | 2 | Standard spelling, noun capitalization, umlauts/ß, and basic punctuation. |
| Simple connection/organization | 1 | Ideas are ordered or connected sufficiently for the task. A1 does not require sophisticated cohesion. |

### Writing Criterion Anchors

For 3-point language criteria:
- 3 = reliable enough for the task with only limited A1-level errors;
- 2 = workable but inconsistent, with recurring errors that do not usually block meaning;
- 1 = very limited or frequently incorrect, sometimes obscuring meaning;
- 0 = little usable evidence.

For 2-point criteria:
- 2 = generally appropriate/controlled;
- 1 = partly demonstrated but inconsistent;
- 0 = absent or seriously inadequate.

A simple but understandable A1 message may earn substantial credit. Do not give full credit solely because the grader can eventually infer the intended meaning.

## 8. Ability-Mode Indices

Use only A1 core points.

| Mode | Raw max |
|---|---:|
| Recognition (R) | 70 |
| Controlled Production (CP) | 70 |
| Independent Production (IP) | 80 |

`Mode % = earned points on items with that mode / mode raw max × 100`

Round headline mode scores to whole percentages.

These scores are not added to the overall result. Highlight large gaps, especially when recognition exceeds controlled production or independent production by roughly 15+ points. Smaller gaps can still be discussed when item-level evidence is coherent.

## 9. Passive and Active Vocabulary Diagnostics

Do not derive these by simply reusing the full weighted vocabulary domain.

### Passive Vocabulary Index

Primary numeric evidence:
- `V001–V012` (12 points).

Corroborating, non-added evidence:
- reading items tagged `vocab_evidence = passive`.

`Passive Vocabulary % = points on V001–V012 / 12 × 100`

### Active Vocabulary Index

Primary numeric evidence:
- `V013–V024` (12 points);
- `P001–P006` (6 points);
- the **Vocabulary & lexical adequacy** criterion from W001–W004 (3 points each; 12 points).

Maximum = 30.

`Active Vocabulary % = (V013–V024 + P001–P006 + writing vocabulary-criterion points) / 30 × 100`

Reading/writing observations can explain topic-specific patterns but must not be added again.

## 10. Confidence Diagnostics

Confidence does not affect scoring.

For each confidence-rated item, compare correctness with the learner's rating:

- correct + Certain: stronger/stable evidence;
- correct + Guess: potentially fragile knowledge;
- incorrect + Certain: possible misconception;
- incorrect + Guess: uncertainty consistent with lack of knowledge.

Report only meaningful patterns, not every confidence response.

## 11. Subtopic Scoring and Error Patterns

A numeric subtopic score normally requires at least three reasonably direct evidence points across at least two contexts.

When evidence is weaker, report **Insufficient evidence** rather than a percentage.

For recurring errors, distinguish:
- meaning-blocking errors;
- errors that change meaning;
- recurring A1 control errors;
- minor form/orthographic errors;
- normal developmental imperfections.

Do not infer a recurring pattern from one occurrence.

## 12. A1 Capability Matrix

Use these status labels:

- Not demonstrated
- Emerging
- Developing
- Strong
- Secure
- Insufficient evidence

Base status on the breadth, consistency, mode, and independence of evidence rather than a single item. Recognition-only success should not normally produce `Secure` for a capability that requires production.

## 13. A2 Ceiling

The A2 ceiling maximum is **20** and is entirely excluded from A1 scoring.

- 0–6: **No meaningful A2 signal**
- 7–12: **Emerging A2 signal**
- 13–20: **Consistent A2 indicators**

Never report `A2 achieved`.
